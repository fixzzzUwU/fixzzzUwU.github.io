Why Reading Personal Finance Books Is the First Step to Building Wealth

Financial literacy isn’t something you’re born with. It's a skill. And like any skill, it gets stronger with practice. Reading books about money exposes you to ideas that school never covered: budgeting, compound interest, passive income, and the psychology behind your spending habits. The best personal finance books don’t just teach you what to do with money. They help you understand why you handle it the way you do. That self-awareness is the foundation for everything that comes next.

The Connection Between Mindset and Money

Here’s a simple truth: your money habits are a direct reflection of your beliefs about money. If you grew up hearing "we can't afford that," you might carry that scarcity mindset into adulthood. If you think investing is only for the wealthy, you'll never start. Reading the right money books gives you a chance to unlearn those limiting beliefs. It’s not about getting rich overnight. It’s about shifting your mindset so you can make smarter decisions over time.

The Best Personal Finance Books to Add to Your Reading List

When it comes to financial literacy books, there are a few titles that stand above the rest. These are the books that readers keep coming back to, the ones that have stood the test of time. Here are two of the most impactful books to get rich in terms of mindset and practical habits.

Rich Dad Poor Dad by Robert Kiyosaki

This is one of the most well-known money books for a reason. Rich Dad Poor Dad challenges the traditional "go to school, get a job, save money" path. Kiyosaki shares his upbringing with two father figures — his biological father (the "poor dad") who valued job security, and his best friend's father (the "rich dad") who valued financial education and asset building.

What makes this one of the best personal finance books is its focus on a simple but powerful idea: put your money to work for you. Instead of working for your paycheck, you build assets that generate income. The book breaks down why the rich buy assets and the middle class buy liabilities disguised as assets. It’s not a step-by-step budgeting guide. It’s a mindset shift that makes you look at every purchase differently.

If you’re just starting a personal finance reading list, this is the book that will fundamentally change how you think about wealth. It’s also an excellent choice for self-improvement seekers who want more than just a surface-level money guide.

The Total Money Makeover by Dave Ramsey

If Rich Dad Poor Dad is the mindset book, The Total Money Makeover is the action plan. Dave Ramsey doesn't waste time with abstract theories. He gives you a no-nonsense, step-by-step system for getting out of debt, building an emergency fund, and saving for retirement. The book introduces his famous "baby steps," a clear sequence that takes you from financial chaos to financial peace.

This book is especially helpful if you're feeling overwhelmed by debt, whether it’s student loans, credit cards, or a car payment. Ramsey’s tone is direct and encouraging, but he never sugarcoats the hard work involved. That’s why it’s consistently ranked among the best personal finance books for practical advice.

It’s also a great complement to other financial literacy books because it answers the "but how do I actually do this?" question that mindset books often leave open. If you’re looking for a money book that feels like a personal coach in print, this is it.

More Money Books Worth Reading

While Rich Dad Poor Dad and The Total Money Makeover are the two books on this list that deserve the top spots, there are a few other financial literacy books worth mentioning as honorable mentions on your journey.

  • The Simple Path to Wealth by JL Collins — A straightforward guide to investing in index funds and understanding why the stock market is a slow but steady wealth builder.
  • The Psychology of Money by Morgan Housel — A collection of short stories about how behavior, not intelligence, drives financial success. Great for self-improvement seekers who like psychology.
  • Your Money or Your Life by Vicki Robin — A classic that helps you align your spending with your values and rethink the relationship between time and money.

These books to get rich in mindset aren’t listed here to distract you from the main recommendations, but to show that there's a whole ecosystem of great personal finance reading out there. Once you've read the top two, these are excellent follow-ups.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best personal finance book for beginners?

For total beginners, Rich Dad Poor Dad is the best place to start. It doesn't require any prior financial knowledge, and it frames money in a way that's easy to understand. The focus on mindset makes it less intimidating than a technical investing guide.

Can reading money books actually help you get rich?

Reading alone won't make you rich, but it's an essential first step. The ideas in these books to get rich help you recognize opportunities, avoid costly mistakes, and build habits that lead to wealth. The knowledge becomes useful only when you put it into action.

How many personal finance books should I read in a year?

There's no magic number, but consistency matters more than quantity. Reading even two or three solid financial literacy books a year, and applying what you learn, is far more valuable than rushing through twenty titles without taking action.
