Best Cashback Credit Cards for Groceries and Gas in 2025

Groceries and gas are two of the few expenses nearly every household pays for every single month. That makes them the best possible targets for a rewards strategy — because unlike travel or dining, you're not going out of your way to earn. A family spending $900 a month on groceries and $250 on gas is putting roughly $13,800 a year through their cards. At 3% back, that's about $414. At 5%, it's closer to $690. Same groceries, same fuel, very different outcome.

This guide compares the best rewards credit card options for 2025, broken down by how you actually spend: heavy grocery runs, long commutes, or a mix of everything. Rates and bonuses change often, so always confirm current terms on the issuer's site before you apply.

How to Choose the Best Cashback Credit Card for Your Spending

Match the rewards rate to your real spending

A card advertising 6% back on groceries is worthless if you spend $150 a month at the supermarket and $400 on gas. Pull up two months of statements and total your grocery, gas, dining, and "everything else" spending. Whichever category is largest should drive your first pick.

Watch the caps, not just the headline rate

Most high-rate cards limit bonus earnings. Common structures include:

  • Annual caps — for example, 6% at US supermarkets on the first $6,000 in purchases each year, then 1%.
  • Quarterly caps — rotating 5% categories that max out at $1,500 in purchases per quarter.
  • Monthly caps — a fixed dollar amount of bonus-eligible spending per billing cycle.

If your grocery bill is $1,200 a month, a card capped at $6,000 a year will stop paying the bonus in May. A flat-rate card plus a capped card is often the better combo.

Weigh the annual fee against the break-even point

A $95 annual fee sounds small until you do the math. If a card pays 6% instead of 3% on $6,000 of groceries, that's an extra $180 — worth the fee. If you only spend $3,000 a year on groceries, the upgrade is worth $90 and the fee eats most of it. Run your own numbers.

Don't ignore the credit card sign up bonus

Welcome offers are frequently worth more than a full year of category rewards. A $200 cash bonus on a no-fee card, or 60,000 points on a travel card, can be the single biggest return you get from a new account. Just make sure the required spending threshold fits your normal budget rather than forcing extra purchases.

The Two-Card Strategy Most Households Should Use

The honest answer is that no single card wins every category. The most efficient setup for most families is:

  1. A capped, high-rate grocery card for supermarket spending — 5–6% back until you hit the cap.
  2. A flat 2% card for everything after the cap, plus gas, bills, and miscellaneous spending.

That combination typically beats any single card, and it keeps your wallet to two pieces of plastic. If you commute long distances, swap the flat-rate card for a dedicated gas card like Costco Anywhere or Sam's Club Mastercard, then add the 2% card back in once you hit the annual gas cap.

One more thing: cash back only counts if you pay the balance in full. Carrying a balance at a 20%+ APR to earn 2% back is a losing trade every time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best cashback credit card for groceries and gas combined?

If you want one card that covers both categories well, the Blue Cash Preferred is the usual answer — 6% at US supermarkets (capped annually) and 3% on gas. If you'd rather avoid an annual fee, the Blue Cash Everyday offers 3% on both. For maximum value, pair a high-rate grocery card with a dedicated gas card.

Do cashback credit cards hurt my credit score?

Applying for a new card typically causes a small, temporary dip from the hard inquiry, and adding a new account lowers your average account age. Those effects fade over time. On-time payments and low credit utilization matter far more, so paying in full each month will usually leave your score better off than before.

Are rotating 5% categories worth the effort?

They can be, if you activate them each quarter and remember which card to use. Rotating-category cards like Chase Freedom Flex and Discover it Cash Back cap bonus earnings at $1,500 in purchases per quarter, which is about $75 in cash back per quarter at 5%. That's meaningful, but a flat 2% card with no tracking required is a reasonable trade-off if you'd rather not manage it.

Bottom Line

For 2025, the best cashback credit card is the one that matches your two biggest recurring expenses. Heavy grocery households should start with a supermarket-focused card. Commuters should start with a gas card. Everyone else should anchor on a flat 2% card and layer a category card on top. Pick one, grab the welcome bonus, and set a reminder to review your setup once a year — because issuers change terms, and your spending changes too.
