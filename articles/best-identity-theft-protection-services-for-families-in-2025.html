Why Families Need Identity Theft Protection in 2025

Identity theft is no longer just a credit card problem. Criminals use personal data to open loans, file tax returns, access bank accounts, take over mobile numbers, and target home equity lines of credit. Homeowners may be especially exposed because property and mortgage data can be used in loan fraud attempts.

Children are also a concern. A child’s clean credit file can be attractive to fraudsters because misuse may go unnoticed for years. A good family identity protection plan should cover more than one adult, include child monitoring where available, and offer recovery help if identity theft occurs.

Credit monitoring alone is useful, but it is not a complete solution. It may not catch bank account fraud, stolen passwords, or misuse of a child’s identity. The best family plans combine credit alerts, dark web monitoring, financial account monitoring, and identity restoration support.

  • Identity theft insurance: Reimbursement for certain recovery costs, subject to plan terms.

How to Protect Your Credit from Identity Theft

Identity protection services work best alongside basic credit hygiene. To protect your credit from identity theft:

  1. Freeze your credit with all three major US bureaus if you are not applying for new credit.
  2. Review credit reports regularly and dispute anything you do not recognize.
  3. Use strong, unique passwords and enable two-factor authentication on bank and credit accounts.
  4. Secure documents like tax returns, Social Security cards, and mortgage paperwork.
  5. Teach children not to share personal information online, and monitor their credit if old enough.
  6. Review bank and credit card statements for small unauthorized charges.
  7. Limit sharing of your Social Security number unless it is truly required.
