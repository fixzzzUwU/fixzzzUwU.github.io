Best Fitness Trackers and Running Watches Compared

Finding the right wearable used to be simple: you either bought a basic step counter or a chunky sports watch. Today, the line between fitness trackers and running watches has blurred, and that's both a blessing and a curse. On one hand, you can find a device that tracks everything from sleep stages to VO2 max. On the other, choosing between a slim Fitbit and a feature-packed Garmin can feel overwhelming.

If you're trying to decide between a fitness tracker for daily wear and a GPS running watch built for serious training, this comparison will help. We've broken down the key differences, highlighted the best options for different lifestyles, and included our top recommendations so you can make an informed choice.

Fitness Tracker vs. Running Watch: What's the Difference?

Before diving into specific models, it helps to understand what separates these two categories.

Fitness Trackers: Everyday Health First

Fitness trackers are designed for all-day wear. They prioritize comfort, battery life, and simplicity. You get step counts, heart rate monitoring, sleep tracking, and notifications—all in a lightweight band that you barely notice on your wrist. They're ideal if your goal is general wellness: moving more, sleeping better, and staying aware of your daily activity.

The trade-off? Most fitness trackers have limited built-in GPS. They often rely on your phone's GPS for outdoor runs, which means carrying your phone along. They also lack the advanced training metrics that serious runners rely on.

GPS Running Watches: Built for Performance

Running watches, on the other hand, are purpose-built for athletes. They include accurate built-in GPS, barometric altimeters, and advanced metrics like cadence, stride length, and training load. They're tougher, more water-resistant, and designed to handle long battery life during multi-hour runs and races.

The downside is that they're usually bulkier and more expensive. If you're not a runner, the extra features can feel like overkill.

The good news? Many modern devices now sit comfortably in the middle, offering enough smart features for daily life while still delivering solid running performance.

Fitness Tracker with Heart Rate Monitor: What to Look For

Heart rate monitoring is a key feature in any fitness tracker, but accuracy varies. Optical sensors on the wrist have improved dramatically in recent years, but they still struggle with rapid heart rate changes during high-intensity intervals.

If accurate heart rate data is your priority, look for a device with a multi-LED sensor, like the Garmin Venu 3 or the Polar Vantage V3. These use multiple light wavelengths to improve accuracy across different skin tones and activity levels.

For most people, a wrist-based optical sensor is more than enough. But if you're a serious cyclist or doing intense interval training, you may want to pair your watch with a chest strap for the most reliable readings.

Calorie Tracking Watch for Workouts

If your primary goal is weight management, you want a calorie tracking watch for workouts that provides consistent and reliable estimates. No wearable is 100% accurate when it comes to calorie burn—they're all estimates based on your heart rate, age, weight, and activity level. But some devices are more consistent than others.

The Fitbit Charge 6 and Garmin Venu 3 both do an excellent job here. Fitbit's calorie algorithm has been refined over years of data, and Garmin's is based on the same Firstbeat analytics used by professional sports teams.

FAQ

Do I need a GPS running watch if I run with my phone?

If you always run with your phone, a fitness tracker with connected GPS can work fine. However, a dedicated GPS running watch is more accurate, drains your phone's battery less, and is essential if you want to track pace and distance without carrying your phone.

Can a fitness tracker accurately track calories burned?

Fitness trackers provide estimates based on your heart rate and personal metrics. They're useful for understanding trends and comparing workouts, but they're not medically precise. For the most accurate calorie data, pair your watch with a chest strap heart rate monitor.

What is the difference between a smartwatch and a fitness tracker?

A smartwatch offers broader functionality like apps, music, and payments, while a fitness tracker focuses primarily on health and activity metrics. Many modern devices, like the Garmin Venu 3, combine both, but fitness trackers generally have longer battery life and a lighter design.

Final Thoughts

The right wearable depends on your goals. If you want a comfortable, all-day device that tracks your health and encourages daily movement, a fitness tracker like the Fitbit Charge 6 is an excellent choice. If you're training for a race and want accurate GPS, advanced metrics, and long battery life, invest in a dedicated running watch like the Garmin Forerunner 265.

There's no single "best" device—only the best one for your lifestyle. Consider what you value most: battery life, accuracy, design, or price, and choose accordingly. Whichever you pick, having reliable data on your wrist is a powerful motivator to keep moving.
