Best Investing Apps for Beginners: Acorns vs Stash vs Robinhood Compared

Starting to invest can feel intimidating, especially when you're working with a limited budget. The good news? You no longer need thousands of dollars to enter the market. Micro-investing apps and beginner-friendly brokerages have made it possible to start with as little as $1.

In this comparison, we'll break down three of the most popular options for new investors: Acorns, Stash, and Robinhood. Each takes a different approach, and the right choice depends on your goals, budget, and how hands-on you want to be.

What Are Micro-Investing Apps?

Micro-investing apps allow you to invest small amounts of money—often by rounding up everyday purchases or setting aside a few dollars at a time. They're designed for people who want to build wealth gradually without needing a large upfront investment.

These apps typically fall into two categories:

  • Robo-advisors that automatically manage a diversified portfolio for you
  • Self-directed brokerages where you choose your own investments

Understanding which category fits your personality is the first step toward picking the right app.

Acorns: Best for Hands-Off Investors

Acorns is one of the most well-known micro-investing apps in the US. It's built around the concept of "round-ups"—when you spend $3.50 on coffee, Acorns rounds it to $4.00 and invests the $0.50 difference.

How Acorns Works

After linking a debit or credit card, Acorns automatically invests your spare change into a diversified portfolio of ETFs. You can also set up recurring transfers starting at $5.

The app offers several tiers:

  • Personal: A basic taxable investment account
  • Personal Plus: Adds a checking account and retirement account access
  • Premium: Includes custodial accounts for kids and more features

Acorns Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • Extremely hands-off—set it and forget it
  • Round-ups make investing effortless
  • Educational content built into the app

Cons:

  • Monthly fees ($3–$12 depending on tier)
  • Limited control over your portfolio
  • Best suited for US residents only

Acorns is ideal if you want a completely automated experience and don't mind paying a small monthly fee for convenience.

Stash: Best for Learning While Investing

Stash takes a slightly different approach. It's part robo-advisor, part educational platform. The app focuses on teaching beginners how to invest while giving them the tools to do so.

How Stash Works

Stash offers curated portfolios based on your goals and risk tolerance. You can also build your own portfolio by selecting individual stocks and ETFs. The app includes "Stash Coach" lessons that explain investing concepts in plain English.

Stash Pricing

  • Stash Growth: $3/month for a personal investment account
  • Stash+: $9/month for two custodial accounts and more features

Stash Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • Strong educational component
  • Low minimum to start ($5)
  • Offers fractional shares

Cons:

  • Monthly fees add up over time
  • Investment options are somewhat limited
  • US-only platform

Stash works well for beginners who want to learn as they invest and don't mind paying for guidance.

Robinhood: Best for Self-Directed Beginners

Robinhood disrupted the brokerage industry by offering commission-free trades. Unlike Acorns and Stash, Robinhood doesn't manage your money for you—it gives you the tools to pick your own investments.

How Robinhood Works

You open an account, deposit funds, and choose from thousands of stocks, ETFs, and options. Robinhood also offers fractional shares, so you can buy a slice of expensive stocks like Amazon or Tesla with just a few dollars.

Robinhood Features

  • Commission-free trades on stocks and ETFs
  • Fractional shares starting at $1
  • Robinhood Gold ($5/month) for margin investing and research
  • Crypto trading available in most states

Robinhood Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • No monthly fees for basic accounts
  • Wide range of investment options
  • User-friendly interface

Cons:

  • No retirement accounts (IRA) currently
  • Less hand-holding for complete beginners
  • You're responsible for your own investment choices

Robinhood is best for beginners who want more control and don't mind doing their own research.

How to Start Investing with $100

If you have $100 to start, here's a simple approach:

  1. Choose your app based on how hands-on you want to be
  2. Open and fund your account—most apps take minutes to set up
  3. Set a recurring transfer—even $10/week adds up over time
  4. Pick a diversified option—ETFs or a managed portfolio reduce risk
  5. Leave it alone—time in the market beats timing the market

Remember: investing involves risk, and you should only invest money you won't need in the short term.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best investing app for beginners with $100?

All three apps work well with $100. Acorns and Stash are best if you want automation and guidance. Robinhood is best if you want to choose your own investments without paying monthly fees.

Are micro-investing apps safe?

Yes, reputable micro-investing apps are regulated and typically SIPC-insured in the US (protecting up to $500,000). However, investments themselves carry market risk—you can lose money.

Can I use Acorns, Stash, or Robinhood in Europe?

Acorns and Stash are US-only. Robinhood has expanded to the UK and select EU countries, but availability varies. European investors should consider alternatives like eToro, Trade Republic, or Scalable Capital.

Final Thoughts

The best investing app for beginners depends on your preferences. If you want zero effort, Acorns is hard to beat. If you want to learn as you go, Stash offers excellent educational tools. If you prefer full control without monthly fees, Robinhood is a solid choice.

Whichever you pick, the most important step is simply starting. Even small, consistent investments can grow significantly over time.
