10 Stylish Entryway Bench Ideas to Upgrade Your Mudroom

Your entryway is the first thing you see when you walk in the door — and the last thing you see as you leave. It sets the tone for your whole home. But in many houses and apartments, this space becomes a dumping ground for shoes, bags, and mail. The solution? A well-chosen entryway bench. A good bench gives you a place to sit while pulling on boots, corrals clutter with built-in storage, and instantly makes your mudroom feel intentional. Whether you're working with a tiny rental foyer or a sprawling mudroom, here are ten entryway bench ideas to help you find the perfect fit.

1. The Classic Entryway Bench with Storage

If you want your mudroom to work harder, start with an entryway bench with storage. These pieces typically feature a hinged top that lifts to reveal a hidden compartment inside. They're perfect for stashing winter scarves, reusable grocery bags, or off-season shoes that you don't want sitting out.

The beauty of this style is its simplicity. A clean, flat wood top gives you a place to drop your keys or set down a grocery bag while you close the door. Inside, you can keep things out of sight but still easy to grab. Look for one with a sturdy wooden frame and a soft-close hinge so the top doesn't slam down on little fingers.

2. Mudroom Bench with Shoe Rack Combo

Let's be honest: the biggest clutter culprit in any entryway is footwear. That's why an entryway bench with shoe rack is arguably the most practical mudroom bench idea on this list. Instead of a solid base, these benches have an open shelf or tiered rack beneath the seat.

The open design means you can kick off your sneakers and slide them right underneath, no bending down or opening a cabinet door. Many models offer two or three tiers, which is perfect for a family of four. If you're short on floor space, feel free to use just the top tier for daily shoes and the lower tier for seasonal pairs.

3. Narrow Benches for Small Entryway Furniture

Not every home has a spacious mudroom. If your entryway is basically a hallway, you need small entryway furniture that works with your square footage. A narrow bench — typically around 10 to 12 inches deep — offers a spot to sit without crowding the path from the front door to the living room.

Many narrow benches are designed to sit flush against the wall, with slim legs and a compact profile. Some even come with a small shoe shelf tucked underneath. This is one of the simplest ways to add function to a cramped entryway without undergoing a major renovation. Pair it with a wall-mounted coat hook, and you've got a complete drop zone in less than three feet of wall space.

4. Modern Entryway Bench with a Light Frame

For a more contemporary look, consider a modern entryway bench with a minimalist metal frame and a solid wood seat. These benches often have clean lines and a slightly elevated look, which keeps a small space from feeling boxy.

The open space under a metal-framed bench also gives you flexibility. You can slide a woven basket underneath for storage or leave it open to make the hallway feel larger. Choose a bench with black metal legs and a light oak seat for a look that works in both farmhouse and modern apartments. This is an especially smart choice if your entryway has dark flooring — the lighter seat will help brighten the area.

5. Cubbie-Style Entryway Bench

If you need dedicated spots for each family member, a bench with open cubbies is a great mudroom bench idea. Unlike a closed storage compartment, cubbies let you see what's inside, which makes it easier for kids to put their shoes away and for you to grab what you need quickly.

Most cubbie benches come in two configurations: a bench seat on one side with cubbies below, or cubbies built into a tall cabinet alongside the bench. The latter is excellent if you need a place for both shoes and backpacks. You can even buy fabric bins that fit inside the cubbies to hide smaller items like gloves and hats.

6. Upholstered Bench for Coat and Shoe Hub

An upholstered bench adds a touch of softness to what is usually a very utilitarian space. These benches are a fantastic choice if you want to sit down comfortably while lacing up your boots. Many upholstered entryway benches come with a built-in shoe rack or a fabric-covered base, which hides clutter while still being lightweight.

Look for performance fabrics that can withstand wet snow, pet hair, and daily wear. Cushioned benches also make the space feel more inviting — they're the kind of piece that makes guests want to linger a little longer. If your entryway leads directly into an open living area, an upholstered bench can double as extra seating when you entertain.

7. Hall Tree Bench with Hooks and Shelves

When your entryway needs maximum function, a hall tree bench is the all-in-one solution. This style combines a bench with an upper backrest that features coat hooks and often a shelf or cubby on top. It's a single piece of furniture that handles coats, shoes, hats, and bags all at once.

These are especially useful for busy households. Instead of wrestling with multiple pieces of furniture, you get one solid unit that anchors the entire mudroom. A tall hall tree can also make a narrow entryway feel more substantial, giving it the visual weight of a proper mudroom. Look for one with a mirror, which is a nice bonus for a final check before you head out the door.

8. Bench with Drawers Plus a Shoe Rack

For a more polished look, consider an entryway bench with storage drawers on the sides and an open shoe rack in the center. This hybrid design gives you the best of both worlds: closed compartments for items you'd rather hide and an open spot for everyday shoes.

Drawers are great for mail, leashes, or umbrellas. The open center keeps your most-worn shoes easy to grab on your way out. This style also tends to look more like a piece of living room furniture than a mudroom utility piece, so it's a good option for apartments where the entryway flows directly into the main living space.

9. Compact Folding Bench for Apartments

If you're renting or have a very tight entryway, a folding bench can be a game-changer. Some designs mount to the wall and fold flat when not in use, while others are freestanding and portable. They're the ultimate solution when you need a seat occasionally but can't spare the floor space full-time.

Wall-mounted folding benches take up only a few inches of floor space when folded, yet they're sturdy enough to hold an adult while putting on shoes. This is an excellent option for apartment dwellers who want the function of an entryway bench without the bulk. Pair it with a few wall hooks, and your tiny foyer instantly becomes a functional mudroom.

10. Wooden Bench with Cushion in a Light Finish

Last but not least, a simple wooden bench with a removable cushion is the most versatile option of all. You can choose a light wood finish to keep things airy or a dark finish for contrast. The best part is the cushion — you can easily swap the fabric as your decor changes without buying a whole new piece of furniture.

Many of these benches come in a solid wood frame with a lower shelf, making them perfect for shoe storage without sacrificing style. It's the kind of piece that works in a farmhouse, a mid-century modern apartment, or a coastal cottage. Plus, if you ever move, this bench can easily transition to the foot of a bed or a hallway.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size entryway bench should I choose for a small entryway?

For a small entryway, look for a bench that is between 30 and 40 inches wide and no more than 12 to 14 inches deep. This gives you room to sit while still leaving a comfortable walking path. Wall-mounted folding benches are another great option when floor space is very tight.

Is an entryway bench with storage worth it?

Absolutely. An entryway bench with storage serves two purposes: it gives you a seat and helps you hide clutter. A lift-top bench can store seasonal shoes, scarves, and bags, while a bench with open shelves keeps everyday footwear within easy reach. If you're decorating a mudroom or a small apartment entryway, it's one of the smartest pieces of small entryway furniture, you can invest in.

How do I style an entryway bench to look inviting?

Start with a few practical touches: a basket for shoes or mail, a decorative tray for keys and change, and a soft throw pillow on the seat. Add a floor mirror or some wall art above the bench to define the space. Keep the area around the bench uncluttered so it feels welcoming rather than chaotic.

Final Thoughts

Your entryway deserves better than a pile of shoes and a doorknob full of coats. A thoughtful bench can transform this high-traffic area into one of the most functional and attractive spots in your home. Whether you opt for a modern entryway bench with clean lines, a cushioned seat, or a full hall tree, the right pick will make coming and going a more pleasant experience every single day.

Start by measuring your space, thinking about your storage needs, and choosing a style that fits your home. Then simply add some hooks, a rug, and a tray for the little things — you'll have an entryway that's ready for anything.
