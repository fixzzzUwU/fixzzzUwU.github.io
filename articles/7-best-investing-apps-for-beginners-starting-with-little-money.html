7 Best Investing Apps for Beginners Starting with Little Money

If you’ve been putting off investing because you think you need thousands of dollars to get started, here’s the good news: you don’t. Thanks to a new wave of micro-investing apps and beginner-friendly brokerages, you can open an account, buy fractional shares, and build a real portfolio with as little as $1—or even less.

For millennials and Gen Z, the biggest barrier to investing has never been interest. It’s been access, confidence, and that awkward feeling of not knowing exactly where to begin. These apps solve all three. They're designed to be intuitive, low-cost, and flexible enough to work with whatever you can afford to put in each month.

If you’ve been searching for the best investing apps for beginners, you’ve probably come across names like Acorns and Stash. But the truth is, there are several excellent options depending on your style, your goals, and how hands-on you want to be. Below, we break down the top seven apps that make it easy to start investing with little money.

Why You Don’t Need a Lot of Money to Start Investing

It’s a common misconception that investing is a rich person’s game. In reality, modern apps let you purchase fractional shares—meaning you can own a tiny slice of a company like Amazon or Apple for just a few dollars. You can also buy into index funds and ETFs with no minimum balance at all.

The habit matters more than the amount. If you can set aside even $10 or $25 per week, you’re building momentum. And with features like round-ups, automatic transfers, and recurring deposits, you can "set it and forget it" while your money grows over time.

That's exactly why micro-investing apps have become so popular. They remove the math, the jargon, and the intimidation factor. Instead, you get a clean interface, guided choices, and the confidence that comes from seeing your balance grow week after week.

The 7 Best Investing Apps for Beginners in 2025

We looked for apps that combine low fees, low minimums, educational content, and an interface that won’t make your head spin. Here are our top picks.

1. Acorns – Best for Spare Change Investing

Acorns is arguably the most famous micro-investing app, and for good reason. It connects to your debit or credit card and automatically rounds up your everyday purchases to the nearest dollar. That spare change gets invested into a diversified portfolio of ETFs.

It’s the ultimate "set it and forget it" option. You answer a few questions about your goals and risk tolerance, and Acorns builds a portfolio for you. There are also options for retirement accounts (Acorns Later) and a checking-like account (Acorns Checking) if you want to keep everything in one place.

Why it’s great for beginners: No decisions required. The app handles everything, and you can start with just a few dollars.

2. Stash – Best for Learning While Investing

Stash is another heavyweight in the beginner space, but it takes a different approach than Acorns. Instead of just investing your spare change, Stash encourages you to learn about what you're buying. You can choose from thousands of stocks and ETFs, with plenty of educational content to help you understand each choice.

One of Stash's standout features is its "slice" system. You can buy fractional shares of a single stock or ETF for as little as $1. The app also offers banking and retirement products, so it's a solid all-in-one financial hub.

Why it’s great for beginners: If you want to be more involved in picking your investments without feeling overwhelmed, Stash gives you training wheels and guidance.

3. Robinhood – Best for Commission-Free Trading

Robinhood changed the game when it introduced commission-free trading, and it remains one of the most popular apps for young investors. You can buy stocks, ETFs, options, and even cryptocurrency—all from a clean, mobile-first interface.

Robinhood doesn’t require a minimum deposit, and you can buy fractional shares, so $5 can get you started. It’s not heavy on educational content, and it’s more "DIY" than Acorns or Stash. But if you want the freedom to trade whenever you want, without fees eating into your small balance, Robinhood is a strong contender.

Why it’s great for beginners: Zero commissions and no account minimums make it easy to start experimenting with small amounts.

4. SoFi Invest – Best for All-in-One Finance

SoFi has evolved into a full-service personal finance platform, and its investing arm is perfect for beginners. You can start investing with no minimum balance, trade fractional shares, and access a wide range of ETFs. There’s also a "recurring investing" feature that lets you automatically buy into your favorite funds each week.

What sets SoFi apart is the broader ecosystem. You can manage your checking account, savings, loans, and investments all in one app. For younger users who want a single app for their entire financial life, SoFi is hard to beat.

Why it’s great for beginners: Free to use, no minimums, and plenty of extra financial tools built in.

5. Webull – Best for Growing Confidence

Webull is often compared to Robinhood, but it offers a bit more in the way of advanced charting and analysis tools. That might sound intimidating, but the app does a solid job of keeping things accessible for beginners too.

You can start with no minimum, buy fractional shares, and trade stocks, ETFs, and options. Webull also offers a paper trading mode, which lets you practice with virtual money before risking your own. That’s a huge plus if you want to learn without pressure.

Why it’s great for beginners: The paper trading feature is perfect for building confidence before going live.

6. Fidelity Bloom – Best for Building Saving Habits

Fidelity Bloom isn't exactly a traditional investing app—it’s designed to help you save and invest small amounts without thinking about it. The app splits your money into two buckets: a "Save" bucket and an "Invest" bucket. You can round up purchases, set up automatic transfers, and even earn rewards for hitting savings milestones.

Fidelity is one of the most trusted names in the industry, so you’re getting institutional-grade security with a beginner-friendly interface. There are no account fees and no minimums to get started.

Why it’s great for beginners: It gamifies the saving and investing process, which makes it easier to stay consistent.

7. M1 Finance – Best for Long-Term, Hands-Off Investors

M1 Finance sits somewhere between a robo-advisor and a traditional brokerage. You create a "pie" of stocks and ETFs you like, and M1 automatically manages your investments and rebalances your portfolio for you.

The best part? You can buy fractional shares and start with just $1. It's an incredibly efficient way to build a diversified portfolio over time, and because you set it up once, you don't have to fiddle with it daily. M1 also offers a credit card and checking account, but the investment side is where it really shines.

Why it’s great for beginners: If you want a hands-off approach but still want control over what you own, M1 gives you the best of both worlds.

Acorns vs. Stash: Which One Is Right for You?

If you're torn between the two biggest names in micro-investing, here's a simple way to think about it.

Choose Acorns if you want to invest completely passively. You don't want to research stocks. You just want your spare change and weekly deposits to go into a smart, diversified portfolio automatically. Acorns is about making investing effortless.

Choose Stash if you want to learn and feel in control. Stash lets you choose your own ETFs and stocks from a curated, beginner-friendly list. It’s more engaging, but it also requires a little more attention and curiosity.

Both are excellent. The "right" answer depends entirely on your personality. If you know yourself, you already know which one fits.

How to Start Investing with Little Money: 4 Simple Tips

If you’re ready to begin, these steps will make the process smooth and stress-free.

1. Pick one app and start small. Don’t overthink it. Download one app, connect a bank account, and make your first deposit—even if it’s just $5.

2. Automate your contributions. Set up weekly or monthly transfers. A $10 weekly deposit is $520 a year, and that doesn't include market growth. Consistency is the secret.

3. Turn on round-ups. Apps like Acorns make this easy. Your spare change adds up faster than you think.

4. Ignore the noise. Markets go up and down. As a beginner, your goal is to build the habit, not to time the market. Stay consistent and give your investments time to grow.

Conclusion

Starting to invest doesn’t require a huge bank account or a finance degree. The best investing apps for beginners have demolished those old barriers. Whether you choose a micro-investing app like Acorns to sweep up your spare change, or a more hands-on brokerage like Robinhood to buy fractional shares, the most important step is simply getting started.

With so many options available, there’s really no excuse to wait. Pick the app that matches your comfort level, set up a small recurring deposit, and let the power of compound growth do the heavy lifting. Your future self will thank you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I really start investing with just $5?

Yes, absolutely. Most of the apps on this list allow you to open an account with no minimum deposit, and many offer fractional shares so you can invest as little as $1 in companies you believe in. The key is consistency, not the initial amount.

What’s better for beginners: Acorns or Stash?

It depends on how hands-on you want to be. Acorns is completely automated and perfect if you want to invest your spare change without thinking about it. Stash is better if you want to learn about investing and make your own choices with guidance.

Are micro-investing apps safe?

Yes, they’re regulated and generally use bank-level encryption to protect your data. Your investments are also protected by SIPC insurance (up to $500,000) at most brokerage apps. Just be sure to choose a reputable app and review its fee structure before signing up.
