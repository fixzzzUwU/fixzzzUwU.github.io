Best Budgeting Apps for Beginners in 2025: 7 Apps That Actually Keep You on Track

If your money disappears faster than you can track it, you're not alone. Millions of millennials and young couples in the US and EU are living paycheck to paycheck, and the biggest hurdle isn't income—it's visibility. You can't fix a budget you can't see.

That's where budgeting apps come in. But here's the catch: most apps are either too complicated for beginners or too simplistic to be useful. So we tested the best budgeting apps for beginners in 2025 and narrowed it down to seven that actually keep you on track—without requiring a finance degree.

Whether you're a couple trying to merge finances, a solo budgeter tired of spreadsheets, or someone looking for how to budget paycheck to paycheck without stress, this comparison will help you pick the right app.

What Makes a Budgeting App Beginner-Friendly?

Before we dive into the picks, let's set the criteria. A good beginner budgeting app should:

  • Sync with your bank automatically (manual entry is a motivation killer)
  • Show your spending clearly without overwhelming dashboards
  • Offer a simple system—not just charts, but actual guidance
  • Work for couples if you share expenses
  • Have a free or low-cost tier so you can test before committing

Now let's look at the best picks.

Best Picks: 7 Budgeting Apps That Keep You on Track

1. YNAB (You Need A Budget) — Best for Paycheck-to-Paycheck Budgeting

YNAB is famous for its "give every dollar a job" philosophy. It's not just an expense tracker—it's a budgeting method built into an app. For anyone wondering how to budget paycheck to paycheck, YNAB's four rules (especially "roll with the punches" and "age your money") are genuinely transformative.

It syncs with your bank, but it does require a learning curve. The upside is a massive library of free workshops and a supportive community. It's also excellent for couples because you can share a budget with unlimited devices.

Best for: Couples and beginners willing to learn a system.

YNAB

2. Rocket Money — Best for Finding Hidden Expenses

Rocket Money (formerly Truebill) is less of a traditional budget app and more of a money-saving assistant. It scans your accounts to find forgotten subscriptions, negotiates bills for you, and gives you a clear view of your recurring expenses.

If you're living paycheck to paycheck, subscription creep is a silent budget killer. Rocket Money catches it fast. It's a great first step before you commit to a full budgeting system.

Best for: Beginners who want quick wins and lower bills.

Rocket Money

3. PocketGuard — Best for "How Much Can I Spend Today?"

PocketGuard answers the simplest budgeting question: after bills and savings, how much is left for spending? Its "In My Pocket" feature shows you exactly that—no complex categories, no spreadsheet energy.

It's one of the best budgeting apps for beginners because it doesn't overwhelm you with charts. It just says: here's what's safe to spend. It's also a solid expense tracker app that syncs with your bank.

Best for: Solo budgeters who want simplicity over structure.

PocketGuard

4. Simplifi by Quicken — Best Mint Alternative for Beginners

If you've been mourning the loss of Mint, Simplifi is the closest replacement in spirit—clean, visual, and easy to set up. It connects to your accounts and gives you a spending plan, watchlists, and real-time progress bars.

It's less rigid than YNAB and more proactive than PocketGuard. For couples who want a shared financial picture without a full budgeting philosophy, Simplifi is a great middle ground.

Best for: Mint refugees and visual learners.

Simplifi

5. EveryDollar — Best Zero-Based Budgeting for Couples

EveryDollar uses the zero-based budgeting method: income minus expenses equals zero. It's fast to set up and very intuitive, especially if you're a Dave Ramsey fan. The free version requires manual entry (which can actually help you stay mindful), while the premium version syncs with your bank.

For a budgeting app for couples, EveryDollar's shared dashboard is straightforward and easy to discuss weekly.

Best for: Couples who want a simple, disciplined system.

EveryDollar

6. Monarch Money — Best All-in-One for Couples

Monarch Money is a newer Mint alternative that's gained serious traction. It combines budgeting, tracking, and goal-setting with a clean, collaborative interface. You can add your partner, assign transactions, and see your net worth in one place.

It's not free, but the design and couple-friendly features make it one of the best budgeting apps for beginners who want to grow into a fuller system.

Best for: Young couples building long-term financial habits.

Monarch Money

7. Copilot Money — Best Design-First Budgeting App

Copilot is iPhone-only for now, but it's arguably the best-looking expense tracker app that syncs with your bank. It uses AI to categorize transactions and provides weekly and monthly insights that are actually readable.

It's not the cheapest option, but for millennials who value design and automation, it's a joy to use—and that means you'll actually stick with it.

Best for: Design-conscious solo budgeters.

Copilot Money

How to Choose the Right Budgeting App for You

Here's a quick decision guide:

  • You want a system and don't mind a learning curve: YNAB
  • You're drowning in subscriptions: Rocket Money
  • You want a simple "safe to spend" number: PocketGuard
  • You miss Mint: Simplifi or Monarch
  • You're a couple who wants a shared budget: EveryDollar or Monarch
  • You prioritize design and automation: Copilot

How to Budget Paycheck to Paycheck Without Burning Out

The biggest mistake beginners make is trying to track every penny manually. Don't. Use an app that syncs with your bank, set a few clear categories, and check in once a week. Ten minutes of weekly review beats two hours of daily guilt.

Also, start with one goal—like saving $500 or cutting one subscription. Budgeting apps are tools, not transformations. The habit is what keeps you on track.

Conclusion

The best budgeting apps for beginners in 2025 aren't the most feature-packed—they're the ones you'll actually use. Whether you choose YNAB for its method, Rocket Money for quick savings, or Simplifi as a Mint alternative, the key is to start small and stay consistent.

Pick one app, give it a two-week trial, and let the visibility do the heavy lifting. Your paycheck will thank you.
