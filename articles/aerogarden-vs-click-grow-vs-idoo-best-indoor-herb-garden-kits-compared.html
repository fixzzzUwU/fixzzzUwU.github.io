AeroGarden vs Click & Grow vs iDOO: Best Indoor Herb Garden Kits Compared

Fresh basil in January, parsley that doesn’t turn limp in a week, and mint ready whenever you want tea — that’s the appeal of an indoor herb garden. But once you start shopping, the options blur together. AeroGarden, Click & Grow, and iDOO are three of the most popular names, and each takes a slightly different approach to helping you grow herbs indoors.

This comparison breaks down how they differ in ease of use, plant selection, maintenance, and long-term value. Whether you’re looking for the best indoor herb garden kit for a small apartment or a hydroponic garden for beginners who want low-maintenance greenery, here’s how to choose.

What to Look for in an Indoor Herb Garden Kit

Before comparing brands, it helps to know which features matter most. Most indoor kits share the same basic setup: a water reservoir, a grow light, and a place for plant pods or seed baskets. The differences come down to how much control and convenience you want.

Grow Light and Footprint

A herb garden kit with grow light is essential for year-round growing, especially in apartments with limited natural light. Look for an adjustable light arm if you plan to grow taller herbs like dill or basil. Also measure your counter space — a compact three-pod kit fits almost anywhere, while larger systems need more room.

Capacity and Plant Selection

Small kits are great for a few herbs, but they fill up fast. If you want to grow basil, parsley, cilantro, and mint at the same time, choose a system with more pods. Also check whether the kit uses proprietary seed pods or allows you to add your own seeds. Proprietary pods are easier; DIY baskets are more flexible and often cheaper over time.

Maintenance and Smart Features

Every kit requires some maintenance, but “low-maintenance” means different things. Some systems remind you when to add water or nutrients. Others use self-watering reservoirs and pre-seeded pods that eliminate guesswork. An indoor smart garden may include app connectivity or automated lighting, but simple timers and water indicators are often enough.

Pod Cost and Availability

The upfront price is only part of the story. Seed pod refills vary widely in cost and availability. Click & Grow uses proprietary plant pods, AeroGarden sells its own pod kits and seed starter systems, and many iDOO models let you use your own seeds with sponges and baskets. Consider how often you’ll replant and whether you prefer convenience or lower refill costs.

AeroGarden Review: The Flexible All-Rounder

AeroGarden is the most recognizable name in indoor hydroponics, and for good reason. Its kits are beginner-friendly, widely available, and supported by a large selection of seed pods. Most models include a built-in grow light, a water reservoir, and simple controls. Many also provide reminders for water and nutrients, which helps new growers avoid common mistakes.

The biggest strength of AeroGarden is flexibility. You can grow herbs, lettuce, cherry tomatoes, and even flowers depending on the model. Larger units offer more height for taller plants, while compact models fit small kitchens. Some higher-end AeroGarden systems include app connectivity and more advanced scheduling, but even the basic models are easy to run.

Pros: Strong beginner guidance, wide pod selection, multiple sizes, good for herbs and beyond.

Cons: Proprietary pods can add up, and larger models take up more counter space.

Best for: Anyone who wants a flexible indoor smart garden with plenty of support and plant options.

Click & Grow: The Low-Maintenance Indoor Smart Garden

Click & Grow takes a different approach. Its indoor smart garden systems use pre-seeded plant pods and a self-watering reservoir, so you mostly just add water and let the built-in LED light do the rest. The smart soil in each pod contains nutrients, which reduces the need to mix fertilizer.

This is one of the easiest systems for busy people. There’s no guessing about seed depth or nutrient strength, and the pods are clearly labeled. The tradeoff is flexibility: Click & Grow is designed around its own pod ecosystem, so you’re limited to the plants it offers. Refill pods cost more than starting herbs from seed, but you’re paying for convenience and consistency.

Pros: Very low maintenance, clean design, excellent for beginners and small spaces.

Cons: Proprietary pods limit plant choice, refills are pricier than DIY seed starting.

Best for: Apartment dwellers who want a truly low-effort indoor smart garden.

iDOO: The Budget-Friendly Hydroponic Garden for Beginners

iDOO kits are popular for one main reason: value. Many iDOO hydroponic garden kits include multiple pods, an LED grow light, and a water pump at a lower price than comparable name-brand systems. If you want to try hydroponic gardening without a big upfront investment, iDOO is a sensible starting point.

Many iDOO models use reusable baskets and sponges, which means you can start your own seeds instead of buying proprietary pods. That can lower long-term costs, but it also means more hands-on work. You may need to manage nutrients yourself and learn how each plant behaves. Build quality and instructions vary by model, so read reviews for the specific kit you’re considering.

Pros: Budget-friendly, often includes many pods, works with your own seeds, good for experimenting.

Cons: Less polished guidance, more manual nutrient management, quality varies by model.

Best for: Budget-conscious growers who don’t mind a little trial and error.

FAQ

Do indoor herb garden kits really work without sunlight?

Yes, most kits include a grow light that provides the light plants need. You still need to keep the light on a consistent schedule and choose herbs that suit indoor growing.

How often do I need to add water or nutrients?

It depends on the system and plant size. Self-watering kits like Click & Grow need less frequent refilling, while hydroponic systems often require water checks every few days and nutrients on a schedule.

Can I grow herbs other than basil in these kits?

Absolutely. Parsley, mint, cilantro, dill, thyme, and chives are common choices. Check the kit’s pod options and growing height, especially if you choose a compact model.

Conclusion
