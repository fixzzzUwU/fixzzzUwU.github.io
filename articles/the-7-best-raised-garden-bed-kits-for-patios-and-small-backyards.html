What to Look For in a Raised Garden Bed Kit

Before you make a purchase, think about these few quick points:

  • Depth: 11–12 inches is fine for herbs and leafy greens; 15–17 inches is better for root vegetables and larger plants.
  • Material: Galvanized steel is rust-proof and durable; cedar is naturally rot-resistant but will eventually need replacement.
  • Patio compatibility: If the bed sits on a wooden deck, check that it either has legs or that you are comfortable with direct contact. If it sits on concrete, make sure water can drain away without staining.
  • Width: Stick to beds no wider than 4 feet so you can reach the center from both sides without stepping on the soil.
  • Assembly: Look for rounded edges and included hardware. The best kits come together in under an hour without special tools.

These small details make a huge difference when you are growing your own vegetables in a covered urban patio or balcony garden.

FAQ

Can you put a raised garden bed directly on a patio or concrete balcony?

Yes, but be careful with drainage. A metal raised bed with legs and a bottom tray prevents rust stains and water pooling. If the bed does not have legs, rest it on brick or pavers so water can escape freely, and protect the surface underneath with a waterproof liner or tray.

What is the best size raised garden bed for small yards?

For most small yards, a bed that is 24–36 inches wide and at least 11 inches deep is ideal. Keep the length around 4–6 feet, or use a modular system so you can adjust it to fit odd corners. Two smaller beds often work better than one large bed because you can rotate crops and use the space more efficiently.

Do I need a liner inside a galvanized raised garden bed?

It is a good idea to line the inside with landscape fabric or a specialty bed liner. Besides keeping soil from escaping through seams, a liner adds a protective barrier between the soil and galvanized steel, prolonging the bed’s life. Just make sure the liner still allows water to drain out the bottom.
