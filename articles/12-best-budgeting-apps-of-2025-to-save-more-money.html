12 Best Budgeting Apps of 2025 to Save More Money

Budgeting doesn’t have to mean endless spreadsheets and guilt. The best budgeting apps of 2025 turn money management into something you can handle in five minutes, whether you want to track every transaction or simply set a savings goal and walk away. For millennials and young families, the right app can be the difference between living paycheck to paycheck and building real momentum. So we tested and rounded up the best budgeting apps that actually help you keep more of your money.

How to Budget Monthly Without the Headache

The simplest way to budget monthly is to list your income, subtract your fixed expenses, and then assign the rest to categories like groceries, gas, and savings. A good app does the heavy lifting for you by tracking spending, flagging overspending, and showing exactly what’s left.

If you’re looking for a budgeting app for beginners, avoid tools that force you to learn complicated accounting. Instead, choose one that feels natural—something you’ll actually open each week. The goal is progress, not perfection.

What to Look for in a Budgeting App

Before you download anything, consider what you need:

  • Bank syncing – Automatic transaction import saves time.
  • Budgeting style – Envelope, zero-based, or simple tracking.
  • Shared access – Useful for couples and family budgets.
  • Free tier or trial – A solid free plan means you can test before committing.

The 12 Best Budgeting Apps of 2025

1. YNAB (You Need a Budget)

Best for hands-on budgeters who want every dollar to have a job. YNAB uses a zero-based method: give every dollar a purpose, whether that’s bills, savings, or fun money. It’s about $14.99 a month, but the 34-day free trial gives you time to see results. If you’re weighing YNAB vs EveryDollar, the short version is this—YNAB wins on bank syncing, flexibility, and long-term habit building, while EveryDollar is simpler and has a genuinely useful free version.

2. EveryDollar

If you follow Dave Ramsey’s approach, EveryDollar is your app. It’s also built on the zero-based system, but the free plan is fully manual, which forces you to think about every purchase. The paid version adds automatic bank sync and recurring transactions. For a budgeting app for beginners who want structure without a steep learning curve, EveryDollar is a strong start.

3. Monarch Money

Monarch is one of the most polished all-in-one apps on the market. It connects all your accounts, tracks your net worth, and lets you build a budget as a couple. It’s a great choice if you need a clear view of shared expenses and long-term savings goals. The interface feels modern and refreshingly simple—no spreadsheet flashbacks here.

4. Rocket Money

Rocket Money is less about strict budgeting and more about finding wasted cash. It scans your recurring subscriptions, negotiates bills on your behalf, and alerts you to charges you forgot about. The free version covers the essentials, and the optional premium membership often pays for itself with one cancelled subscription. Use it alongside a separate budgeting app for the best results.

5. Simplifi by Quicken

Simplifi is the “no-pressure” budgeting app. It gives you a clean picture of what’s left to spend after bills and savings goals, without forcing you into a specific method. It’s ideal if you want to understand how to budget monthly but don’t want to obsess over categories. At around $4 a month, it’s one of the most affordable paid options.

6. PocketGuard

PocketGuard answers one question: how much can I actually spend today? It connects your accounts, subtracts bills and savings goals, and shows you a remaining “in your pocket” amount. It’s simple, visual, and perfect for overspenders who need a reality check. The free version is genuinely useful, and the premium plan adds extra features like custom categories.

7. Goodbudget

Goodbudget uses the classic envelope system in digital form. You divide your income into envelopes—groceries, dining, entertainment—and spend only what’s inside each one. It doesn’t sync to your bank, which sounds old-school, but that’s exactly why it works for people who want to be deliberate with every dollar. You can share envelopes with a partner, making it a great family budgeting app.

8. Honeydue

Honeydue is built for couples. Both partners get a shared view of their combined balances, bills, and spending habits, but you can choose which transactions to keep private. It’s free, which makes it one of the best low-risk tools for young families learning to budget together. Honeydue also sends reminders for upcoming bills so nothing slips through the cracks.

9. Copilot Money

Copilot is the most beautiful budgeting app you’ll find. It uses smart rules to automatically categorize your spending and offers a really intuitive calendar view. It’s only available on Apple devices, and it’s a paid subscription, but for design lovers who actually enjoy opening the app, it’s worth it. If your daily driver is an iPhone, Copilot is a top-tier pick.

10. Empower (Personal Capital)

Empower is best known for investing tools and net worth tracking, but it also includes a solid cash-flow planner and budgeting dashboard. It’s free for the budgeting features, which makes it appealing if you want to keep an eye on your financial big picture. Use it to track savings growth, retirement accounts, and monthly spending in one place.

11. Fudget

Fudget is the anti-budget app. There’s no bank linking, no complicated categories, and no charts. You just add income lines and expense lines, and it shows you your running balance. It’s perfect for people who feel intimidated by finance apps but still want to track where their money goes. Honestly, it might be the easiest way to start saving more money this year.

12. Oportun (formerly Digit)

Oportun doesn’t build your budget—it builds your savings. It analyzes your income and spending patterns, then automatically moves small amounts into a savings account. It’s a companion tool that works beautifully alongside any budgeting app. If you’re the type who spends first and panics later, Oportun does the saving for you.

Conclusion

You don’t need to be a spreadsheet wizard to save more money. Whether you choose a detailed zero-based tool like YNAB, a free option like EveryDollar, or an effortless savings sidekick like Oportun, the best budgeting apps of 2025 all share one thing: they make progress automatic. Start with one app, set aside five minutes each week to review it, and watch your savings grow.

FAQ

What is the best budgeting app for beginners?

EveryDollar is a great starting point because its free plan is simple, structured, and based on the zero-based method. Fudget is another option if you want something even simpler. Both help you understand how to budget monthly without overwhelming you with features.

How do I choose between YNAB and EveryDollar?

The main difference is bank syncing and flexibility. YNAB automatically imports your transactions and handles irregular income beautifully, but it costs about $14.99 a month. EveryDollar’s free version is fully manual, which some people find more intentional. Choose YNAB if you want automation, and EveryDollar if you want a free, structured start.

Are free budget apps actually safe?

Yes. Reputable free apps like Honeydue, PocketGuard, and EveryDollar use bank-level encryption to protect your data. Still, you should always use unique passwords and enable two-factor authentication. Linking your bank account to a budgeting app is generally safe as long as you stick to well-known, established providers.
