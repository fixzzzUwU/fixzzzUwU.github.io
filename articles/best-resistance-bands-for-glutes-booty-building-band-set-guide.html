What to Look for in a Glute Resistance Band Set

Not all resistance bands are created equal. The word "resistance" is just the beginning. If you want a set that lasts and performs, keep these factors in mind.

Fabric vs. Latex Bands

This is the biggest decision you’ll make. Latex loop bands are thinner, cheaper, and usually come in a pack of five with varying resistance levels. They’re great for beginners and for exercises where you don’t want too much friction.

Fabric bands, on the other hand, have a soft, non-slip texture that stays in place on your thighs without rolling down or pinching. They feel sturdier and are generally preferred for deep, glute-focused moves like banded walks and hip thrusts. If you’ve ever had a latex band snap mid-squat, you already know why fabric is worth considering.

Resistance Levels

You don’t want a set where every band feels identical. The best booty bands for growing glutes come in multiple levels — typically light, medium, heavy, and extra heavy — so you can start with a lighter band and progress without buying a whole new set. If you’re a beginner, look for a set with increasing resistance rather than a single band.

Length and Width

Width matters more than you think. Narrow bands dig into your thighs and can create painful lines (and bruises). Wider bands distribute pressure evenly, making those lateral walks and squats much more comfortable. Also check the length — a band that’s too short will be nearly impossible to get into position without awkwardly shimmying.

Material Quality and Stitching

A band is only as good as its stitching. Look for reinforced seams and high-density fabric. The last thing you want is a band that frays after a few weeks of use. Read reviews and steer clear of brands with consistent complaints about snags or tearing.

Resistance Band Glute Exercises to Get You Started

You’ve got your bands — now it’s time to use them. Here’s a quick primer of effective resistance band glute exercises to include in your routine, no matter your level.

Banded Glute Bridge

Place the band just above your knees, lie on your back with your feet flat, and drive your hips upward. Make sure to push your knees outward against the band as you rise. This keeps tension on the glutes and prevents the quads from taking over.

Monster Walk

Step into a light-to-medium band around your ankles. In a quarter-squat position, take small, deliberate steps forward and backward. Keep your chest up and your core engaged. Don’t go too far — the lateral pressure is the point.

Frog Pump

Lie on your back and bring the soles of your feet together, letting your knees fall open. Place a band around your thighs. Press your feet into the floor and drive your hips up, squeezing your glutes at the top. This move burns quickly, so it’s perfect as a finisher.

Banded Kickback

Loop a band around both feet, get on all fours, and kick one heel toward the ceiling. Control the movement on the way down. Keep your hips level and avoid arching your lower back. This isolates the glute on one side and helps correct imbalances.

Side-Lying Leg Raise

With a band around your ankles, lie on your side and lift your top leg as high as you can without tilting your hips. This targets the glute medius, which is key for that round, lifted shape from the side. Slow and steady beats fast and sloppy here.

Aim for 3 rounds of 15–20 reps per exercise, and remember: the last few reps in every set are where the results happen. If you’re not struggling a little, size up to the next band.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I choose the right resistance level for booty bands?

Start with the lightest band that makes the movement challenging while still allowing good form. For isolation moves like lateral walks, you might need a lighter band; for glute bridges and hip thrusts, you can usually go heavier. The best booty bands for growing glutes will force you to work, but they shouldn’t compromise your technique.

Can you build glutes with just resistance bands?

Yes. You can absolutely build and strengthen your glutes with resistance bands alone, especially if you consistently increase the resistance over time. They may not add massive size as quickly as heavy barbell training, but for home workouts, they’re one of the most effective and accessible tools you can use.

How often should I train my glutes with bands?

Two to three focused sessions per week with at least one rest day in between tends to work well for most women. That frequency allows your glutes enough stimulus to grow while giving your muscles time to recover. Keep each session to about 20–30 minutes if you’re doing it purely with resistance.
