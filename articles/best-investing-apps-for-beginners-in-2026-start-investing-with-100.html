Best Investing Apps for Beginners in 2026: Start Investing with $100

You don't need a big pile of cash to start investing. You need an app that lets you buy small amounts, charges almost nothing, and doesn't bury you in jargon. A hundred dollars is genuinely enough to open an account, buy a diversified fund, and learn how markets feel when real money is on the line.

The hard part isn't the amount — it's picking a platform. The best investing apps for beginners in 2026 have three things in common: no account minimums, fractional shares so your $100 buys a full slice of a diversified portfolio, and fees low enough that they don't eat your returns before compounding gets a chance to work.

Here's what to look for, the apps worth your time, and exactly how to put your first $100 to work.

What Actually Matters When You're Starting With $100

Before you compare logos, compare these:

  • Minimums. Many apps now start at $0, but some still require $500–$1,000. At $100, a zero minimum is non-negotiable.
  • Fees, in three places. Trading commissions, annual advisory fees (robos typically charge roughly 0.25%–0.50% of assets), and fund expense ratios (index funds can be as low as 0.00%–0.10%).
  • Fractional shares. This is what makes $100 workable. You can own a piece of a $400 share instead of waiting until you can afford the whole thing.
  • Account types. A regular taxable brokerage account is the default. If you're in the US and have earned income, a Roth IRA is often the better home for long-term money. In the UK/EU, look at ISAs or tax-advantaged wrappers where available.
  • Automation. Recurring deposits of $25–$50 a month beat one-time deposits almost every time.
  • Regulation. US accounts should be SIPC members; UK/EU platforms should be authorized by the FCA or a national regulator. Check before you deposit.

Best Investing Apps for Beginners in 2026

1. Betterment — Best Overall Robo-Advisor for Hands-Off Beginners

Betterment builds a diversified portfolio of low-cost ETFs for you, then rebalances automatically. You answer a few questions about goals and risk, deposit money, and that's it. It's the closest thing to "set it and forget it," and it supports both taxable accounts and IRAs.

Betterment

2. Wealthfront — Best for Automated Portfolios With Extra Tools

Wealthfront is another top-tier robo-advisor with a clean interface, automatic rebalancing, and optional features like tax-loss harvesting on larger balances. It's a strong pick if you want a portfolio managed for you without paying a human advisor.

Wealthfront

3. Fidelity — Best for Low Fee Index Funds and Fractional Shares

Fidelity combines zero-commission stock and ETF trades with fractional shares and some of the cheapest index funds on the market, including funds with a 0.00% expense ratio. It also offers a robo option (Fidelity Go) and a full range of retirement accounts. A great "one account for life" choice.

Fidelity

4. M1 Finance — Best for Building Your Own Fractional Share Portfolio

M1 lets you create a custom "pie" — a set of stocks and ETFs with target percentages — and every deposit gets split across it automatically. Fractional shares mean even a $20 deposit buys every slice. Ideal if you want a simple, self-directed portfolio you rarely touch.

M1 Finance

5. Acorns — Best for Spare-Change Micro-Investing

Acorns rounds up your purchases and invests the difference into a diversified ETF portfolio. It's a gentle on-ramp if the idea of picking investments feels intimidating, and it works well alongside a more traditional account.

Acorns

6. Robinhood — Best for a Simple, Mobile-First Start

Robinhood made fractional, commission-free investing mainstream. The interface is about as frictionless as it gets, which matters when you're trying to build the habit. Just remember: easy to use doesn't mean you should trade often.

Robinhood

7. Public — Best for Multi-Asset Investing in One App

Public offers fractional shares, ETFs, and alternative assets in a single app, plus a social feed that helps beginners see how others think about positions. Good for people who want context alongside the buy button.

Public

8. Stash — Best for Learning While You Invest

Stash pairs investing with short, plain-English lessons on how markets and diversification work. If you want education baked into the product rather than bolted on, it's worth a look.

Stash

9. Trade Republic — Best EU Option for Fractional Shares and Savings Plans

Popular across Germany and much of the EU, Trade Republic offers fractional shares, commission-free savings plans (Sparpläne), and a straightforward mobile experience. A solid entry point for European beginners.

Trade Republic

10. Scalable Capital — Best EU Robo-Advisor Plus ETF Plans

Scalable combines a managed robo portfolio with self-directed ETF savings plans, so you can start hands-off and take more control later without switching platforms.

Scalable Capital

11. Vanguard — Best for Long-Term Low-Cost Index Investors

Vanguard built its reputation on low-cost index funds, and its platform is built for people who plan to hold for decades. The interface is less flashy than newer apps, but the fund lineup is hard to beat.

Vanguard

How to Invest $100 (Step by Step)

  1. Pick one app and open an account. Don't compare for three weeks. Choose from the list above and move.
  2. Decide taxable vs. retirement. US: consider a Roth IRA if this money is for retirement. UK: an ISA. EU: whatever tax-advantaged wrapper your country offers.
  3. Fund it. Link your bank and transfer $100. Set a recurring $25–$50 deposit for the same day each month.
  4. Choose one diversified fund or portfolio. A broad total-market or S&P 500 index fund, or a robo-managed portfolio, is plenty. You do not need ten holdings.
  5. Automate and ignore it. Turn on automatic investing. Check the balance monthly at most.

Fractional Shares Apps: Why They Matter at $100

Fractional shares are the single biggest unlock for small accounts. Without them, a $100 budget limits you to cheap stocks and a handful of ETFs. With them, you can own a slice of a broad index fund, a global equity ETF, or a basket of large companies — and still have cash left over.

Most apps on this list support fractional investing. If a platform doesn't, it's probably not built for beginners with small balances.

Low Fee Index Funds to Pair With Any App

You don't need to be clever here. Low fee index funds tracking broad markets — total US market, total international, or a global all-world index — give you diversification in a single ticker. Look for expense ratios under 0.10%, and prefer funds with no minimum investment or transaction fee. Pair one core fund with an automated deposit and you've built a portfolio most professionals would call sensible.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Chasing hot stocks from social media. Concentration cuts both ways.
  • Trading frequently. Every trade is a chance to be wrong twice.
  • Ignoring fees. Small percentages compound too — just not in your favor.
  • Investing money you'll need soon. Money for rent, emergencies, or next year's goals belongs in savings, not equities.
  • Stopping after one deposit. Consistency is the actual strategy.

FAQ

What's the best investing app for a complete beginner with $100?

For most people, a robo-advisor like Betterment or Wealthfront is the easiest start because the portfolio is built for you. If you'd rather pick your own funds, Fidelity or M1 Finance give you fractional shares and very low costs.

Can you really start investing with $100?

Yes. Fractional shares and zero-minimum accounts mean $100 can buy a diversified index fund or a managed portfolio. The amount matters less than starting early and adding money regularly.

Are robo-advisors worth it for small accounts?

Often, yes — the fee is small in absolute terms and you get automatic rebalancing and diversification without doing research. Once your balance grows, compare the annual fee against a low-cost index fund you'd manage yourself.

The Bottom Line

Starting with $100 in 2026 is less about finding a secret app and more about removing friction. Pick a regulated platform with no minimum, fractional shares, and low fees. Choose one diversified fund or a managed portfolio. Automate a small monthly deposit. Then leave it alone and let time do the heavy lifting.

Pick your app this week, fund it this month, and let the compounding start.
