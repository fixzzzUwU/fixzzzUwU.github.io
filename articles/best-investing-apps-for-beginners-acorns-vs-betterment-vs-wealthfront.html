Best Investing Apps for Beginners: Acorns vs Betterment vs Wealthfront

You know you should start investing. You’ve heard the phrase “time in the market beats timing the market” more times than you can count. But when you open a brokerage app and see fancy charts, margin rates, and option chains, it’s easy to feel like a tourist in a foreign country.

That’s where robo-advisors and micro-investing apps come in. They simplify investing to the point where you just connect a bank account, answer a few questions, and let the software build and manage a portfolio for you. If you’re looking for the best investing apps for beginners, three names are going to keep showing up: Acorns, Betterment, and Wealthfront.

Each one takes a slightly different approach to getting you invested with small amounts of money. In this robo advisor comparison, we’ll break down how they work, what they charge, and which one is the right fit for your first dollar.

Why These Three Apps?

Acorns is the most famous micro investing app out there. It focuses on the “set it and forget it” crowd by turning your everyday purchases into miniature investments. Betterment and Wealthfront, meanwhile, are full-scale robo-advisors. They build and automatically rebalance a diversified portfolio of low-cost ETFs based on your goals and risk tolerance.

All three are beginner-friendly, but they solve different problems. Acorns is about building the habit without thinking about it. Betterment is about clarity and goal-setting. Wealthfront is about maximum automation with a few extra advanced tricks under the hood.

Acorns at a Glance

Acorns is built around a delightfully simple concept: your spare change. You link a credit or debit card, and every time you make a purchase, Acorns rounds it up to the nearest dollar and invests the difference. Buy a latte for $4.40, and Acorns quietly invests $0.60 into a diversified portfolio of ETFs.

What Makes Acorns Different

Acorns is the perfect choice for people who have never invested a dime because it removes the biggest obstacle: getting started. You don’t need to understand asset allocation or expense ratios. The app also lets you schedule recurring deposits—say, $20 per week—and offers a feature called Found Money that earns cash back from participating retailers into your investment account.

Fees and Minimums

There’s no account minimum to open Acorns, which is a huge bonus for true beginners. Instead of a percentage fee, Acorns charges a flat monthly subscription. The basic plan starts at around $3 per month and includes an individual investment account and a retirement IRA. That flat fee can feel a little disproportionate if you only have a small balance, but it pays for the seamless automation and the strong educational content in the app.

Betterment at a Glance

Betterment is one of the original robo-advisors, and it still sets the standard for the category. When you sign up, the app asks about your financial goals, timeline, and comfort level with risk. Then it builds a portfolio of low-cost ETFs and handles the rebalancing, dividend reinvestment, and tax efficiency behind the scenes.

What Makes Betterment Different

Betterment is the best all-around option if you want a little more control over your money. You can open multiple “buckets” within one account—one for a vacation, one for a home down payment, one for retirement—and give each one its own risk level. The app also offers a compelling Cash Reserve feature with competitive interest rates, so you can keep your emergency fund sitting right next to your investments.

Another standout is the human touch. The Digital plan costs just 0.25% per year, but if you choose the Premium plan (0.40%), you get unlimited access to certified financial planners via phone. That hybrid approach is rare in the robo-advisor world.

Fees and Minimums

Betterment has no account minimum. You can transfer $10, $50, or $500 and the system will put your money to work immediately. The annual fee of 0.25% on the Digital plan means that if you have $1,000 invested, you’re paying just $2.50 per year. That’s an incredibly cheap way to get professional portfolio management.

Wealthfront at a Glance

Wealthfront is the closest thing to a “self-driving” money system. Like Betterment, it builds and rebalances a portfolio of ETFs based on your answers to a short questionnaire. But Wealthfront pushes the automation further with a suite of financial planning tools that let you model out major life events, like buying a home or planning retirement, right inside the app.

What Makes Wealthfront Different

Wealthfront’s biggest bragging rights are tax-loss harvesting and advanced rebalancing. Tax-loss harvesting is a way of using losing investments to offset tax liabilities on winners, and Wealthfront automates the entire process for you. For beginners just starting out, that feature won’t matter much, but it’s valuable as your balance grows.

Wealthfront also offers a cash account that functions like a high-yield checking account, complete with a debit card and no monthly fees. It feels like a mini-bank and investment firm glued together, all managed from a clean mobile interface.

Fees and Minimums

Wealthfront charges a simple, flat annual fee of 0.25% of assets under management. No tiered pricing, no hidden costs. It does have a minimum initial deposit, which makes it slightly less accessible than Acorns or Betterment if you literally have pennies to invest. However, if you’re starting with a typical “first paycheck” amount of $100 or $200, it’s rarely a problem.

Acorns vs Betterment vs Wealthfront: Side-by-Side Comparison

Here’s the short version:

  • Acorns is best for people who want investing to happen on autopilot. The round-up feature is a brilliant behavioral hack.
  • Betterment is best for goal-driven savers who want zero minimum, transparent fees, and optional human advice.
  • Wealthfront is best for tech-savvy beginners who want the most advanced automation and bank-like features in a single app.

All three offer retirement accounts, SIPC insurance through their brokerage partners, and automatic recurring deposits. The core difference comes down to how active you want to be. Acorns is the most passive. Betterment is the most guided. Wealthfront is the most automated at the portfolio-management level.

How to Invest $100 with These Apps

The most common search among new investors is how to invest 100 dollars. It’s a perfectly reasonable starting point, and each of these apps has a different way of handling it.

With Acorns, the easiest path is to set up a recurring weekly deposit of $25 and let round-ups fill in the rest. Over the course of a month, you’ll cross the $100 mark without feeling the pinch.
