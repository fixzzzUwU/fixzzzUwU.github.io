Best Investing Apps for Beginners: Robo-Advisors vs Micro-Investing Apps Compared

If you’re in your 20s or 30s and want to start investing, the hardest part is often choosing where to begin. The best investing app for beginners isn’t the one with the most features or the loudest marketing—it’s the one that fits how much you can invest, how hands-on you want to be, and how much you care about fees.

Most beginner-friendly platforms fall into two camps: robo-advisors and micro-investing apps. Robo-advisors build and manage a diversified portfolio for you, while micro-investing apps make it easy to start with small amounts, recurring deposits, or spare change. This comparison will help you decide which approach makes sense before you open an account.

Robo-Advisors vs Micro-Investing Apps: What’s the Difference?

Robo-advisors are automated portfolio platforms. You answer a few questions about your goals, timeline, and risk tolerance, and the platform suggests a diversified portfolio—usually built with ETFs. Many robo-advisors handle automatic deposits, rebalancing, and sometimes tax-loss harvesting. They’re a good fit if you want a hands-off approach and don’t want to pick individual investments.

Micro-investing apps are designed for small balances and habit-building. They often support fractional shares, recurring buys, and spare-change round-ups. Some also include educational content or themed portfolios. They’re useful if you want to start with $5, $20, or $100 and learn as you go, but they may charge subscription fees or encourage more frequent checking than long-term investors need.

| Feature | Robo-Advisors | Micro-Investing Apps |

|---|---|---|

| Best for | Hands-off diversified portfolios | Small deposits, spare change, learning |

| Typical minimum | Often low, but varies by platform | Often very low, but varies |

| Automation | Automatic deposits and rebalancing | Round-ups, recurring buys, fractional shares |

| Fees | Advisory and fund fees vary | Subscription and fund fees vary |

| Control | Lower—portfolio is managed for you | Varies—some offer more choice |

Neither option is automatically better. The right choice depends on whether you want a managed portfolio or a simple way to invest small amounts consistently.

How to Start Investing with $100

You don’t need a large sum to begin, but you do need a basic plan. Here’s a practical way to start investing with $100 without overcomplicating it.

  1. Cover the basics first. Before investing, make sure you have a small emergency fund and a plan for high-interest debt. Investing is for money you won’t need for a while.
  2. Choose the right account type. A taxable brokerage account is flexible. A retirement account like an IRA may offer tax advantages, but it comes with rules. Pick based on your goal and time horizon.
  3. Pick one app and keep it simple. A robo-advisor is a good default if you want diversification without research. A micro-investing app works if you want to start small and build the habit.
  4. Automate a recurring deposit. Even $25 or $50 per month can help you stay consistent. Consistency usually matters more than timing the market.
  5. Check fees and minimums. Small balances can be hurt by monthly subscription fees or high fund costs. Read the fee schedule before signing up.
  6. Diversify and ignore hype. Broad ETFs and automated portfolios are generally more suitable for beginners than betting on a single stock or trend.

Many apps let you start with $100 or less, but not all do. Always check the current account minimum and fee structure directly on the platform.

The Bottom Line

Robo-advisors and micro-investing apps solve different problems. Choose a robo-advisor if you want a diversified portfolio managed for you with minimal effort. Choose a micro-investing app if you want to start with small amounts, invest spare change, or build confidence with a simple interface.

The best investing app for beginners is the one you’ll use consistently without paying unnecessary fees. Start small, automate what you can, and review your account periodically rather than checking it every day.

FAQ
