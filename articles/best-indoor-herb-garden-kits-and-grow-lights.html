Best Indoor Herb Garden Kits and Grow Lights

Fresh herbs can transform a simple meal into something that tastes like it came from a professional kitchen. But if you live in an apartment, have a tiny balcony, or simply don't want to deal with outdoor pests and weather, growing your own herbs might feel out of reach. It's not. With the right indoor herb garden kit and a solid grow light, you can have basil, mint, and parsley growing steps away from your stove — all year long.

Whether you're a complete beginner or someone who has accidentally killed a few houseplants (we've all been there), this guide breaks down the best kits, the best grow lights for indoor herbs, and the easy herbs to grow indoors.

Why Grow Herbs Indoors?

There's nothing quite like snipping fresh basil straight into a pot of pasta sauce. Indoor herb gardening gives you that convenience every single day, regardless of the season or where you live. Apartment dwellers don't need a backyard; a sunny windowsill or a small countertop corner is enough. Plus, store-bought herbs are expensive and often go bad before you use them. Growing your own means you use exactly what you need, when you need it.

What to Look For in an Indoor Herb Garden Kit

Before you hit "add to cart," here's what matters most when choosing an indoor herb garden kit.

Light Source

If your kitchen doesn't get at least six hours of direct sunlight, you'll want a kit with built-in grow lights. Full-spectrum LED lights are the gold standard. They mimic natural sunlight and give herbs the energy they need to grow lush, not leggy.

Growing Medium

Some kits use soil; others use hydroponics (water with nutrients). Both work well. Hydroponic kits tend to be cleaner and faster-growing, while soil kits feel more traditional and are often more forgiving for beginners.

Size and Design

Think about where you'll place your kit. Countertop models are compact and stylish, while vertical or wall-mounted options free up valuable prep space. Make sure the kit fits your kitchen aesthetic — you'll be looking at it daily.

What's Included

Many kits come with seeds, nutrient solutions, and even planting pods. Others require you to buy those separately. Factor in the long-term cost of replacement pods or nutrients before committing.

Easy Herbs to Grow Indoors

Not all herbs thrive indoors, but these five are practically foolproof:

  • Basil — Loves warmth and light; grows quickly from seed.
  • Mint — Hardy and vigorous; just keep it contained so it doesn't crowd other herbs.
  • Chives — Grow like grass, tolerate lower light, and taste great on everything.
  • Parsley — Slow to start but easy to maintain once established.
  • Thyme — A low-growing, forgiving herb that tolerates dry conditions and lower humidity.

If you're new to this, start with basil and chives. They germinate fast, smell amazing, and give you quick wins that'll boost your confidence.

Creating Your Own Windowsill Herb Garden

Prefer to build your own setup instead of buying a kit? Here's what you need:

  1. A sunny south- or west-facing windowsill
  2. Containers with drainage holes (or self-watering planters)
  3. High-quality potting mix
  4. Your choice of herb seeds or young plants
  5. A grow light if your window doesn't provide enough sun

Position your herbs so they get at least 6–8 hours of light daily. Rotate the pots every few days to ensure even growth. Water when the top inch of soil feels dry, and harvest often — regular trimming encourages bushier, healthier plants.

FAQ

How much light do indoor herbs really need?

Most easy herbs to grow indoors need at least 6–8 hours of light per day. If natural sunlight isn't available, a full-spectrum grow light works just as well — just set a timer to keep the light schedule consistent.

Can I grow herbs indoors year-round?

Absolutely. That's the biggest advantage of an indoor herb garden kit. As long as your plants get enough light, consistent moisture, and occasional nutrients, they'll grow through all four seasons, no matter what's happening outside.

Are hydroponic kits better than soil for growing herbs?

Hydroponic kits tend to grow herbs faster and require less day-to-day maintenance, but they need replacement nutrients and seed pods. Soil-based systems are more traditional, more forgiving, and let you use your own potting mix, but they require more attention to watering. Both work well — it just comes down to your preference.
