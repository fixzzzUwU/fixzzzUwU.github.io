How to Start Investing with $100 (or Even Less)

Let's be clear: $100 won't turn you into a millionaire overnight, but it's absolutely enough to get started. The key is choosing an app that supports fractional shares. Fractional shares let you buy a small piece of a company or exchange-traded fund (ETF) instead of needing the full share price. For example, if a single share costs $400, you can invest a portion of that share with just $100.

Many investment apps also have no account minimum, so you can begin with a small transfer and build from there. Here's a simple game plan to start investing with $100:

  • Choose an app that matches your goals and comfort level.
  • Link your bank account and make a one-time transfer of your first $100.
  • Decide how much risk you are comfortable taking.
  • Set up a recurring deposit for as little as $5 or $10 weekly.

Consistency matters more than the amount when you're starting out. A small, regular investment habit will usually beat waiting until you have thousands saved up.

What to Look for in the Best Investment Apps for Beginners

Not every investing app is built with beginners in mind. Some assume you already know how to read market data, while others hide fees in hard-to-find places. Here's what separates the best investment apps for beginners from the rest:

  • Low or no account minimums so you can start with $100 or less.
  • Fractional shares so you can invest in pricey companies without paying full price.
  • Automation features like round-ups and recurring deposits to keep you consistent.
  • Educational content that explains investing in simple, easy-to-understand language.
  • Clear pricing with no hidden fees or surprise charges.
  • Regulation and security in the country where you live.

Acorns — One of the Best Apps to Invest Spare Change

Acorns is often the first name that comes to mind when people talk about the best apps to invest spare change. You link a debit or credit card, and Acorns automatically rounds up each purchase to the nearest dollar. That spare change is invested into a diversified portfolio of ETFs. You can also add recurring deposits to keep momentum going, and the app will happily invest any one-off lump sum like $100 or a tax refund. If you want to build an investing habit without thinking about it, Acorns makes that effortless.

Stash — A Solid Micro Investing App for Beginners

Stash takes a slightly different approach. It's a micro investing app for beginners that gives you more hands-on control. You can open an account with a small monthly subscription and build a portfolio using fractional shares of well-known stocks and ETFs. Many beginners appreciate Stash because it lets you start investing with modest amounts — including an initial $100 — and includes a library of plain-English lessons. You can also choose investments based on your personal interests, like technology or renewable energy, which keeps the process engaging.
