Before comparing brands, it helps to know what actually matters. A strong identity theft protection service should include:

Why a Credit Monitoring Service Isn’t Enough

A credit monitoring service is useful, but it is not a complete identity theft defense. Credit monitoring generally focuses on activity in your credit files. Identity theft can also involve your Social Security number, medical information, tax records, or online accounts—areas that may not show up as a credit inquiry.

That doesn’t mean credit monitoring is unnecessary. It is one of the clearest early-warning tools available. The key is to pair it with identity monitoring, account alerts, strong passwords, and a restoration plan. If you are comparing services, look for a provider that monitors both credit and identity activity rather than relying on credit alerts alone.

How to Monitor Your Credit Score and Protect It

Learning how to monitor your credit score does not need to be complicated. Start with these habits:

  1. Check your credit reports regularly. You can request free reports from AnnualCreditReport.com, the official site for the three nationwide credit bureaus.
  2. Use a credit monitoring service. Alerts can help you spot new inquiries, accounts, or suspicious changes sooner.
  3. Review bank and card statements. Small unauthorized charges are often the first sign of account takeover.
  4. Set up fraud alerts or credit freezes when needed. A freeze restricts access to your credit file and can be a strong preventive step if you suspect fraud.
  5. Keep login credentials secure. Use unique passwords and a password manager, especially for financial accounts.

A credit monitoring service will not prevent every type of identity theft, but it can shorten the time between suspicious activity and your response.
