12 Best Budgeting Apps to Finally Stick to a Budget in 2025

Let’s be honest: creating a budget is the easy part. Sticking to it is where most of us struggle. If you’ve ever spent hours building a detailed spreadsheet in January, only to abandon it by February, you’re not alone. The problem usually isn't your discipline—it’s your tools.

In 2025, the best budgeting apps have evolved far beyond simple expense trackers. They now use AI to predict your bills, sync with your bank in real-time, and even help couples manage shared finances without arguing over who paid for dinner.

Whether you are a solo saver, part of a couple, or managing a busy family, this guide breaks down the best budgeting apps 2025 has to offer. We’ll compare features, pricing, and philosophies to help you find the perfect fit for your financial goals.

The Heavyweights: YNAB vs Monarch Money

If you are looking for a premium experience, two names dominate the conversation this year: YNAB and Monarch Money. Both are subscription-based (no free tiers here), but they offer vastly different approaches to money management.

YNAB (You Need A Budget)

YNAB is the gold standard for the zero-based budgeting app method. The philosophy here is simple: give every dollar a job. You don't forecast future income; you only budget the money you actually have in your bank account right now.

Who it’s for: People who want to actively change their spending habits and break the paycheck-to-paycheck cycle.

Standout Features:

  • Loan Planner: A newer feature that helps you visualize the true cost of debt and payoff timelines.
  • Habit Building: It’s less about tracking the past and more about planning the present.
  • Excellent Education: Their support materials are arguably the best in the industry.

YNAB

Monarch Money

Monarch Money has positioned itself as the modern successor to Mint. It focuses on holistic wealth management. Instead of just looking at your checking account, Monarch pulls in investments, crypto, and property values to give you a complete picture of your net worth.

Who it’s for: Couples and individuals who want a high-level view of their finances without the strict rules of zero-based budgeting.

Standout Features:

  • Collaboration: Built with couples in mind, allowing you to assign transactions and leave notes for your partner.
  • Custom Dashboards: You can build a dashboard that shows what matters to you, whether that’s cash flow or investment performance.
  • Flexible Budgeting: You can set category limits without needing to assign every single dollar.

Best Zero-Based Budgeting Apps

If you prefer the "envelope system" method but want to digitize it, these tools are for you.

  • YNAB: As mentioned, this is the king of zero-based budgeting. It requires a learning curve, but the results speak for themselves.
  • EveryDollar: Created by Ramsey Solutions, this app is incredibly simple. The free version requires manual entry (which makes you more mindful of spending), while the Premium version syncs with your bank.
  • Goodbudget: This is a digital version of the envelope system. It’s great for people who want to track shared expenses without linking bank accounts—you simply fill your digital envelopes each month.

Best Budgeting App for Couples and Families

Managing money with a partner requires transparency and communication. The best apps in this category make it easy to see who spent what and how it fits into the family plan.

Honeydue

Honeydue is designed specifically for couples. It allows you to see a combined view of your finances while keeping individual account details private. You can set monthly limits for shared categories like groceries or date nights, and the app will notify both partners when a limit is close to being reached.

Monarch Money

Monarch is also a top contender here. Its shared dashboard allows both partners to log in, categorize transactions, and track goals together. It’s ideal for couples who have combined finances or want to track separate accounts side-by-side.

Best Monthly Budget Planner Apps for Simplicity

Sometimes you just want an app that tells you if you can afford takeout tonight without a philosophy lecture.

  • Rocket Money: Formerly Truebill, this app is less about strict budgeting and more about saving money on autopilot. It scans your bills for errors, negotiates lower rates for you, and cancels subscriptions you don't use. It’s a "set it and forget it" tool.
  • PocketGuard: This app operates on the "In My Pocket" principle. It looks at your balance, subtracts upcoming bills and goals, and tells you exactly how much safe-to-spend money you have left. It is excellent for preventing overdrafts.
  • Copilot Money: (iOS only) If you care about design, Copilot is the most beautiful app on the market. It uses AI to categorize transactions and offers a sleek, intuitive interface that makes checking your budget feel less like a chore.

The Best of the Rest: Free and Niche Options

You don't always have to pay to get a handle on your finances. Here are a few other options that round out the list of the best budgeting apps 2025 has to offer:

  • Credit Karma: Best known for credit scores, it now offers a robust net worth and spending tracker for free. It makes money through targeted credit card offers, but the tracking tools are solid.
  • Empower (formerly Personal Capital): The best free tool for tracking investments and net worth. The budgeting features are secondary, but the dashboard is unmatched for wealth tracking.
  • Fudget: A no-frills, manual-entry app. It’s perfect for freelancers or people with irregular income who just need a simple list of money in and money out.
  • Mint: While Mint officially shut down and migrated users to Credit Karma, many users are still looking for alternatives. We recommend checking out Monarch or Copilot as the spiritual successors to Mint's legacy.

How to Choose the Right App

With so many options, how do you decide? Ask yourself these three questions:

  1. Do I want to be told what to do? If you need strict rules to stay on track, choose a zero-based budgeting app like YNAB. If you just want to see where your money went, choose Monarch or Copilot.
  2. Am I managing this alone? If you have a partner, prioritize apps with sharing features like Honeydue or Monarch Money.
  3. Do I want to pay? Most premium apps cost between $50 and $100 per year. If that’s not in the budget, look at EveryDollar (manual version) or Credit Karma.

FAQ

1. What is the best budgeting app for couples in 2025?

For couples, Monarch Money is generally considered the best paid option due to its collaborative features and holistic view of net worth. If you prefer a free option, Honeydue is excellent for tracking shared expenses without merging accounts.

2. Is YNAB better than Monarch Money?

It depends on your style. YNAB is better if you want a proactive, zero-based budgeting system that forces you to plan every dollar. Monarch Money is better if you want a flexible, comprehensive view of your entire financial life (including investments) with less rigid rules.

3. Do I really need a paid budgeting app?

Not necessarily. If you only need basic expense tracking, free apps like Credit Karma or EveryDollar (manual entry) work well. However, paid apps often offer better bank syncing, customer support, and advanced features like bill negotiation or investment tracking that can save you more money in the long run.
