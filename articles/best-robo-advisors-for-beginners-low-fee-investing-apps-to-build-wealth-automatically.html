What Is a Robo-Advisor—and Why Beginners Use Them

A robo-advisor is a digital investment service that uses software to create and manage a portfolio based on your goals, timeline, and risk tolerance. Most robo-advisors ask a few questions, recommend a mix of stocks and bonds, and automatically handle the boring parts: deposits, diversification, and rebalancing.

Beginners like them because they remove common hurdles. You don’t need to research individual securities, and many automated investing apps support small recurring deposits. That makes them useful if you’re learning how to start investing with $100 or want to build a habit before increasing contributions.

How We Compared the Best Robo-Advisors for Beginners

We looked at the factors that matter most for new investors:

  • Fees: advisory fees, fund expense ratios, and account minimums.
  • Automation: recurring deposits, automatic rebalancing, and dividend reinvestment.
  • Ease of use: clear onboarding, simple dashboards, and goal-based planning.
  • Account options: taxable accounts, retirement accounts, and tax-advantaged accounts where relevant.
  • Availability: whether the service is open to US, UK, or EU investors.
  • Support and education: helpful guidance without overwhelming jargon.

Betterment vs Wealthfront: Which Is Better for Beginners?

Both Betterment and Wealthfront are low-fee automated investing apps, but they fit slightly different beginners.

  • Choose Betterment if you like goal-based investing, simple recurring deposits, and a clear “set it and forget it” experience.
  • Choose Wealthfront if you want a hands-off portfolio and are specifically looking for tax-aware automation features.
  • Consider your location first: both are primarily US-focused. If you’re in the EU or UK, compare local robo-advisors instead of assuming US apps are available.

In short: Betterment feels more goal-oriented for beginners, while Wealthfront appeals to passive investors who want automation with tax features. Neither is automatically “better”—the right choice depends on your country, account type, and how much control you want.

How to Start Investing with $100

You don’t need a large lump sum to begin. Here’s a simple process:

  1. Build a small cash buffer first. Investing is for money you won’t need immediately.
  2. Choose the right account. In the US, consider a taxable brokerage account or a retirement account if eligible. In the UK/EU, look at tax-advantaged options like an ISA where available.
  3. Pick one low-fee investing app. Compare advisory fees, fund costs, minimums, and account availability.
  4. Set your risk level honestly. A longer timeline can usually handle more stock exposure; a short-term goal needs more stability.
  5. Automate a small deposit. Start with $25, $50, or $100 per month and increase when your budget allows.
  6. Leave it alone. Automated investing works best when you contribute consistently and avoid reacting to headlines.

Low-Fee Investing Apps: What to Watch

“Low fee” doesn’t mean “no fee.” Check the annual advisory fee, underlying fund expense ratios, account or platform fees, and any charges for transfers or withdrawals. Also confirm whether the app offers the account type you need and whether it is regulated in your country. A slightly higher fee can be acceptable for better usability, but beginners should avoid stacking multiple unnecessary costs.

FAQ

Can I start investing with $100?

Yes. Many robo-advisors and automated investing apps support small initial deposits or recurring contributions. Starting with $100 is less about the amount and more about building a consistent habit while keeping fees low.

Are robo-advisors safe?

Robo-advisors are typically operated by regulated financial firms, but investing always involves risk. Your portfolio can lose value, and investment accounts are not the same as insured bank deposits. Check registration, fees, and account protection details before signing up.

Betterment vs Wealthfront for beginners—which should I choose?

Choose Betterment if you want a simple, goal-based investing experience. Choose Wealthfront if you prefer a hands-off portfolio with tax-aware features. Both are low-fee options for US investors; outside the US, compare locally available robo-advisors first.
