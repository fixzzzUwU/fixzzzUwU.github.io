Best Investment Apps for Small Amounts of Money

You don't need a trust fund to start investing. And you definitely don't need to wait until you "have more money." With today's micro-investing apps, you can build a real portfolio with pocket change — literally. If you've been putting off investing because you think $50 or $100 isn't enough, this article is for you.

Why Micro-Investing Apps Changed the Game for Beginners

Not long ago, opening an investment account meant high minimum balances, trading fees, and a steep learning curve. Micro-investing apps flipped that model. Today, you can:

  • Start with just a few dollars
  • Invest spare change from everyday purchases
  • Use a simple, beginner-friendly interface
  • Get access to professionally managed portfolios (robo-advisors)
  • Learn without the fear of making expensive mistakes

The result? A whole generation of new investors who are growing wealth in small, consistent steps.

How to Start Investing with $100 (or Less)

If you're wondering how to start investing with $100, here's the honest answer: pick an app, link your bank account, and set up automatic deposits. Even $10 a week adds up over time thanks to compound growth.

Here's a simple four-step approach:

  1. Choose a micro-investing app that matches your style (more on that below).
  2. Link your bank account securely through the app's encrypted connection.
  3. Set up recurring transfers — weekly or monthly both work well.
  4. Let the app do the heavy lifting and check in occasionally to adjust your risk level.

The key is consistency, not the amount. Investing $25 per month is far better than waiting until you have $5,000 sitting in a savings account.

What to Look For in a Micro-Investing App

Not all investing apps are created equal, even if they look similar on the surface. Before you download anything, consider these four factors:

  • Fees: Most micro-investing apps charge a small monthly fee (usually between $1 and $5). Make sure you're comfortable with it.
  • Minimum investment: Some apps require no minimum at all. Others ask for $5 or $10 to get started.
  • Automation features: Round-ups, auto-deposits, and rebalancing are worth their weight in gold for beginners.
  • Educational content: Apps that explain what you're buying help you grow as an investor, not just as a saver.

Keep those criteria in mind as you read through the comparison below.

Acorns vs Stash: A Head-to-Head Comparison

When people search for the best investment apps for beginners, Acorns and Stash always come up. They're often lumped together, but they serve slightly different purposes. Let's break it down.

Acorns: Set It and Forget It

Acorns is built around the "round-up" concept. Every time you buy something with a linked card, Acorns rounds up the transaction to the nearest dollar and invests the difference. Buy a $4.50 coffee? That 50 cents goes straight into your portfolio.

Acorns is ideal if you want a fully hands-off experience. The app builds a diversified portfolio of ETFs based on your risk tolerance and goals, and you can adjust it anytime. It's the most popular choice for people who want to invest small amounts of money without thinking too much about it.

The main downside? You don't get to pick individual stocks with Acorns. It's all professionally managed portfolios, which is honestly the smarter move for most beginners, but some people miss the control.

Stash: Learn While You Invest

Stash takes a different approach. Instead of fully automated investing, Stash encourages you to learn through bite-size explanations and articles. You can invest in fractional shares of individual stocks and ETFs, which means you can own a tiny piece of companies like Amazon or Apple for just a few dollars.

Stash also offers banking and debit card features, complete with a round-up feature very similar to Acorns. The educational angle is what really sets it apart — you'll always know what you're buying and why.

The downside is that Stash's interface feels a bit busier, and the constant nudge to explore new content can be overwhelming if you just want a simple dashboard.

Which One Should You Choose?

Here's a quick summary:

  • Choose Acorns if you want a fully automatic investing experience and don't want to make any decisions.
  • Choose Stash if you want to learn about investing, hold fractional shares of individual companies, and feel more in control.

Both are excellent examples of what most micro investing app reviews would call "best for beginners." But there are other solid options worth knowing about too.

Other Micro-Investing Apps Worth Considering

While Acorns and Stash are the giants, they're not the only players in the space. Depending on your priorities, one of these might be a better fit:

  • Robinhood: Zero-commission trading with no minimum deposit. It's more advanced and not a robo-advisor — you pick your own investments.
  • Betterment: A classic robo-advisor with no minimum balance and personalized portfolios. Great if you want hands-off management without a gamified app.
  • SoFi Invest: An all-in-one finance platform. You can invest, save, bank, and even borrow money in one convenient place.

That said, for most people specifically looking for micro-investing apps with small initial deposits, Acorns and Stash remain the strongest choices.

FAQ

Can I start investing with less than $100?

Yes, absolutely. Acorns has no minimum balance for its Standard account, and Stash lets you start with as little as $5. SoFi Invest also allows you to begin investing with whatever amount you have available. The barriers to entry are extremely low across most micro-investing apps.

Is Acorns better than Stash?

It depends on your preferences. Acorns is better for fully hands-off, automatic investing. Stash is better for beginners who want to learn the ropes, pick individual fractional stocks, and feel more involved. Both are reliable platforms that let you invest small amounts of money safely and effectively.

Are micro-investing apps safe?

Yes. Reputable platforms like Acorns and Stash are regulated financial companies that use bank-level encryption to protect your data. Your investments are also covered by SIPC insurance, which protects your securities up to $500,000. As with any investment, the value can go up and down over the short term, but the underlying platforms themselves are safe to use.
