Best Budgeting Apps Compared: YNAB vs Rocket Money vs Monarch

Most people don't fail at budgeting because they lack discipline. They fail because their system is annoying enough that they stop opening it. That's why choosing the right app matters more than choosing the "perfect" budget method.

YNAB, Rocket Money, and Monarch Money are three of the most popular paid options in the US and EU right now, but they solve completely different problems. One teaches you to budget. One hunts down wasted subscriptions. One gives couples and families a shared financial dashboard.

This comparison breaks down what each does well, where each falls short, and which one fits your situation.

How to Budget With an App: What Actually Matters

Before comparing features, it helps to know what separates a budgeting app you'll keep using from one you'll abandon in three weeks.

  • Bank syncing reliability. If transactions don't import cleanly, you'll be reconciling by hand forever.
  • How it handles irregular income. Freelancers, commission earners, and bonus-heavy households need flexibility.
  • Shared access. Couples and families need real multi-user support, not a shared password.
  • The learning curve. Some apps teach a method. Others just display data and leave the thinking to you.
  • Price versus payoff. A subscription is only worth it if the app either saves you money or saves you time.

Keep those five criteria in mind as you read the breakdowns below.

YNAB Review: Best for Hands-On Budgeters

YNAB (You Need A Budget) is built around zero-based, envelope-style budgeting. Every dollar you have gets assigned a job before you spend it. If you overspend in one category, you have to move money from somewhere else — the app makes you confront that trade-off directly.

Where YNAB shines:

  • It's genuinely educational. The method, the workshops, and the community teach you how money moves through your month.
  • Goal tracking is excellent for sinking funds — car repairs, holidays, annual insurance premiums.
  • It handles irregular income better than almost any competitor, because you budget only money you already have.
  • Loan and debt payoff planning is built in.

Where YNAB falls short:

  • There's no free tier, and the learning curve is real. Expect a couple of weeks before it clicks.
  • It's not a "set it and forget it" tool. If you won't engage with it a few times a week, you're paying for something you won't use.
  • Investment and net worth tracking are minimal compared to Monarch.

Best for: people who want to change their spending habits, not just observe them.

Rocket Money Review: Best for Cutting Wasteful Spending

Rocket Money takes the opposite approach. Instead of asking you to build a budget from scratch, it analyzes what you're already spending and finds money to claw back.

Its headline features are subscription detection and cancellation, plus bill negotiation on services like internet, phone, and insurance. You connect your accounts, it flags recurring charges you may have forgotten about, and it can contact providers on your behalf. Negotiation typically works on a success-fee basis, so you only pay if it saves you money.

Where Rocket Money shines:

  • Fastest path to a tangible win. Cancelling two forgotten subscriptions often covers the subscription cost immediately.
  • The free tier is genuinely usable for basic expense tracking and spending categories.
  • Bill negotiation is a service most budgeting apps simply don't offer.
  • Clean, simple interface that non-budgeters won't find intimidating.

Where Rocket Money falls short:

  • It's a spending tracker more than a budgeting philosophy. You won't get envelope-style discipline here.
  • Premium pricing has crept up, and some users find the upsells persistent.
  • It's primarily US-focused, which limits its usefulness for EU readers.

Best for: anyone who suspects they're leaking money on subscriptions and wants an app to do the digging.

Monarch Money: Best for Couples and Families

Monarch Money positions itself as a complete financial picture rather than a budgeting drill sergeant. It aggregates bank accounts, credit cards, loans, and investments into one dashboard, then layers budgeting, goal tracking, and net worth monitoring on top.

The standout feature is collaboration. Partners get their own logins, and you can assign transactions, set shared goals, and see who spent what without sharing a password or arguing over a spreadsheet.

Where Monarch shines:

  • Best-in-class household sharing for couples and families.
  • Net worth and investment tracking in the same place as your budget.
  • Custom categories, rules, and tags make it flexible without being overwhelming.
  • Clean reporting that's actually readable — useful for monthly money check-ins.

Where Monarch falls short:

  • No free tier. It's a paid subscription with a trial, so you need to be committed.
  • It shows you your spending more than it teaches you to change it. The discipline is on you.
  • Bank sync quality varies by institution, especially with smaller EU banks.

Best for: couples, families, and anyone who wants one dashboard for everything.

YNAB vs Mint: Why the Comparison Still Matters

For years, "best budgeting app" searches meant YNAB vs Mint. Mint's shutdown in 2024 left a lot of people looking for a replacement, and the answer depends on what you used Mint for.

If you mainly used Mint to see all your accounts in one place, Monarch is the closest replacement — it does account aggregation, net worth, and budgeting in a similar spirit but with better collaboration. If you used Mint casually and only wanted to know where your money went, Rocket Money's free tier covers much of that ground. And if you always wanted Mint to actually help you budget rather than just report, YNAB is the upgrade.

How to Choose an Expense Tracking App

A simple decision path:

  1. Do you want to be told what to do with your money? Go with YNAB.
  2. Do you just want to stop wasting money? Go with Rocket Money.
  3. Do you need your partner on the same page? Go with Monarch.

Most of these apps offer free trials, so the honest answer is to test one for a full month. A budgeting app only works if it survives past the novelty phase — and that's a personal fit question, not a feature-list question.

FAQ

Is YNAB worth it for a complete beginner?

Yes, if you're willing to learn. YNAB has the steepest learning curve of the three, but it also teaches budgeting rather than assuming you already know how. If you want something you can understand in ten minutes, Rocket Money is a gentler starting point.

Can Rocket Money replace a full budgeting app?

For many people, yes. It tracks spending, categorizes transactions, and finds recurring charges. What it won't do is enforce a budgeting method or give you a shared household dashboard. If you want structure, pair it with a method or choose YNAB or Monarch instead.

Which budgeting app is best for couples?

Monarch Money. It's the only one of the three built with proper multi-user collaboration, separate logins, and shared goals. YNAB and Rocket Money can work for couples, but sharing is more of a workaround than a core feature.

The Bottom Line

There's no single best budgeting app — only the one that matches how you want to manage money. YNAB changes behavior, Rocket Money recovers wasted spending, and Monarch Money connects households. Pick the problem you actually have, use the trial, and give it a full month before deciding.
