Best Fitness Trackers for Running and Heart Rate Monitoring in 2025

Running is one of those sports where a little data goes a long way. Whether you're chasing a new 5K personal best or just trying to keep your easy days easy, the right fitness tracker helps you train smarter, recover better, and understand your body — all without guesswork. The challenge? There are dozens of options, and the differences matter more than you'd think. Let's break down the best fitness trackers for running and heart rate monitoring in 2025 across a few key categories, so you can find the one that actually fits your wrist (and your routine).

What to Look For in a Fitness Tracker for Running

Before we get to the best picks, here's the short version of what matters most when you're shopping for a running-focused wearable.

Built-in GPS beats phone-connected GPS every time. If you're serious about running, you don't want to carry your phone to log your route. A fitness watch with GPS built in gives you accurate pace, distance, and route data straight from your wrist.

Optical heart rate has come a long way. Modern sensors are solid for steady-state runs, intervals, and daily resting heart rate. They aren't as accurate as a chest strap for every single beat, but for most runners, the wrist-based data is more than enough.

Battery life and comfort are the real dealbreakers. The most feature-packed watch in the world is useless if you forget to charge it or can't sleep in it. Think about what you'll actually want to wear 24/7, not just on race day.

With that in mind, here's how the top contenders compare in 2025.

Garmin Forerunner 265: The Runner's Standard

If your priority is training data, the Forerunner 265 remains the benchmark. It's a dedicated GPS running watch first, and everything else second. That means you get dual-band GPS that holds onto a signal even in city canyons and tree-covered trails, plus features like race-day pacing, wrist-based running dynamics, and personalized daily suggested workouts that adapt to your recovery.

The heart rate sensor is also a step above older models, and the watch does a genuinely great job tracking the "training load" your body is accumulating — that's the kind of insight that prevents you from pushing hard when your cardiovascular system is already spent.

Best Smartwatch for Workouts: Apple Watch Series 10

The Apple Watch Series 10 isn't a pure running watch, but it's hands-down the best smartwatch for workouts if you want one device that does everything. The new display is larger and brighter, which helps when you're glancing at your pace mid-sprint. The GPS is accurate, the optical heart rate sensor is reliable, and the integration with apps like Strava, TrainingPeaks, and Apple Fitness+ makes it a genuine training companion.

What still holds it back for some runners is battery life. You'll charge it daily, and that's not ideal if you want multi-day heart rate tracking or overnight recovery data. But if you're already living in the Apple ecosystem, it's a seamless fit.

Garmin Venu 3: The Health-Conscious All-Rounder

Between the Forerunner and the Apple Watch sits the Garmin Venu 3. It gives you the full Garmin running suite — accurate GPS, advanced heart rate monitoring, and structured workouts — but wraps it in a more lifestyle-friendly design with a speaker, microphone, and excellent sleep coaching.

For runners who also care about daily health, the Venu 3 is arguably the best fitness watch for heart rate in Garmin's lineup, because it balances workout accuracy with continuous wellness metrics like HRV status, stress tracking, and naps. The battery lasts about four days on a single charge, which is a comfortable middle ground.

Why a Fitness Watch with GPS Is Worth It

If you've ever gone for a run with your phone strapped to your arm, you already know the annoyance. A fitness watch with GPS adds convenience and safety. You get real-time pace adjustments without digging your phone out of your pocket, and you can leave it at home on shorter runs. Plus, mapping features — helpfully built into Garmin and Coros watches — save you when you're exploring unfamiliar routes.

The mid-2025 generation of GPS chips is impressively accurate, too. On open roads, most of these watches are within a few meters per mile. That's more than enough for training zones, intervals, and race pacing.

Best Fitness Watch for Heart Rate: Optical Sensors, Tested

Wrist-based optical heart rate monitors have matured. The sensors on the Forerunner 265, Venu 3, Coros Pace 3, and Apple Watch Series 10 all do a commendable job during steady-state runs and intervals. For daily resting heart rate and sleep-stage tracking, they're genuinely excellent.

That said, if your training is extremely heart-rate-driven — think long threshold intervals with specific target zones — a chest strap pairs with any of these watches for more precise data. Wrist-based monitors still lag slightly on rapid heart rate changes, but for 95% of runners, the in-watch optical sensor is all you need.

Best Fitness Tracker for Women: Lightweight Options That Work

One size definitely doesn't fit all when it comes to wrist comfort. The best fitness tracker for women is the one that doesn't feel like a brick on your wrist — and there are two standout options.

Fitbit Charge 6 is the pick for a slim, unobtrusive form factor. It's a band, not a bulky watch, and it looks great at the office or the gym. You still get reliable heart rate monitoring, connected GPS (i.e., it borrows GPS from your phone), and excellent sleep tracking. The battery easily lasts a full week.

For runners who want a smaller lightweight watch with true built-in GPS, Garmin Forerunner 265S is the smaller case variant of the Forerunner 265, and it's a favorite among runners who find standard 46mm watches too heavy. It keeps all the running features in a lighter, lower-profile design.

How the Coros Pace 3 Fits In

Don't sleep on the Coros Pace 3. It's lighter than most rivals, has a battery that lasts weeks in regular use, and includes sports like trail running, cycling, and swimming, all with dedicated profiles. For the price, it's arguably the best budget-friendly option without dropping the features runners actually need. The heart rate accuracy is solid, and the GPS capture is on par with Garmin's mid-tier watches.

Final Thoughts

There isn't one perfect tracker for everyone, and that's okay. The Garmin Forerunner 265 is the best fitness tracker for running if you want standalone GPS and everything a serious runner needs. The Apple Watch Series 10 wins for those who want the best smartwatch for workouts with a great app ecosystem. The Fitbit Charge 6 is a comfortable, stylish option for daily wear, and the Coros Pace 3 delivers unbeatable value. Start with your priorities — battery life, GPS, heart rate accuracy, or design — and the right choice becomes clear.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are wrist-based heart rate monitors accurate enough for running?

Yes, for most runners, modern optical sensors are accurate enough for steady-state runs, daily resting heart rate, and recovery tracking. If you train strictly by heart rate zones at high intensities, a chest strap is still slightly more accurate.

Do I need a fitness watch with built-in GPS?

Not necessarily. If you always run with your phone, connected GPS works fine. For dedicated runners who want accurate pace and route tracking without carrying a phone, built-in GPS is the better choice.

How long do fitness tracker batteries last in 2025?

It varies by model. The Garmin Forerunner 265 lasts about a week with training, the Coros Pace 3 can last two weeks or more, and the Apple Watch Series 10 charges daily. Consider your training volume and how often you want to charge before you choose.
