10 Best Budgeting Apps to Take Control of Your Finances in 2025

Let’s be honest: keeping track of where your money goes every month is harder than it looks. Between rent, subscriptions, groceries, and the occasional coffee run, expenses have a way of slipping through the cracks. The good news? You don’t need a spreadsheet or a finance degree to get organized. A good budgeting app can do the heavy lifting for you.

We’ve rounded up the 10 best budgeting apps for 2025, with a special focus on options for beginners, free budgeting tools, and apps that actually help you change your spending habits. Whether you’re paying off debt or just trying to save a little more, there’s a tool here for you.

Why Budgeting Apps Work

Most people don’t fail at budgeting because they’re bad with money. They fail because they choose the wrong system. Budgeting apps help by automating the boring parts—tracking expenses, categorizing spending, and showing you exactly what’s left. When you can see your financial picture in real time, it’s much easier to make smarter decisions.

And you don’t need to be “good at math” or understand complicated finance terms. Modern apps are designed for everyday people, with clean interfaces and simple features. That’s why the best budgeting apps are the ones you actually open on a regular basis.

The 10 Best Budgeting Apps for 2025

1. YNAB (You Need A Budget)

If you’ve ever searched for a “YNAB review,” you already know this app has a cult following. And for good reason. YNAB is built on a simple principle: give every dollar a job. It’s a zero-based budgeting app that forces you to plan for every expense, from your rent to your morning latte.

YNAB is especially great if you want to stop living paycheck to paycheck. It’s not just about tracking expenses—it’s about changing the way you think about money. The app syncs with your bank accounts, imports transactions automatically, and sends you alerts when you’re close to your limits.

It’s a subscription-based service, but there’s a free trial. Many users find that the money they save in the first month more than covers the annual fee. If you’re serious about taking control of your finances, this is the app for you.

2. EveryDollar

EveryDollar is Dave Ramsey’s budgeting app, and it’s one of the best budgeting apps for beginners. The interface is clean and intuitive, and the whole system is based on the zero-based budget idea. You list your income, plan your expenses, and make sure every dollar is accounted for.

The free version lets you create a budget manually, which is actually a great way to stay connected to your spending. If you want automatic bank syncing, you’ll need the paid version. But for those just starting out, the free tier is more than enough.

EveryDollar is also great if you prefer a no-nonsense approach. There are no confusing charts or investment dashboards—just a simple, effective budgeting tool.

3. PocketGuard

PocketGuard is perfect for people who just want to know one thing: “How much money can I spend today?” The app links to your accounts, calculates your bills, and tracks your spending. Then it shows you the money left over after bills, savings, and essentials.

What makes PocketGuard stand out is its “In My Pocket” feature. It’s a simple, stress-free way to avoid overspending. The free version includes most of the core features, making it one of the best free budgeting tools out there. If you’ve tried other apps and found them too complicated, this one is worth a shot.

4. Goodbudget

Goodbudget is a digital envelope system. You set up categories—like groceries, dining out, and entertainment—and allocate a set amount of money to each envelope. Once the money is gone, it’s gone.

This approach works especially well for people who prefer a more hands-on method. Goodbudget syncs across your devices, so you and your partner can track shared expenses in real time. The free version gives you a limited number of envelopes, but it’s enough to get started. If you like the idea of using cash envelopes but want something digital, Goodbudget is a solid choice.

5. Monarch Money

Monarch Money has quickly become one of the most popular budgeting apps for a reason. It takes a comprehensive approach to personal finance, allowing you to track your budget, monitor your net worth, and manage your investments—all in one place.

The app connects to all your financial accounts, so you can see your entire financial picture without logging into multiple websites. It also has excellent tools for setting savings goals and tracking your progress. While it’s a subscription app, many people find the price reasonable for the level of detail you get.

If you’re looking for a budgeting app that grows with you as your finances become more complex, Monarch is a great option.

6. Empower (formerly Personal Capital)

Empower is technically a financial dashboard, not just a budgeting app. It’s best known for its powerful investment tracking and net worth calculator. But it also has a solid budgeting feature that automatically categorizes your spending.

The best part? The budgeting and net worth tools are completely free. You only pay if you decide to use their advisory services for investment management. For young professionals who want to keep an eye on both their budget and their long-term financial health, Empower is an excellent choice.

7. Simplifi by Quicken

Simplifi is Quicken’s modern answer to personal finance. It’s designed for people who want to see all their accounts in one place without the complexity of traditional Quicken software. The app gives you a clear, real-time view of your cash flow, upcoming bills, and spending trends.

One of the standout features is the “Watchlist” for subscriptions. It helps you spot recurring charges you might have forgotten about. Simplifi is a paid app, but it’s one of the more affordable options. It’s a great middle ground for people who want more than a basic tracker but don’t need a full investment platform.

8. Honeydue

Honeydue is built specifically for couples. If you’re looking to manage your finances together without merging everything, this app is for you. You can link your individual accounts, choose what to share with your partner, and set up joint budgets for shared expenses.

The app also allows you to chat with your partner and leave comments on transactions. It’s a great way to stay on the same page without having to have awkward conversations about spending. Honeydue is free to use, with optional premium features.

9. Cleo

Cleo is a budgeting app with a personality. It’s an AI-powered assistant that connects to your bank account and chats with you through a friendly, sometimes sarcastic, interface. You can ask Cleo questions like “How much can I spend this week?” and get a quick answer.

Cleo is great for people who find traditional budgeting apps boring. It gives you insights into your spending habits and even helps you build an emergency fund. The free version has plenty of features, but you can upgrade to Cleo Plus for more advanced tools like credit monitoring and cash advances.

10. Fudget

Fudget is the simplest app on this list. There are no bank connections, no complicated charts, and no syncing issues. You just enter your income and expenses as a list, and the app does the math for you.

This makes Fudget a great choice for people who want a completely private and straightforward way to budget. It’s perfect for those who don’t want to link their bank accounts to an app. Fudget is free to download, with a one-time purchase option for the premium version.

How to Choose the Right Budgeting App for You

With so many options, it can be tough to decide. Here’s a quick guide:

  • Start with your goal. Are you trying to pay off debt, save more, or just stop overspending? YNAB and EveryDollar are great for debt payoff. PocketGuard and Cleo are better for spending awareness.
  • Consider your style. Do you want to manually enter every transaction? Go with EveryDollar or Fudget. Prefer automatic syncing? YNAB, Monarch, or Simplifi are better.
  • Check the price. Many great free budgeting tools are available, but sometimes a paid app is worth it if it helps you reach your goals faster.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best budgeting app for beginners?

EveryDollar is one of the best budgeting apps for beginners because it’s simple, guided, and based on a clear zero-based budgeting method. The free version is a great starting point, and you don’t need any prior experience to get the hang of it.

Are budgeting apps safe?

Yes, most reputable budgeting apps use bank-level encryption and two-factor authentication to keep your data secure. However, you should always read the privacy policy and check whether the app connects to your bank directly or uses a third-party service like Plaid. Sticking with well-known apps like YNAB, Monarch, or PocketGuard is a safe bet.

How do I start budgeting money with an app?

Start by linking your bank accounts and credit cards to the app. Then, list your monthly income and fixed expenses like rent, utilities, and loan payments. Next, assign a category to your variable spending like groceries, dining, and entertainment. Finally, set a spending limit for each category and track your progress throughout the month. The app will do the rest.
