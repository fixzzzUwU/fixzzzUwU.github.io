Best Robo-Advisors for Beginners: Betterment vs Wealthfront vs M1 Finance

If you want to start investing but don’t want to research individual stocks, a robo-advisor can be a practical first step. These platforms use automation to build and manage a diversified portfolio based on your goals, timeline, and comfort with risk. For many beginners, that means less guesswork, fewer decisions, and a lower chance of letting emotions drive investment choices.

This comparison looks at three popular options: Betterment, Wealthfront, and M1 Finance. Each works well for different types of investors, so the best robo advisor for beginners depends on whether you want maximum hands-off simplicity, automated tax features, or more control over a low-cost portfolio.

What a robo-advisor does for a beginner

A robo-advisor is an automated investing app that helps you invest without picking individual stocks. You answer a few questions about your goals and risk tolerance, then the platform suggests a portfolio—usually made up of low-cost index funds or ETFs. From there, it can automatically reinvest dividends, rebalance your portfolio, and help you stay on track.

For beginners, the biggest benefits are:

  • Automation: Set up recurring deposits and let the platform invest for you.
  • Diversification: Your money is spread across many holdings instead of one or two stocks.
  • Low fees: Many robo-advisors use low-cost funds and charge a small annual advisory fee.
  • Less decision fatigue: You don’t need to watch the market or choose individual securities.

A robo-advisor won’t eliminate investment risk, and it isn’t a replacement for emergency savings or debt payoff. But it can be a sensible way to begin investing consistently.

Betterment: best overall robo-advisor for hands-off beginners

Betterment is one of the most beginner-friendly robo-advisors because it focuses on goals. You can create separate goals for things like retirement, a home down payment, or a general investing account. The platform then recommends a portfolio and automates deposits, rebalancing, and dividend reinvestment.

For someone who wants a simple automated investing app, Betterment is easy to understand. You don’t have to build a portfolio from scratch, and the interface is designed around progress toward goals rather than trading. Betterment also offers tax-loss harvesting and other planning features, depending on the account type and service level.

Betterment is a strong fit if you:

  • Want a clear, goal-based investing experience.
  • Prefer to be hands-off and let automation handle the details.

Wealthfront: best for automated tax optimization and long-term passive investors

Wealthfront is another well-known robo-advisor for beginners who want a passive, automated portfolio. It builds diversified ETF portfolios, automatically rebalances them, and offers tax-loss harvesting as part of its automated investing approach. That can be useful for long-term investors who want the platform to handle some tax-related maintenance.

Wealthfront may appeal to young professionals who want a clean, automated experience and don’t want to manage a portfolio themselves. It also offers planning tools and a cash account, which can make it a convenient hub for long-term saving and investing. One thing to check: Wealthfront may require a higher opening balance than some competitors, so compare current minimums before you commit.

Wealthfront is a strong fit if you:

  • Want a passive, automated portfolio with tax optimization features.
  • Prefer a simple long-term investing experience.
  • Are comfortable checking minimum balance requirements.

M1 Finance: best low-fee index fund app for customization

M1 Finance takes a different approach. Instead of assigning you a standard managed portfolio, M1 lets you build a custom “pie” of stocks or ETFs—or choose from prebuilt portfolios. You can set automatic deposits and let M1 invest according to your target allocations.

For beginners who want a low fee index fund app, M1 can be attractive because it generally does not charge a management fee for self-directed portfolios. That makes it a good option if you’re comfortable choosing a simple ETF mix, such as a broad market fund plus a bond fund, and want automation without paying for active management. M1 also supports fractional shares, which can make it easier to invest smaller amounts.

The trade-off is that M1 gives you more control—and more responsibility. If you don’t want to think about portfolio construction at all, Betterment or Wealthfront may feel simpler.

M1 Finance is a strong fit if you:

  • Want low costs and are willing to choose or adjust your own portfolio.
  • Like the idea of automated deposits into a custom ETF mix.
  • Want more control than a traditional robo-advisor offers.

How to start investing with $100

You don’t need a large sum to begin. Here’s a practical way to start investing with $100:

  1. Choose a platform with a low minimum. Some robo-advisors and brokerage accounts let you start with small amounts, while others require more. Check current minimums before signing up.
  2. Pick the right account type. A taxable brokerage account is flexible, while an IRA is designed for retirement. Choose based on your goal.
  3. Use a diversified portfolio. A low-cost index fund or a robo-advisor portfolio can give you broad exposure without stock picking.
  4. Automate small deposits. Even $25 or $50 per month can help you build the habit. Consistency matters more than starting with a large balance.
  5. Keep fees low. Compare advisory fees, fund expense ratios, and any premium add-ons. A low fee index fund app can help your returns compound over time.

If you’re deciding between Betterment vs Wealthfront for a first account, Betterment tends to feel more goal-oriented, while Wealthfront leans into automated tax optimization. M1 Finance is the better choice if you want to build a low-cost ETF portfolio yourself and automate it.
