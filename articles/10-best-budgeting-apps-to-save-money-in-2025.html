10 Best Budgeting Apps to Save Money in 2025

Let's be real: checking your bank account balance and hoping for the best isn't a strategy. You already know you should be tracking your spending, but between busy mornings, endless subscriptions, and that sneaky "just this once" coffee run, keeping a budget by hand is exhausting. And if you were a Mint user, you already know the pain of that app shutting down. The good news? A new wave of budgeting tools is better, smarter, and easier to stick with than ever before. These aren't just spreadsheets with a mobile login—they're actual money management assistants. If you're a Millennial or Gen Z adult ready to take control of your cash and build a real savings cushion, this list is your cheat sheet. Here are the best budgeting apps in 2025, whether you're totally new to this or you're ready to level up your financial game.

How We Picked the Best Budgeting Apps

We know "best" is subjective. What works for a freelancer with irregular income may not work for a couple splitting rent. So, we compiled this list based on a few simple criteria:

  • Ease of Use: Does it look like a spreadsheet or a modern app? Is setup quick?
  • Bank Syncing: Does it automatically pull in transactions, or do you have to enter everything manually?
  • Cost: Is there a solid free version? Are the paid plans worth the price?
  • Goal Tracking: Does it help you save money app-style, like automatically transferring funds or offering visual progress bars?
  • Beginner Friendly: Can someone who hates numbers figure it out in five minutes?

The 10 Best Budgeting Apps to Save Money in 2025

Here's our full breakdown of the top apps that are actually worth your time this year.

1. YNAB (You Need A Budget)

YNAB is arguably the most famous name in the budgeting world, and for good reason. Its entire philosophy centers on giving every dollar a job. It’s called zero-based budgeting, and it forces you to account for every penny you earn, making it incredibly difficult to "lose" money to mindless spending.

  • Why it works: The app is proactive, not reactive. It asks you to plan for next month, not just track what you spent last month. It also has some of the best educational content and live workshops in the industry.
  • The catch: There is no free version after the 34-day trial. It's about $15 per month (billed annually), which makes it an investment. But for many, the savings realized in the first few months far outweigh the cost.

2. EveryDollar

Created by Dave Ramsey, EveryDollar is perfect for those following the "Baby Steps" method or anyone who loves a straightforward, structured plan. The user experience is pretty and highly visual, making it one of the best budgeting apps for beginners.

  • Why it works: It uses a simple, manual approach. You build your budget line by line. While the free version requires you to enter transactions manually, that manual effort often leads to a deeper awareness of your spending habits.
  • The catch: The free version is great, but if you want automatic bank syncing, you'll need the paid "Plus" version (about $79.99/year). You can't import prior months or do investment tracking easily, but for simple, sound budgeting, it’s stellar.

3. Empower (formerly Personal Capital)

If your financial focus is on building wealth rather than just tracking groceries, Empower is the budgeting tool for you. It is a hybrid between a budgeting app and an investment tracker. It gives you a high-level view of your net worth, cash flow, and retirement plan projections.

  • Why it works: As part of a list of the best budgeting apps, this one stands out because it isn't just about restricting your spending—it’s about seeing the big picture. The dashboard is incredibly intuitive, and it shows you how much you’re spending on fees in your 401(k), which is a hidden money killer.
  • The catch: The budgeting feature is a bit less structured than YNAB. It’s more about monitoring cash flow than setting strict spending limits. But if you want to manage your entire financial universe in one clean interface, it’s genuinely brilliant.

4. Rocket Money

Rocket Money (formerly Truebill) is a lifesaver for people who are tired of losing small amounts to expenses they forgot about. It is primarily known as a subscription tracker and cancellation service, but it has evolved into a full budgeting app.

  • Why it works: It automatically scans your bank and credit card statements for recurring charges. It will identify subscriptions you don't use and can even negotiate bills on your behalf to lower your cable, internet, or phone bills.
  • The catch: The app is free to use, but the "premium" features (like the negotiation service and smart savings) require a variable membership fee based on your income. However, splitting a subscription list with a partner at the end of a big trip is a major relief.

5. Goodbudget

If you aren't comfortable connecting your bank account to an app—and a lot of people aren't—Goodbudget offers a refreshing alternative. It is based on the old-school cash envelope system but digitized. You allot a certain amount of "envelope money" to different categories like groceries, gas, or entertainment.

  • Why it works: It's entirely manual, giving you a very tactile sense of how much is left in each category. This makes it one of the safest budgeting tools if you're security-conscious. It is also great for couples, without the complexity of YNAB.
  • The catch: You have to be disciplined enough to enter every transaction. You get a limited number of envelopes on the free plan, but the paid version is relatively cheap (around $8/month) and gives you unlimited envelopes and multiple devices.

6. PocketGuard

Where YNAB wants you to plan ahead, PocketGuard is designed for "set it and forget it" simplicity. It hooks up to your accounts, analyzes your spending, and tells you how much money you have "In My Pocket" for the day or week—after accounting for bills, goals, and essentials.

  • Why it works: It’s one of the best save money app options for people who simply want to know what they can afford to spend right now. The user interface is extremely clean and uncomplicated, with no complex categories or jargon.
  • The catch: Some users find the automatic categorization a little too simplified. If you have complex investment accounts or like to micromanage every category, it might feel limiting.

7. Honeydue

Money fights are a leading cause of stress in relationships. Honeydue was built specifically for couples to manage their finances together. You can see your partner's accounts, set shared budgets, and send little "I paid for dinner" notes, or simply "like" their purchases.

  • Why it works: It balances privacy with transparency. You can choose which accounts to share and which to keep private. It gives you an excellent combined view of your future, and it helps you track spending limits side-by-side, so you aren't nagging each other.
  • The catch: It’s less robust for complex financial planning. If you're not in a committed relationship managing shared expenses, it probably won't be your go-to.

8. Monarch Money

Monarch Money is the anti-Mint tool. When Mint shut down, many users flocked to Monarch because it offers the same "all-in-one" view—bank cards, loans, investments—but with a more modern, privacy-focused edge. It is a premium tool that feels very put-together.

  • Why it works: It lets you create custom goals, track your net worth, and share your dashboard with your partner. The budgeting side is flexible, allowing you to use whatever method works best (categories, rollover, etc.).
  • The catch: There is no free version. You have to use a 30-day free trial, but it costs roughly $14.99/month after that. You're definitely paying for the refined experience.

9. Simplifi by Quicken

Simplifi is Quicken's answer to the modern minimalist budgeting app. It’s heavily automated and built for people who want up-to-date information without spending time constructing a budget. It uses "Watchlists" and "Spending Plan" modes to help you monitor your cash flow.

  • Why it works: It’s a leaner, cleaner Quicken. It handles complicated financial lives (multiple accounts, properties, subscriptions) surprisingly well without clogging the interface. It also has excellent customer support with a strong web app.
  • The catch: While it's cheaper than some competitors (around $4/month if billed annually), it simply doesn't have the educational resources or community of YNAB.

10. Copilot Money

Copilot is a premium subscription-based budgeting tool known for its stunning user interface and custom analytics. It was originally iOS-only, but has expanded to Android, making it a top contender for 2025. If you want your budgeting app to feel like a slick social media feed, this is the one.

  • Why it works: It offers "Insights" that help you understand spending trends at a glance. It uses natural language processing for transactions and is extraordinarily accurate with auto-categorization. It’s one of the best budgeting tools for people who are into data and design.
  • The catch: It is expensive (around $13/month) and the learning curve for setting up complex rules is a bit steep. Also, the Android app is still slightly behind the iOS version in terms of stability.

How to Start Budgeting with an App

Okay, so you've picked one of the budgeting apps above. Now what do you do? Here's how to reduce your financial stress in three steps:

  1. List your "Why". Do you want to pay off student loans? Save for a down payment? This motivation matters more than the app.
  2. Overview your accounts. Log all of your bank accounts and credit cards into the app. Get a clear view of your current cash flow.
  3. Set a single goal. Don't try to overhaul every spending habit at once. Pick one thing (like the grocery bill) and use your budget to fix that. Once you master that, expand your focus.

Remember, a "budget" isn't about restricting fun. It's about giving your money a job and skipping the financial anxiety.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Are budgeting apps safe to use?

Yes, most reputable budgeting apps like YNAB and Empower use bank-level encryption (AES-256). They also use a two-step verification or multi-factor authentication process. That said, never share your login password for your bank directly with a budgeting app—they should use secure financial data aggregators (like Plaid) to connect to your bank securely, rather than requiring your bank credentials.

2. What is the best budgeting app for beginners?

For total beginners, EveryDollar is a fantastic choice because of its straightforward, visual layout and basic "sinking fund" approach. PocketGuard is also a great pick because it does the math for you, showing you exactly how much "safe to spend" money you have left at any given moment.

3. Can budgeting apps really help me save money?

Absolutely. Using a save money app can help you save money by simply making you more aware of your spending patterns. Features like donation rounding, recurring auto-transfers, and subscription tracking help find hidden money. However, the app is only a tool—you still have to commit to the handshake with your calendar. But for most people, just tracking spending reduces financial mistakes significantly because it forces you to read your account activity.

Your 2025 savings goal won't achieve itself. Pick an app, connect your accounts, and take back control of your money today.
