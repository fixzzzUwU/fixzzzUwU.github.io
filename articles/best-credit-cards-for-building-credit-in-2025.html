Best Credit Cards for Building Credit in 2025

If you're new to credit or trying to rebuild after a rough patch, you're in good company. Millions of Americans have either no credit history or a credit score that needs some work. The good news? A credit card that reports your monthly activity to the three major credit bureaus is one of the most effective tools you can use to fix that.

The tricky part is knowing which card to choose. Some come with heavy fees, some do very little to actually help your score, and some are outright predatory. That's why we've put together this straightforward comparison of the best credit cards for bad credit and for people with no credit history at all. Whether you're a student opening your first account or someone rebuilding after a difficult couple of years, you'll find a clear recommendation here.

How to Build Credit from Zero: The Basics

Before we jump into product picks, let's quickly cover how credit building actually works. Your credit score is a snapshot of how reliably you handle borrowed money. The most widely used scoring model, FICO, weighs payment history (35%) and amounts owed (30%) heavier than everything else combined. So the formula for building credit is simple:

  • Use your card for small, routine purchases.
  • Keep your balance low — ideally under 30% of your credit limit.
  • Pay your bill in full and on time, every single month.
  • Give it time. Your first FICO score can appear within 3 to 6 months of credit activity.

That's the entire playbook. The card you choose only matters because it gives you the chance to execute that plan without getting nickel-and-dimed along the way.

Compare Credit Cards for Rebuilding Credit: Secured vs. Unsecured

When you're weighing your options, you'll mostly be choosing between two types of cards.

Secured credit cards are the conventional starting point for people with no credit or damaged credit. You pay a refundable deposit — usually equal to your credit limit — and the issuer holds it as collateral. After 6 to 12 months of on-time payments, many issuers will convert you to an unsecured card and return your deposit. Secured cards are the best way to build credit when your history is thin or nonexistent.
