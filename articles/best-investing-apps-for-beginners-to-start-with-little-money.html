Best Investing Apps for Beginners to Start with Little Money

Investing used to feel like a game for people with thousands of dollars sitting in a brokerage account. You'd have to save up, place a phone call, and commit a big chunk of cash to a single stock. That world is gone. Today, the best investing apps for beginners let you start with pocket change, automate your contributions, and learn as you go.

If you're a student or a young professional trying to figure out how to start investing with little money, the apps below are the easiest on-ramps. I've compared the top micro-investing apps by fees, features, and how much hand-holding they offer so you can decide which one actually fits your life.

Why Micro-Investing Apps Work So Well

The biggest barrier to investing isn't intelligence—it's friction. Opening a traditional brokerage account feels intimidating. You have to choose between dozens of funds, understand expense ratios, and keep track of what to buy next.

Micro-investing apps remove nearly all of that friction. They let you buy fractional shares, so you can own a piece of a $500 stock with just $10. Many also manage your portfolio automatically, which means you don't have to research companies or make trade decisions. Just link a bank account, set a schedule or round-up rule, and the app handles the rest.

For beginners, this is the difference between thinking about investing and actually doing it.

How to Start Investing with Little Money: Features That Matter

Before you download an app, it helps to know what you're actually looking for. Here's what separates a useful beginner investing app from a confusing one.

Low or No Minimum Deposit

Every app on this list allows you to open an account with zero dollars. That's critical when you're just starting out. You shouldn't have to wait until you have a $500 lump sum. The whole point is to invest in small, manageable amounts.

Fractional Shares

This is the single biggest game-changer for micro-investing. With fractional shares, you're not buying a whole share of Amazon or Apple. You're buying a tiny slice of it. That means your $20 can be spread across multiple companies instead of being locked into one low-priced stock.

Automation

The most successful beginner investors don't rely on willpower. They set up automated contributions. Apps like Acorns let you invest spare change from purchases or make recurring weekly transfers. When investing happens automatically, you never forget to do it.

Clear Pricing

All the apps on this list make their fees visible. You shouldn't need a finance degree to understand pricing. Look for flat monthly subscription fees or commission-free trades, and avoid platforms with confusing margin requirements.

Educational Content

You're new to this. A good app should help you understand the basics, not bury you in jargon. Stash, in particular, does this well with short, digestible lessons inside the app.

Acorns Review: Put Your Spare Change to Work

Acorns is the most popular micro-investing app, and for good reason. It doesn't ask you to make any decisions. You connect a bank account and a debit card, and every time you buy something, Acorns rounds up the total to the nearest dollar. If your coffee costs $3.60, Acorns invests the extra $0.40.

That's the core hook, but the app does more. You can set up recurring investments, earn bonus cash back from Shop with Acorns, and pick from a set of diversified portfolios built by a team of experts using ETFs from companies like Vanguard and BlackRock.

The fee is a flat $3 per month on the basic plan, which includes a checking account. That can feel expensive if you're only investing a few dollars, but if you round up regularly and build good habits, the value becomes clearer. Acorns also offers an Acorns Later account, which is an IRA, and an Acorns Early account for kids.

Who is Acorns for? It's perfect for anyone who wants to invest without thinking about it. If you're the type of person who finds tracking investments boring, Acorns does the work for you. It's also the best answer to "how do I start investing with little money when I don't know what to buy?"—because you don't have to choose anything.

Webull Review: A Step Up for Active Beginners

Webull is a different beast. While Acorns is set-and-forget, Webull is a real brokerage platform that gives you access to the stock market directly. You can buy fractional shares of stocks and ETFs, and trading is completely commission-free. No hidden fees, no monthly charges.

What makes Webull one of the best investing apps for beginners who want to actively manage their money is how much it gives you for free. You get real-time market data, charting tools, and paper trading—a feature that let's you practice making trades with fake money before risking your real savings. That's a powerful learning tool.

Webull also offers IRA accounts, a cash management account, and an easy-to-navigate mobile app. However, it has more features than you need at the very start, which can feel overwhelming. You'll also need to do your own research; Webull won't tell you what to buy. But if you enjoy the idea of picking stocks and learning through experience, this is a solid choice.

The biggest catch is that Webull is not truly "automated." It's a trading platform, not a robo-advisor. You will have to make your own decisions. That's fine if you want to be hands-on. Just know that if you don't want to manage anything, stick with Acorns or Stash.

Stash Review: Learn While You Invest

Stash sits right in the middle. It lets you choose your own investments, but it guides you the whole way. You can start with as little as $1, and you'll get access to fractional shares of individual stocks and a wide range of ETFs, all organized into themes. Want to invest in "Clean Energy" or "Video Games"? Stash bundles the relevant companies for you.

What makes Stash stand out is its emphasis on education. Every time you buy something, the app explains what the company does, why it might be a good long-term hold, and how the stock fits into the broader economy. There are also short courses, breakdowns of money concepts, and financial wellness tips.

Stash charges a monthly fee depending on your plan. The basic plan is $3 per month, the mid-tier plan is $9, and the top-tier plan is $12. There's also a checking account available in some plans, with features like automatic savings and early paycheck deposits.

Who is Stash for? It's for beginners who want more control than Acorns but more help than Webull. If you're genuinely curious about how investing works, Stash will teach you while still letting you automate your contributions.

Which Investing App Should You Choose?

It really comes down to how involved you want to be.

  • Choose Acorns if you want the absolute easiest experience. Link your card, let the round-ups do their thing, and don't touch anything.
  • Choose Webull if you want to learn how to research and pick individual stocks without paying commissions.
  • Choose Stash if you want a middle ground—a guided experience with educational content and the freedom to make your own choices.

All three are excellent investing apps for beginners. None of them require a large upfront deposit, and all of them allow you to start with pocket change. The "best" one is simply the one that matches your personality and habits.

Conclusion

Starting to invest with little money isn't just possible—it's easier than it's ever been. Micro-investing apps have torn down the barriers of minimum balances, expensive commissions, and confusing research. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I really start investing with just $5 or $10?

Yes. Almost every micro-investing app on this list allows you to open an account with no minimum deposit. To actually buy something, you'll need at least $1 or $5, depending on the app. Fractional shares make this possible, so you can own a piece of an expensive stock without needing hundreds of dollars.

Are micro-investing apps safe to use?

Yes, these apps are regulated by the SEC and FINRA. Stocks, ETFs, and cash balances in brokerage accounts are protected by SIPC insurance up to $500,000. Additionally, any uninvested cash held in partner banks is typically FDIC insured, so your money is not just floating around unprotected.

Do I have to pay taxes on money I make from investing apps?

Yes. If you sell investments for a profit, you'll owe capital gains tax. The same applies to dividends you receive. However, many of these apps offer retirement accounts, like a Roth IRA, where investments grow tax-free. If you're just starting out and staying below certain income thresholds, your tax bill will likely be minimal, but it's still a good idea to track your activity.
