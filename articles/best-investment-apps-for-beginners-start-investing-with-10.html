Best Investment Apps for Beginners: Start Investing with $10

The biggest myth in personal finance is that you need a fat bank account to start investing. The truth? You can open an account with $10 and start buying fractional shares of the same companies wealthy investors own. The real barrier isn't money—it's knowing which app fits your style.

That's where this guide comes in. We compared the most popular beginner-friendly platforms on the market—robo-advisors, micro investing apps, and commission-free brokers—so you can find the right fit without the overwhelm. By the end, you'll know exactly how to start investing with little money and which app deserves a spot on your home screen.

How to Start Investing with Little Money

Starting small isn't just possible—it's actually smart. With just a few dollars, you can build an investing habit, learn how markets move, and let compound growth do its thing over time. The best part? Most modern apps don't require a minimum balance, and some let you invest spare change from everyday purchases.

Here's the simple formula: pick an app, connect your bank account, set up a small recurring deposit (even $5 a week), and let automation handle the rest. You don't need to watch the market daily or memorize financial jargon. You just need consistency and a little patience.

What to Look for in a Beginner Investing App

Before you download anything, keep these three things in mind:

  • Low or no minimums – You want an app that lets you start with $10 or less. Many platforms now offer $0 minimums and fractional shares, so your first few dollars go a long way.
  • Fractional shares – This is a game-changer for beginners. Instead of needing the full price of a stock like Amazon or Google, you can buy a tiny slice of it for as little as $1.
  • Education and automation – Beginners benefit from apps that explain what you're buying or make investing automatic. If you're not sure where to start, a robo-advisor that builds your portfolio for you is worth serious consideration.

Once you know what to look for, comparing platforms gets much easier.

Best Investment Apps for Beginners

Here are the top options worth your time. Each one takes a slightly different approach, so think about the kind of investor you want to be before committing.

Acorns – Best Micro Investing App for Spare Change

Acorns is the classic "set it and forget it" app. It links to your bank account, rounds up your everyday purchases to the nearest dollar, and invests the difference into a diversified portfolio. You can also set up recurring deposits of just a few dollars.

If you're not sure how to choose investments, Acorns does it for you—which is why it's consistently rated one of the best robo advisors for beginners who want zero decision-making. It takes a small monthly fee, but for many users, the automated discipline is worth every penny.

Robinhood – Best for Hands-On Beginners

Robinhood made commission-free trading mainstream, and it's still one of the most popular apps for new investors. You can buy stocks, ETFs, and even crypto with no trading fees, and fractional shares mean you don't need hundreds of dollars to own a piece of a big-name company.

It's a great fit if you want to learn by doing. The trade-off? There's no built-in portfolio management. You pick your own stocks and ETFs, so it helps to be willing to learn the basics. If you're the kind of person who likes control, this is your app.

SoFi Invest – Best All-in-One for New Investors

SoFi Invest stands out because it gives you the best of both worlds: an active investing account and an automated robo-advisor option. You can start with a few dollars, trade stocks and ETFs with no fees, and even talk to a human financial planner if you upgrade.

For younger investors who also want banking, loans, and credit cards in one ecosystem, SoFi is tough to beat. It's an excellent choice if you want to grow your money and manage your everyday finances from a single app.

Betterment – Best Robo Advisor for Beginners

Betterment is one of the most established robo-advisors on the market. You tell it your goals and risk tolerance, and it builds and manages a diversified portfolio of ETFs for you. The digital plan has no minimum balance, so you can genuinely start with just $10.

Automation is the main draw here. Betterment handles rebalancing, dividend reinvestment, and tax-efficiency strategies behind the scenes. If you want to invest without watching the market every day, it's hard to beat.

Acorns vs Robinhood: Which Is Right for You?

If you've done any research on investing apps, you've probably seen this comparison everywhere. Here's the honest breakdown.

Acorns is built for passive investors. It automatically invests your spare change, builds a diversified portfolio, and rebalances it for you over time. You don't need to know anything about the stock market—just connect your card and let the app do the heavy lifting.

Robinhood is built for active learners. You get full control, real-time prices, and the freedom to build your own portfolio. It's a fantastic place to learn how trading works, but it doesn't hold your hand. You make the decisions, and you own the results.

So which should you choose? If you want to "set it and forget it," go with Acorns. If you want to be involved in every buy and sell and genuinely learn the ropes, Robinhood is the better fit. Both are excellent ways to start investing with little money, just with very different philosophies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I really start investing with just $10?

Yes. Many top apps, including Acorns, Robinhood, and SoFi Invest, let you open an account with no minimum balance. Fractional shares mean your $10 can buy into companies and ETFs that would normally cost hundreds of dollars per share.

Acorns vs Robinhood: what's the actual difference?

Acorns is a robo-advisor—it invests your money automatically using pre-built portfolios based on your goals and risk tolerance. Robinhood is a brokerage—it gives you a platform to buy and sell investments yourself. Acorns is passive; Robinhood is active. Choose based on how involved you want to be.

What is a robo-advisor?

A robo-advisor is a digital platform that uses algorithms to build and manage a diversified portfolio for you. You answer a few questions about your goals and risk tolerance, and the app handles the rest—selecting investments, rebalancing, and reinvesting dividends automatically. It's ideal for beginners who want professional-level investing without expensive human advisors.

The Bottom Line

The hardest part of investing isn't picking the perfect stock or timing the market—it's starting. With $10 and the right app, you can begin building wealth today. If you want automation, go with Acorns or Betterment. If you want hands-on control, Robinhood is a solid choice. And if you want a bit of everything, SoFi Invest is a great middle ground.

Whichever you choose, remember this: consistency beats intention. Start small, invest regularly, and let time do the heavy lifting. Your future self will thank you.
