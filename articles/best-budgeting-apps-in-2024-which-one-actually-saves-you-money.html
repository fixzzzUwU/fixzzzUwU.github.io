Best Budgeting Apps in 2024: Which One Actually Saves You Money?

If you’re still tracking every latte in a Google Sheet, you’re not alone—but you’re also making life harder than it needs to be. The best budgeting app in 2024 should do more than show you pie charts. It should automate the boring parts, help you build savings goals, and connect to a high yield savings account so your emergency fund earns something while it sits there. Since Intuit shut down Mint in 2024, many people are hunting for apps like Mint that combine budgeting, bill tracking, and net worth in one place. What to Look for in a Budgeting App in 2024

The best budgeting app for you depends on how much control you want. Some people want a zero-based budget that assigns every dollar a job. Others want a dashboard that automatically categorizes spending and tells them what’s safe to spend. Both are valid.

Focus on four things:

  • Automation: Does it sync accounts reliably and categorize transactions without constant cleanup?
  • Cost: A free tier is great, but paid apps often offer better automation, support, and savings tools.
  • Savings features: Look for goal tracking, automated transfers, and the ability to connect a high yield savings account.
  • Household fit: If you share money with a partner, choose an app with shared budgets and permissions.

Best Budgeting Apps Compared

YNAB — Best for Hands-On Budgeters

YNAB, short for You Need A Budget, is still the gold standard for zero-based budgeting. It asks you to give every dollar a job, which is powerful if you’re willing to check in regularly. It’s not the most automated option, and it’s a paid subscription, but its method and educational resources are excellent for people who want to break the paycheck-to-paycheck cycle. If you’ve tried spreadsheets and failed, YNAB is the structured upgrade.

Copilot Money — Best for Automation-First iPhone Users

Copilot Money is a favorite for people who want a beautiful, low-effort budgeting experience. It uses strong categorization and clean visuals to show spending trends, recurring bills, and net worth. It’s best for Apple users, and it’s a paid app, but the automation is the selling point. If you want an app that does the heavy lifting and nudges you when spending looks off, Copilot is a strong Mint replacement.

Monarch Money — Best Mint Alternative for Couples

Monarch Money is one of the closest replacements for Mint’s all-in-one dashboard. It combines budgeting, account tracking, investments, and shared access for partners. It’s a paid app, but the household features make it worth considering if you’re managing money with a spouse or partner. It’s less about strict envelope budgeting and more about seeing your whole financial picture in one place.

Rocket Money — Best Free Budget App With Bill Negotiation

Rocket Money has a free tier that covers spending tracking and a premium tier for bill negotiation, subscription cancellation, and deeper insights. For anyone asking “what’s the best free budget app,” Rocket Money is a practical starting point because it helps you find wasted subscriptions and recurring charges. The free version has limits, but it’s a low-friction way to start automating your money management.

Simplifi by Quicken — Best for a Simple Spending Plan

Simplifi focuses on a straightforward spending plan rather than complex categories. It shows what you have left to spend after bills and savings goals, which is helpful for people who don’t want to micromanage every category. It’s a paid app with a clean interface and solid reporting. If Mint felt cluttered but YNAB feels too intense, Simplifi is a good middle ground.

Goodbudget — Best Free Envelope Budgeting

Goodbudget uses digital envelopes to help you divide income into spending categories. It has a free tier that works for simple budgets, plus a paid tier for more envelopes and accounts. It’s not as automated as Copilot or Monarch, but it’s a solid choice if you like the envelope method and want a free budget app without a steep learning curve.

Best Free Budget App Options

Free budgeting apps are a smart way to test a system before paying. Rocket Money, Goodbudget, and Empower are common starting points. Empower is especially useful if you want a free dashboard for net worth and investments, though it’s less of a traditional monthly budget tool. The trade-off with free apps is usually fewer connected accounts, more upsells, or less automation. That’s fine if you’re willing to review transactions weekly. If you want set-and-forget categorization, a paid app like Copilot or Monarch may save you enough time and friction to justify the cost.

Apps Like Mint: Which Replacement Is Right for You?

Mint’s shutdown left a gap for people who liked one dashboard for budgets, bills, and net worth. The right replacement depends on your priority:

  • You want strict budgeting: YNAB.
  • You want automation and design: Copilot Money.
  • You share money with a partner: Monarch Money.
  • You want a free start: Rocket Money or Goodbudget.
  • You want a simple spending plan: Simplifi by Quicken.

If you mainly used Mint to watch subscriptions and bills, Rocket Money is the closest free fit. If you used Mint as a full financial dashboard, Monarch or Copilot will feel more familiar.

Automatic Savings Apps and High Yield Savings Accounts

An automatic savings app can make saving feel less like willpower and more like a system. Apps like Qapital let you set rules—round up purchases, save when you skip a purchase, or transfer a set amount after payday. Acorns and Chime offer similar automation with different features. The goal is to remove the monthly decision to save.

Pair that automation with a high yield savings account. Traditional big-bank savings accounts often pay very little, while online banks and fintech accounts offer competitive APY. Rates change, so check the current rate before opening an account. Look for no monthly fees, FDIC insurance, and goal buckets if you’re saving for multiple things. Ally is known for easy goal buckets, Marcus by Goldman Sachs is known for simplicity, and SoFi and Wealthfront offer competitive savings features. The best high yield savings account is the one that makes automatic transfers easy and keeps your emergency fund separate from your checking account.

FAQ

What is the best free budget app in 2024?

Rocket Money and Goodbudget are strong free options. Rocket Money is better for tracking subscriptions and bills, while Goodbudget is better for envelope-style budgeting. Empower is a good free dashboard if you mainly want net worth tracking.

Are there good apps like Mint now that Mint is gone?

Yes. Copilot Money and Monarch Money are close replacements for Mint’s all-in-one dashboard. Simplifi by Quicken is a good middle-ground option, and Rocket Money is a practical free alternative for bill and subscription tracking.

Do budgeting apps actually save you money?

They can, but not automatically. Budgeting apps save money by making spending visible, catching forgotten subscriptions, and automating savings transfers. The app is the tool; the savings come from the habits you build around it.
