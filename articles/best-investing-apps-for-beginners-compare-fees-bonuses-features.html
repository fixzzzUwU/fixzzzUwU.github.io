Best Investing Apps for Beginners: Compare Fees, Bonuses & Features

Getting started with investing in your twenties or thirties can feel overwhelming. Between confusing financial jargon, unpredictable market headlines, and the fear of losing your hard-earned money, it is easy to put off investing until "someday." But the truth is, you do not need thousands of dollars or a degree in finance to start building wealth. You just need the right tools.

The rise of fintech has made it easier than ever for US and EU millennials and Gen Z to enter the market. Whether you have $100 to spare or $1,000 ready to grow, there is an app designed for your specific needs. This guide breaks down the best investing apps for beginners, comparing fees, bonuses, and features so you can choose the platform that fits your lifestyle.

Understanding the Types of Investing Apps

Before we dive into specific recommendations, it helps to understand the three main categories of apps available to new investors. Your choice depends on how hands-on you want to be and what your financial goals are.

Micro-Investing Apps

These apps are designed for absolute beginners. They allow you to start with very small amounts—sometimes as little as $1. They often use "round-ups," where the app rounds your spare change from everyday purchases up to the nearest dollar and invests the difference. If you are looking for passive investing apps that fit seamlessly into your daily routine, this is a great starting point.

Robo-Advisors

A robo-advisor is a digital platform that manages your investment portfolio for you. You answer a few questions about your risk tolerance and goals, and the algorithm builds a diversified portfolio of ETFs (Exchange Traded Funds). This is the best option for beginners who want a "set it and forget it" approach to passive investing.

Traditional Brokerage Accounts

These are standard platforms that allow you to buy individual stocks and ETFs. While historically these came with high fees, modern brokerages now offer commission-free trading. These are ideal if you want to learn how to pick individual companies, but they require more research and effort on your part.

What to Compare: Fees, Minimums, and Bonuses

When comparing the best investing apps for beginners, you need to look beyond the marketing. Here are the three most critical factors:

  1. Expense Ratios and Management Fees: Robo-advisors typically charge a management fee (usually 0.25% of your assets). Micro-investing apps often charge a flat monthly fee (e.g., $1–$5). Traditional brokerages usually charge $0 for stock trades but may charge for options or mutual funds.
  2. Account Minimums: Many apps now have a $0 minimum. However, some robo-advisors still require a minimum deposit (often $500 or $1,000) to start.
  3. Sign-Up Bonuses: Many platforms offer a cash bonus or free stock when you open an account and fund it. This is free money that can give your portfolio an immediate boost.

How to Start Investing with $100

If you have $100 to start, you are in a great position. Here is a simple roadmap to get started using the apps above:

  1. Choose your platform: If you want to be hands-off, go with a robo-advisor like Betterment or Wealthfront. If you want to learn about stocks, try a micro-investing app like Stash or a brokerage like Public.
  2. Open your account: Most apps allow you to sign up in minutes. You will need your Social Security Number (or equivalent for EU users) and a bank account link.
  3. Fund your account: Transfer your $100. Many apps have a $0 minimum, but some robo-advisors might require a higher initial deposit.
  4. Select your portfolio: If you are using a robo-advisor, answer the risk assessment questions. If you are using a brokerage, consider starting with a broad market ETF (like one tracking the S&P 500) to diversify your risk.
  5. Automate your contributions: Set up a recurring transfer of $10, $25, or $50 per month. Consistency is the key to building wealth over time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid as a Beginner

  • Trying to Time the Market: It is impossible to predict the market's highs and lows. Instead, focus on "time in the market." Consistent investing over years beats trying to buy low and sell high.
  • Ignoring Fees: A 1% fee might not sound like much, but it can eat into your returns significantly over 30 years. Always check the expense ratios of the funds you buy.
  • Panic Selling: When the market dips, it is natural to want to sell to stop the bleeding. However, dips are normal. If you are invested for the long term, the best move is usually to do nothing or buy more.
  • Not Diversifying: Don't put all your money into one stock. Use ETFs or robo-advisors to spread your risk across hundreds of companies.

FAQ

1. What is the best investing app for a complete beginner?

For a complete beginner who wants a hands-off experience, Acorns or Betterment are excellent choices. Acorns is great for saving spare change, while Betterment offers a more robust automated portfolio for long-term goals.

2. Can I start investing with just $100?

Yes. Many modern apps have no minimum deposit requirements and offer fractional shares, meaning you can buy a portion of a stock or ETF for as little as $1. This makes it very accessible to start investing with $100.

3. Are micro-investing apps safe?

Yes, reputable micro-investing apps are safe. They are typically SIPC-insured (in the US) or similarly protected in the EU, which means your securities are protected up to a certain limit if the brokerage fails. However, micro-investing apps often charge monthly fees, so it is important to ensure the fees do not eat into your returns.
