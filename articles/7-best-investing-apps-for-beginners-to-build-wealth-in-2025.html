7 Best Investing Apps for Beginners to Build Wealth in 2025

If you've been waiting until you have "enough" money to start investing, here's the good news: you don't need thousands of dollars to begin. You need a few dollars, a little patience, and an app that doesn't make you feel like you need a finance degree to use it.

The problem is that there are dozens of investing apps competing for your attention, and most of them are marketed with the same promises. This guide cuts through that. Below are seven genuinely beginner-friendly options — micro-investing apps, robo-advisors, and commission-free brokerages — plus a simple framework for choosing the right one for your goals in 2025.

What to Look For in a Beginner Investing App

Before comparing names, know what actually matters:

  • Low or no minimum balance. You should be able to start with $100 or less — ideally $5.
  • Transparent fees. Subscription fees, advisory fees, and expense ratios all eat into returns. Know what you're paying.
  • Fractional shares. This lets you own a slice of an expensive stock or ETF instead of buying a full share.
  • Automation. Recurring deposits and automatic rebalancing are what turn investing into a habit rather than a chore.
  • Real education. Plain-English explanations of what you own and why.
  • Regulation. In the US, your broker should be a member of FINRA/SIPC. In the EU, look for entities regulated under MiFID II. This protects you if the firm fails.

The 7 Best Investing Apps for Beginners in 2025

1. Acorns — Best for Spare-Change Micro-Investing

Acorns rounds up your everyday purchases to the nearest dollar and invests the difference into a diversified portfolio. It's the classic "set it and forget it" app for people who struggle to save consistently. You can also add recurring transfers and choose from a handful of pre-built portfolios based on your risk tolerance. The trade-off is a small monthly subscription fee, so it makes the most sense if you'll actually use the round-ups.

2. Stash — Best for Learning as You Invest

Stash is built around education. It offers themed investment portfolios, short lessons on investing basics, and a simple interface that explains what you're buying. Like Acorns, it charges a monthly subscription rather than per-trade commissions. If you want to understand why you're investing in something — not just tap a button — Stash is a strong starting point.

3. Betterment — Best Robo-Advisor for Hands-Off Beginners

A robo-advisor builds and manages a diversified portfolio for you based on a short questionnaire about your goals and risk tolerance. Betterment is one of the most established options, offering goal-based investing, automatic rebalancing, and tax-efficient features on higher tiers. It's ideal if you want professional-style portfolio management without paying a human advisor.

4. Wealthfront — Best for Automated Passive Investing

Wealthfront takes a similar automated approach and pairs it with a strong cash management offering, which makes it convenient if you want your emergency fund and your investment account in one place. It's particularly popular with younger investors who want a long-term, hands-off strategy and don't want to pick individual stocks.

5. Robinhood — Best Commission-Free Brokerage for Fractional Shares

Robinhood popularized commission-free trading and fractional shares, which means you can buy a slice of a company for as little as a dollar. It's a solid choice if you want to learn by owning individual stocks and ETFs. Just be honest with yourself: an app that makes trading easy can also make over-trading tempting. Buy and hold beats constant tinkering almost every time.

6. M1 Finance — Best for Building a Custom Portfolio

M1 lets you create a "pie" — a custom mix of stocks and ETFs with your chosen percentages — and then automatically routes new deposits to keep that mix balanced. It's a nice middle ground between a fully automated robo-advisor and a DIY brokerage, and it works well for beginners who have opinions about what they want to own.

7. Fidelity Go — Best Low-Cost Robo-Advisor From a Big Name

If you'd rather trust a household-name brokerage, Fidelity Go offers automated portfolio management with no minimum balance and no advisory fee on smaller accounts. You get the stability of an established firm plus the simplicity of a robo-advisor. It's a good fit for beginners who want to start small but plan to scale up over time.

How to Start Investing With $100

You don't need a complicated strategy. Here's a straightforward path:

  1. Pick one app. Not three. Opening multiple accounts spreads $100 too thin and makes tracking harder.
  2. Fund the account. Transfer your $100 and set up a recurring deposit — even $10 or $20 a month. Consistency matters more than the starting amount.
  3. Choose a diversified fund or portfolio. A broad market ETF or a robo-advisor portfolio gives you exposure to hundreds of companies at once.
  4. Automate everything. Set the deposit and forget it. Automation removes emotion from the equation.
  5. Leave it alone. Check in quarterly, not daily. Short-term price movements are noise.
  6. Increase your contribution when your income grows. A raise is the easiest time to bump your monthly deposit.

Acorns vs Stash: Which Is Right for You?

This is the most common comparison among first-time investors, and the answer depends on your habits.

Choose Acorns if you want saving to feel invisible. Round-ups turn everyday spending into investments, and the app does the thinking for you.

Choose Stash if you want to understand what you're buying. The educational content and themed portfolios make it better for people who want to build knowledge alongside their portfolio.

Both charge a monthly fee, both are beginner-friendly, and both are best suited to long-term investors rather than active traders.

Passive Income Investing Apps: Setting Realistic Expectations

Search for "passive income investing apps" and you'll find promises of easy money. Here's the honest version: investing builds wealth over years and decades, not weeks. Dividends, interest, and long-term price appreciation can generate income, but the amounts start small and grow with your contributions.

What these apps do make genuinely passive is the process. Automatic deposits, automatic rebalancing, and automatic dividend reinvestment mean you can build wealth without watching charts. That's the real value — not instant income.

The Bottom Line

The best investing app for beginners is the one you'll actually use consistently. If you want spare change to do the work, look at Acorns. If you want to learn as you go, Stash is a natural fit. If you'd rather hand the wheel to an algorithm, Betterment, Wealthfront, or Fidelity Go will manage a diversified portfolio for you. And if you want to pick your own investments without commissions, Robinhood and M1 Finance give you the tools.

Start with $100, automate a small monthly deposit, and let time do the heavy lifting.

FAQ

What's the best investing app for a complete beginner with $100?

Any of the seven above can work with $100. If you want the simplest possible experience, a robo-advisor like Betterment or Fidelity Go handles the portfolio decisions for you. If you want to learn by doing, Stash or Robinhood are good starting points.

Are investing apps safe?

Reputable apps hold your investments with a regulated broker-dealer and carry SIPC protection in the US (up to $500,000 for securities, including $250,000 for cash claims), or equivalent protections under MiFID II in the EU. That protects you if the firm fails — it does not protect you from market losses, which are a normal part of investing.

How much money do I need to start investing?

Many beginner apps have no minimum or a very low one, so you can start with $5 to $100. What matters more than your starting amount is contributing regularly. A small automatic deposit every month will outperform a large one-time investment you never follow up on.
