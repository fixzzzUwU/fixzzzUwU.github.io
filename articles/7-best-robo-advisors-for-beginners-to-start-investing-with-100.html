7 Best Robo-Advisors for Beginners to Start Investing with $100

You don't need thousands of dollars to start investing. You don't need to read earnings reports, either. What you need is a small amount of money, a few minutes to set things up, and a tool that does the boring parts for you.

That's exactly what robo-advisors were built for. They take your money, spread it across a diversified mix of low-cost funds, and rebalance it automatically as the market moves. For first-time investors — especially young professionals who want to build wealth without babysitting a portfolio — they remove almost every excuse to get started.

Below are seven of the best robo-advisors for beginners, what each one does well, and how to pick the right one when you're starting with just $100.

What Is a Robo-Advisor, and Why Are They Great for Beginners?

A robo-advisor is an automated investing platform. You answer a short questionnaire about your goals, timeline, and comfort with risk. The platform then builds a portfolio for you — usually a mix of stock and bond ETFs — and manages it going forward.

Three things make them ideal for beginners:

  • Low minimums. Many let you open an account with $1 to $100, or nothing at all.
  • Automatic everything. Deposits, purchases, and rebalancing happen without you logging in.
  • Built-in guardrails. You can't accidentally put your entire $100 into one meme stock.

If your goal is passive investing for beginners — set it, fund it, forget it — a robo-advisor is the shortest path there.

How to Start Investing with Little Money: 4 Things That Matter

Before comparing platforms, know what actually separates a good fit from a bad one.

1. Minimum deposit

If you have $100, a platform with a $500 or $1,000 minimum is off the table. Look for low or no minimums.

2. Fees

Watch for two types: an annual advisory fee (a percentage of your balance) and fund expense ratios (a small cost built into each fund). Both are usually low with robo-advisors, but they're not zero. Always check the current fee schedule on the provider's site before signing up.

3. Automation

The single best feature for beginners is recurring deposits. Even $25 a week beats a one-time $100 deposit, because consistency does more work than timing.

4. Fractional shares

Fractional shares let your $100 buy a slice of an expensive fund instead of sitting in cash. Most modern robo-advisors and micro-investing apps handle this automatically.

The 7 Best Robo-Advisors for Beginners

1. Betterment — Best Overall for Hands-Off Investors

Betterment is the classic starting point: clean interface, goal-based portfolios, and automatic tax features as your balance grows. You set a goal, connect your bank, and it handles the rest.

  • Best for: Beginners who want a true "set it and forget it" account
  • Standout features: Goal-based investing, automatic rebalancing, tax-loss harvesting on larger balances
  • Watch out for: Advisory fees apply — compare tiers before choosing
  • Get started: Betterment

2. Acorns — Best for Investing Spare Change

Acorns rounds up your purchases to the nearest dollar and invests the difference. It's one of the easiest ways to start investing with little money because the money comes from spending you're already doing.

  • Best for: People who struggle to save consistently
  • Standout features: Round-ups, recurring investments, simple preset portfolios
  • Watch out for: A flat monthly fee, so it's less efficient at very small balances
  • Get started: Acorns

3. Stash — Best for Learning While You Invest

Stash leans into education. It explains what you're buying in plain English and lets you start with a small amount, which makes it a comfortable first app for anyone nervous about the stock market.

  • Best for: First-time investors who want guidance, not jargon
  • Standout features: Guided portfolios, educational content, fractional shares
  • Watch out for: Monthly subscription pricing — check the current tiers
  • Get started: Stash

4. M1 Finance — Best for Building Your Own Portfolio

M1 combines automated investing with a "pie" system that lets you choose your own allocation of stocks and ETFs. Once your pie is set, deposits are distributed automatically according to your percentages.

  • Best for: Beginners who want control without constant trading
  • Standout features: Custom portfolios, automatic deposits, fractional shares
  • Watch out for: You're responsible for your own allocation choices
  • Get started: M1 Finance

5. Public — Best Beginner-Friendly Investing App

Public is a straightforward investing app with a social, community-driven feel. It supports fractional shares, so a $100 deposit goes to work immediately, and it's simple enough for a total beginner to navigate.

  • Best for: Beginners who want to invest in specific companies and ETFs
  • Standout features: Fractional shares, themed collections, easy-to-read interface
  • Watch out for: This is self-directed investing, not fully automated management
  • Get started: Public

6. Fidelity Go — Best No-Minimum Managed Account

Fidelity Go is a managed robo-advisor from a major brokerage. It's appealing for beginners because there's no account minimum, and the managed tier is designed to be accessible to small balances.

  • Best for: Beginners who want a big-name brokerage behind their account
  • Standout features: No minimum to open, automatic portfolio management, low-cost funds
  • Watch out for: Fee structure changes as your balance grows — verify current terms
  • Get started: Fidelity Go

7. Titan — Best for Hands-Off Active Management

Titan takes a more involved approach than a standard robo-advisor, running concentrated portfolios that are actively managed by its team. It typically requires a starting deposit around $100, which keeps it within reach for beginners.

  • Best for: Investors who want professional management without picking stocks
  • Standout features: Actively managed portfolios, mobile-first experience
  • Watch out for: Higher fees than passive robo-advisors, and more risk
  • Get started: Titan

How to Choose the Best Robo Advisor for Beginners

Ask yourself three questions:

  1. Do I want to pick investments, or have them picked for me? If it's the latter, choose Betterment, Fidelity Go, or Acorns. If you want some control, M1 Finance or Public.
  2. Will I actually fund it consistently? If yes, any of these work. If no, pick Acorns and let round-ups do the work.
  3. What's the real cost at my balance? A flat monthly fee can eat a meaningful chunk of a $100 account. A percentage-based fee is usually gentler at small balances.

A Simple $100 Starter Plan

Here's a realistic way to begin:

  1. Open one account — not three. Splitting $100 across multiple platforms just multiplies fees.
  2. Deposit your $100 and set a recurring transfer of $25 to $50 per payday.
  3. Choose a moderate risk portfolio if your horizon is 10+ years.
  4. Turn on automatic rebalancing and dividend reinvestment.
  5. Log in once a quarter. That's it.

The goal in year one isn't returns — it's building the habit.

FAQ

What is the best robo-advisor for beginners with only $100?

There's no single winner, but Betterment and Fidelity Go are strong all-around choices for hands-off investors, while Acorns and Stash are better if you want to start with very small amounts and learn as you go.

Can I really start investing with $100?

Yes. Fractional shares mean your money buys into diversified funds immediately instead of sitting in cash. The bigger factor is consistency — recurring deposits matter more than your starting balance.

Are robo-advisors safe for first-time investors?

They're regulated financial platforms, and accounts typically carry SIPC protection for brokerage accounts in the US. That protects against broker failure, not market losses — your portfolio value will still rise and fall with the market.

Final Thoughts

The best investing app for you is the one you'll actually use. If you want zero decisions, pick a fully automated robo-advisor. If you want a bit more involvement, choose a platform with custom portfolios. Either way, starting with $100 today puts you ahead of waiting for the "right time" — which never arrives.

Pick one, fund it, automate it, and let it run.
