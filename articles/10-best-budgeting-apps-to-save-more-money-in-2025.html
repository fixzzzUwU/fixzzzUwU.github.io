10 Best Budgeting Apps to Save More Money in 2025

Getting control of your money doesn't require a finance degree or a spreadsheet obsession. Whether you're a student tracking your first paycheck or a family trying to stretch a single income, the right budgeting app can make saving feel less like a chore and more like a habit. We've tested the most popular options on the market to bring you the 10 best budgeting apps for 2025, covering everything from no-frills trackers to full-on zero-based budget systems.

Why Use a Budgeting App at All?

Manual tracking with a notebook is easy to abandon by mid-February. A good app automates the tedious parts while showing you exactly where your money goes each month. And if you're wondering how to save money on a low income, a budgeting app helps you spot small leaks and redirect those dollars toward savings. The best budgeting apps do the heavy lifting so you can focus on making smarter decisions.

The 10 Best Budgeting Apps to Save More Money in 2025

1. YNAB — You Need A Budget

YNAB is the gold standard for zero-based budgeting. Every dollar you earn gets assigned a job: rent, groceries, savings, or guilt-free spending. It's not the cheapest app on this list, but many users report saving the subscription cost within the first month by cutting waste they didn't realize existed. YNAB is particularly helpful for people with irregular income because it forces you to budget only what you actually have, not what you expect to earn.

2. EveryDollar

Created by Dave Ramsey, EveryDollar brings his famous "baby steps" approach to your phone. The free version is enough for basic zero-based budgeting, while the paid version connects to your bank and syncs transactions automatically. EveryDollar is one of the most intuitive budgeting apps for beginners — if you can write a list, you can use it. Families who share a single bank account will appreciate the straightforward, month-at-a-glance layout.

3. PocketGuard

PocketGuard answers one question: "What can I safely spend today?" It syncs your bills, savings goals, and income, then shows your "Spendable" balance — the money left after everything important is covered. For young professionals who want visible guardrails without constant manual tracking, PocketGuard fits the bill. It's a practical monthly budget planner app for people who prefer the big picture over line-by-line categories.

4. Goodbudget

Goodbudget modernizes the classic envelope system. You allocate cash to digital envelopes — groceries, eating out, transport — and once an envelope is empty, that category is done for the month. It's a wonderful choice for couples and families because you can share envelopes across devices, making it clear who has spent what. If your goal is to save money on a low income, the free version's limited envelopes still work surprisingly well.

5. Fudget

Fudget strips budgeting down to its bare essentials: income on one side, expenses on the other. No bank syncing, no complex categories, no learning curve. You simply add numbers and see your balance. For students who feel overwhelmed by apps filled with charts and graphs, Fudget is a breath of fresh air. It's also completely free for basic use, which is hard to beat when you're on a tight budget.

6. Honeydue

Money conversations can be tense for couples. Honeydue allows both partners to view shared accounts while setting boundaries on what each person can see. You can comment on purchases, send gentle reminders for shared bills, and set monthly spending limits for each category. The app was designed with couples in mind, but it's also a useful monthly budget planner app for roommates splitting utilities and rent.

7. Empower — Personal Capital

Empower, formerly known as Personal Capital, is a hybrid between budgeting and wealth management. It tracks your spending automatically but focuses on net worth, investment fees, and retirement planning. If your budget is healthy and you're ready to look at the bigger picture, this is a natural next step. The free dashboard is excellent, with tools to stress-test your savings rate and project future growth.

8. Simplifi by Quicken

Simplifi is Quicken's answer to simpler, modern budgeting. It gives you a clear overview of projected cash flow for the month, and its "Watchlists" notify you when a bill is higher than usual. The long track record of Quicken's support team is a plus — real people answer when you get stuck. Simplifi is ideal for families who want the power of a desktop tool with a genuinely useful mobile app.

9. Albert

Albert is part budgeting app, part savings assistant. It analyzes your income and spending, then automatically transfers small amounts into a built-in savings account — a technique that works wonders for people who forget to save. You can also chat with human financial experts whenever a money question comes up. For young professionals building an emergency fund, Albert removes the willpower problem entirely.

10. Rocket Money

Rocket Money, formerly Truebill, is a subscription tracker and bill negotiator disguised as a budgeting app. It identifies subscriptions you've forgotten about, negotiates lower rates on your cable or internet bills, and suggests a budget based on your real spending patterns. Many users save more than the cost of the premium tier within the first month by canceling a single forgotten subscription.

How to Choose the Right Budgeting App

Start by asking yourself whether you're comfortable with manual entry or want automatic bank syncing. Manual apps like YNAB and EveryDollar keep you aware of every purchase, which can be a feature, not a bug. Auto-sync apps like Simplifi and PocketGuard save time but require you to review transactions regularly so nothing slips through.

Next, check the price. Many free tiers are genuinely useful, so don't pay for premium unless you've outgrown the free version. Finally, try two or three apps side by side for a week. The best budgeting app is simply the one you actually open every day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which budgeting app is best for beginners?

Fudget and EveryDollar have the simplest learning curves. Fudget is great for quickly tracking income and expenses without setup, while EveryDollar's zero-based approach is intuitive for beginners who want a clear monthly plan.

Are budgeting apps worth it if I have a low income?

Yes — that's exactly when they help most. Apps like Goodbudget and Fudget are free, and the simple act of writing down every expense highlights the small leaks that add up quickly. A low income leaves little room for error, so knowing where each dollar lands is critical.

What's the difference between a zero-based budget app and a regular budgeting app?

A zero-based budget app, like YNAB or EveryDollar, gives every dollar a purpose until nothing is left unassigned. A regular budgeting app simply tracks spending against broad categories like groceries or entertainment. Zero-based budgeting is more proactive, making it effective for paying off debt and building savings faster.

The Bottom Line

The best budgeting apps for 2025 share one trait: they make your money visible. Whether you choose the deep discipline of YNAB, the no-frills simplicity of Fudget, or the automatic savings power of Albert, consistency matters more than the app itself. Start small, track honestly, and you'll watch your savings grow month by month.
