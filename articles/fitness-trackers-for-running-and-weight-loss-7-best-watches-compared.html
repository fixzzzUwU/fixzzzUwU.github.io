What to Watch For: Key Features for Running and Weight Loss

Before we get into the list, a quick rundown of the specs that actually matter.

Heart Rate Monitoring Is Non-Negotiable

For weight loss, the goal isn’t just burning calories—it’s staying in the right zone. A fitness watch with a reliable heart rate monitor helps you determine whether you’re in fat-burning mode, building your aerobic base, or pushing into anaerobic effort. Wrist-based optical sensors have improved massively, but if you’re serious about training, check if the watch supports external chest strap pairing for extra precision.

GPS Accuracy With Long Battery Life

A GPS running watch is only as good as its ability to lock onto a signal quickly and hold it through a long run. Good battery life matters too. Ideally, your watch should survive a week of daily wear plus several GPS-tracked runs without needing a charger in your bag.

Smartphone Integration and App Quality

The data is only useful if you can see it clearly. Look for a companion app that displays trends over time—not just a daily summary. This is where Most people abandon their trackers. An intuitive app that connects to Apple Health, Google Fit, or Strava will keep you honest and engaged.

FAQ

1. Do heart rate monitors on fitness watches actually help with weight loss?

Yes, indirectly, they make a big difference. Your watch calculates calorie burn based on heart rate. If you're simply eating based on a guess from a treadmill, you're likely overestimating your burn by 20-30%. A wrist-based fitness watch with heart rate monitor can tell you the actual exertion level of your exercise session. By pairing these numbers with a modest calorie deficit from your food tracker, you can fine-tune your net calorie budget and monitor trends over time.

2. Which is better for weight loss: a fitness tracker or a full GPS running watch?

It depends on your exercise preference. Weight loss via calorie burn is easier if you enjoy walking briskly, using the elliptical, or doing strength routines—a standard fitness tracker is accurate and lightweight enough to wear all day comfortably. If running is your primary exercise of choice, especially intervals or varied terrain workouts, a dedicated GPS running watch will offer much better pace data and most will provide heart-rate monitoring with better training zone guidance, helping you burn more calories in less time.

3. How do I know if my fitness tracker is accurately measuring calories burned?

The best way to check is to compare it against a known baseline measurement—which is tricky since true lab testing requires medical equipment. Instead, cross-reference your watch's calorie reading against your rate of weight loss over four to six weeks. If you consistently eat according to its burn prediction and your weight isn't trending according to the math, your watch might be overestimating. On average, wrist-based devices tend to overestimate calories by about 10%, so if weight loss plateaus, err on the side of eating smaller portions on light days.
