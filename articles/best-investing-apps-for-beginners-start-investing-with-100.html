Best Investing Apps for Beginners: Start Investing with $100

Let’s be honest: investing can feel intimidating. Between unfamiliar terms, scary market headlines, and the old belief that you need a chunk of money to get started, it’s easy to put it off. But here’s the good news — you don’t need thousands of dollars. In fact, $100 is enough to start your journey today, thanks to a new generation of investing apps built specifically for beginners.

Whether you want a fully automated robo advisor or the ability to buy fractional shares of your favorite companies, there’s an investing app for you. This guide breaks down the best investing apps for beginners, what makes them worth your time, and exactly how to start investing with $100 or less.

Why Start Investing with $100?

The biggest barrier to investing is usually not a lack of interest — it’s a lack of confidence. Many people assume they need a large lump sum just to get in the game. That’s simply not true anymore.

Modern apps allow you to buy fractional shares, which means you can own a piece of a company like Amazon or Google for a fraction of the price of one full share. You can also automate your savings so that loose change from daily purchases gets invested without you even thinking about it.

Starting small teaches you the most important part of investing: consistency. The goal isn’t to get rich overnight. It’s to build the habit, see how the market works, and watch your money grow over time.

What to Look For in the Best Investing Apps for Beginners

Not every investing platform is created equal. Some are designed for active traders, while others focus on passive, hands-off investing. As a beginner, here’s what you should keep an eye out for:

Low Minimums and Fractional Shares

If you’re starting with $100, the app needs to let you start small. Look for platforms that have no account minimums or very low ones. Fractional share investing is a must-have feature — it allows you to diversify your money across several companies and ETFs instead of putting all $100 into one single share.

Simple, Clean Interface

The best investing apps for beginners are the ones that don’t feel like a trading terminal. Look for apps that show your balance clearly, explain things in plain English, and don’t overwhelm you with charts and jargon right out of the gate.

Low Fees

Fees can quietly eat into your returns, especially when you’re starting with a small balance. The best robo advisors and micro investing apps typically charge a small monthly fee or a tiny percentage of your invested assets. Many beginner-friendly apps now offer $0 commissions on stock and ETF trades, so there’s no excuse to pay high trading fees.

Automation Options

One of the smartest ways beginners can start investing is by "paying yourself first." Some apps automatically round up your everyday purchases to the nearest dollar and invest the spare change. Others let you set up automatic recurring transfers, so you invest a small amount from every paycheck without even thinking about it.

Educational Resources

The platform you choose should help you grow as an investor. Built-in articles, quick guides, and easy-to-understand market news will help you learn while you invest.

Best Picks: Investing Apps We Recommend for Beginners

Here are our top recommendations for the best investing apps for beginners, each with its own strengths depending on your investing style and goals.

Best for Hands-Off Investing: Acorns

Acorns is the classic micro investing app, and for good reason. It connects to your bank account and automatically invests your spare change from everyday purchases. You can also set up recurring weekly or monthly contributions, making it one of the easiest ways to start investing with $100.

  • Acorns – Perfect for people who want investing to be completely automated. Round up purchases, set a schedule, and let the platform do the rest.
  • Best for: Total beginners who struggle to save consistently.

Best for Automated Portfolio Management: Betterment

Betterment is a leading robo advisor that builds and manages a diversified portfolio for you. It takes your financial goals, timeline, and risk tolerance into account, then automatically rebalances your investments over time. If you want a "set it and forget it" approach, this is a strong option.

  • Betterment – Offers professionally managed portfolios without requiring a huge account balance.
  • Best for: People who want a personalized investment strategy managed for them.

Best for Active Investing and Learning: Robinhood

Robinhood helped pioneer commission-free trading. It’s an intuitive, easy-to-use app that allows you to buy and sell stocks, ETFs, and even crypto with no commission fees. It’s great for beginners who want more control and want to learn by doing — plus fractional shares mean you can start with just $1.

  • Robinhood – Ideal for beginners who want to actively build and manage their own portfolio with zero commissions.
  • Best for: Curious investors who want a hands-on approach and educational tools.

How to Start Investing with $100: A Simple Step-by-Step Plan

You have the apps — now here’s how to actually get started. Follow these steps to make your first investment this week.

Step 1: Set a clear goal. Do you want to save for retirement, build wealth over the next decade, or just learn how the market works? Your goal determines which app is the best fit.

Step 2: Choose your app. If you want automation, go with Acorns or Betterment. If you want full control over picking stocks, Robinhood is a solid choice. You can even use two apps, but for simplicity, starting with one is best.

Step 3: Connect your bank account. Most apps allow you to sync directly with your bank. This lets them transfer funds securely and, in the case of micro investing apps, round up your purchases automatically.

Step 4: Make your first deposit. Transfer your first $100. If the app supports fractional shares — and most do — you’ll instantly be able to diversify your money across several investments instead of buying one single share.

Step 5: Start small and stay consistent. Invest another $25 or $50 per month. Regular contributions, no matter how small, are what truly build wealth over time thanks to the power of compound interest.

Micro Investing Apps, Robo Advisors, or Active Trading?

You’ll often see the terms "micro investing apps" and "robo advisor" used interchangeably, but they can mean different things.

Micro investing apps like Acorns focus on small, automated contributions. They’re designed for people who want to invest without thinking about it. Robo advisors like Betterment go a step further, actively managing your portfolio to keep it aligned with your goals. Active trading apps like Robinhood appeal to people who want to choose their own investments, press the buttons, and learn as they go.

The good news? As a beginner, you don’t have to pick a side. You can start with a micro investing app, learn the basics, and eventually graduate to a more active platform if that interests you.

FAQ: Investing Apps for Beginners

1. Can I really start investing with $100?

Yes. Most modern investing platforms have no minimum account balance or allow you to start with as little as $1. Fractional shares let you spread your $100 across multiple companies and ETFs instead of buying a full share of a single stock.

2. Are micro investing apps safe?

Generally, yes. Apps like Acorns and Betterment are regulated financial services companies. Your investments may go up and down in value, but the apps themselves use bank-level security and industry-standard protections. Just make sure to choose a reputable platform and avoid apps that promise guaranteed returns.

3. What’s the difference between a robo advisor and a regular investing app?

A robo advisor automatically builds and manages a diversified portfolio based on your goals and risk tolerance. A regular investing app gives you full control to choose and manage your own investments. Robo advisors are ideal for hands-off investors, while direct trading apps are better for those who want to be actively involved.

The Bottom Line

The hardest part of investing is simply getting started. With just $100 and the right app, you can open your first account, begin building the habit, and set yourself up for long-term financial success. Whether you choose a micro investing app like Acorns, a robo advisor like Betterment, or an active platform like Robinhood, what matters most is that you take that first step.

Start small, stay consistent, and let time do the heavy lifting. Your future self will thank you.
