Best Investing Apps for Beginners: Acorns, Betterment, and M1 Finance Compared

If you’re new to investing, the hardest part is often just starting. You might think you need thousands of dollars, a finance degree, or hours of free time to research stocks. In reality, many modern apps are built for people who want to begin small, automate the process, and learn as they go.

This comparison looks at three popular options for beginners: Acorns, Betterment, and M1 Finance. Each takes a different approach to micro-investing and robo-advisor investing. By the end, you’ll understand which one may fit your goals, how to invest with $100, and what to consider before opening an account.

What Are Micro-Investing Apps and Robo-Advisors?

Micro-investing apps are designed to make investing feel less intimidating. They often let you start with small amounts, invest in fractional shares, and automate contributions. Some, like Acorns, also round up everyday purchases and invest the spare change.

A robo-advisor for beginners is a digital platform that builds and manages a diversified portfolio for you. You answer questions about your goals, timeline, and risk tolerance, and the app handles the investment mix, rebalancing, and automatic deposits. Betterment is a well-known example of this model.

M1 Finance sits somewhere in between. It offers automation and pre-built portfolios, but it also gives you more control to create custom “pies” of stocks and ETFs. That makes it appealing if you want to learn by doing without starting from scratch.

One important note: Acorns, Betterment, and M1 Finance are primarily available in the US. If you’re in the EU, you may need to look for locally regulated robo-advisors or micro-investing platforms instead. Always confirm availability and account eligibility before signing up.

How to Invest with $100 (Without Overthinking It)

You don’t need a large sum to begin. Here’s a simple framework for how to invest with $100:

  1. Pick a goal. Are you investing for retirement, a rainy day, a house, or just to build the habit? Your goal affects how much risk makes sense.
  2. Choose one app. Don’t spread $100 across three platforms. Start with one that matches your style.
  3. Open and fund the account. Many apps have low or no minimums, and fractional shares let you buy portions of expensive investments.
  4. Select a portfolio. With a robo-advisor, you’ll answer a questionnaire. With M1 Finance, you can choose a pre-built pie or customize one.
  5. Automate a recurring deposit. Even $10 or $25 per week can help you stay consistent.
  6. Leave it alone. Checking daily can lead to emotional decisions. Automation is your friend.

This isn’t financial advice, and investing involves risk, including possible loss of principal. But the mechanics of starting small are easier than ever.

Acorns: Best for Spare-Change Beginners

Acorns is one of the most recognizable micro-investing apps. It links to your bank card and rounds up purchases to the nearest dollar, then invests the difference. You can also set recurring transfers. The app builds a portfolio based on your goals and risk tolerance, so you don’t have to pick individual investments.

What Acorns does well

  • Makes investing feel automatic and low-effort
  • Turns everyday spending into potential investments
  • Offers a simple, guided experience for beginners
  • Good for people who want to “set it and forget it”

Where Acorns may fall short

  • Less control over your exact portfolio
  • Subscription-based pricing may not suit everyone
  • Primarily US-focused, so EU availability is limited

Who Acorns is best for

Acorns is a strong fit if you struggle to save consistently and want a gentle nudge. It’s also good if you prefer a simple app over a full brokerage experience.

Betterment: Best Robo-Advisor for Goal-Based Beginners

Betterment is a classic robo-advisor for beginners. You choose a goal, such as retirement or a safety net, answer a few questions, and the platform recommends a diversified portfolio. It then automates deposits, rebalancing, and dividend reinvestment.

What Betterment does well

  • Goal-based investing makes it easy to stay motivated
  • Automated portfolios reduce the need for research
  • May offer socially responsible portfolio options and cash features
  • Strong fit for long-term, hands-off investors

Where Betterment may fall short

  • Less customizable than a self-directed platform
  • Advisory fees apply, so check current pricing
  • Primarily US-focused

Who Betterment is best for

Betterment works well for beginners who want professional-style portfolio management without picking stocks. If you like the idea of a robo-advisor handling the details, it’s a natural starting point.

M1 Finance: Best for Hands-On Beginners Who Want Customization

M1 Finance combines automation with customization. Its “pies” let you group investments into slices, such as ETFs or individual stocks. You can use pre-built pies or create your own. Fractional shares make it possible to invest small amounts across many slices.

What M1 Finance does well

  • Customizable portfolios for beginners who want control
  • Fractional shares make small investments easier
  • Automation helps keep your target allocation on track
  • Good for learning how portfolios work

Where M1 Finance may fall short

  • More responsibility falls on you
  • Less guided than a traditional robo-advisor
  • Fee structure differs from Acorns and Betterment; check current details
  • Primarily US-focused

Who M1 Finance is best for

M1 Finance is ideal if you want to learn by doing but still want automation. It’s a good middle ground between a fully managed robo-advisor and a DIY brokerage account.

How to Choose the Best Investing App for Beginners

When comparing the best investing app for beginners, look beyond the sign-up bonus. Consider:

  • Fees: Subscription fees, advisory fees, expense ratios, and trading costs can add up.
  • Minimums: Some apps require a minimum deposit; others let you start with very little.
  • Automation: Recurring deposits, automatic rebalancing, and round-ups can help you stay consistent.
  • Investment options: ETFs, individual stocks, fractional shares, and socially responsible portfolios vary by platform.
  • Availability: Acorns, Betterment, and M1 Finance are mainly US-focused. EU readers should check local regulated alternatives.
  • Tax documents and support: Make sure the platform provides clear statements and responsive help.

If you’re in the EU, don’t force a US-only app. Look for a locally regulated robo-advisor or micro-investing service that supports your country. The same principles apply: low costs, automation, and diversification.

Final Thoughts

Acorns, Betterment, and M1 Finance each solve a different beginner problem. Acorns makes investing feel effortless through round-ups. Betterment offers a guided, goal-based robo-advisor experience. M1 Finance gives you more control and customization while still offering automation.

If you’re wondering how to invest with $100, the answer is less about the exact amount and more about starting consistently.
