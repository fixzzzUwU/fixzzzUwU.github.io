Best Investing Apps for Beginners to Start Investing with $100

Starting your investment journey with just $100 feels intimidating, especially when the media makes it seem like you need thousands of dollars to open a brokerage account. The good news? That barrier to entry is gone. Thanks to financial technology, finding the best investing app for beginners is easier than ever. Whether you want to buy a slice of Apple or let an algorithm manage your money, there is a platform designed for your budget.

This guide breaks down the best micro-investing apps, robo-advisors, and brokerage accounts that allow you to start building wealth without worrying about high fees or minimum balances.

Why Start Investing with $100?

You might wonder if $100 is even worth investing. The answer is a resounding yes, but not because of the immediate returns. Investing $100 is about building a habit. It removes the psychological barrier that investing is only for the wealthy.

When you learn how to invest 100 dollars effectively, you are training yourself to prioritize saving and understand market fluctuations. Because of compound interest, that initial $100—plus whatever you add monthly—can grow significantly over the next 20 to 30 years. The goal isn't to get rich quick; it's to get started.

Key Features to Look for in a Beginner App

Before we dive into the specific platforms, you need to know what makes a micro investing app or brokerage worth your time.

  • Low or No Minimums: You shouldn't need $1,000 to open an account. The best apps let you start with $1 or even $0.
  • Fractional Shares: This is crucial. It allows you to buy a portion of a high-priced stock (like Amazon or Tesla) rather than paying for the whole share. A fractional shares app makes expensive stocks accessible to small investors.
  • Low Fees: Watch out for monthly maintenance fees or high expense ratios. Many modern apps offer commission-free trades.
  • Educational Resources: Since you are a beginner, the app should teach you how the market works.

Best Picks: Top Investing Apps for Beginners

Here are the top platforms that cater to beginners with limited capital. These picks balance ease of use, low costs, and educational value.

1. Best for Spare Change: Micro-Investing Apps

If you want to invest without even thinking about it, micro-investing apps are your best bet. These apps round up your everyday purchases to the nearest dollar and invest the difference.

  • Acorns: The pioneer of "round-ups." You link your debit card, and Acorns invests your spare change into a diversified portfolio. It is arguably the best investing app for beginners who want a hands-off approach.
  • Stash: Great for learning. Stash offers fractional shares and educational content that explains why you are investing in specific sectors. It has a low minimum ($5) to get started.
  • Public: A social investing app that allows you to buy fractional shares of US stocks with zero commissions. It’s great for following other investors and asking questions.

2. Best for Hands-Off Automation: Robo-Advisors

A robo advisor for beginners is a digital platform that manages your investment portfolio for you. You answer a few questions about your risk tolerance, and the algorithm builds a diversified portfolio of ETFs (Exchange Traded Funds).

  • Betterment: One of the most popular robo-advisors. It offers a "Socially Responsible" investing option and automatically rebalances your portfolio. No minimum to start.
  • Wealthfront: Excellent for those who want a mix of automation and access to advanced strategies. It offers a "Risk Parity" portfolio for those who want to diversify beyond just stocks and bonds.
  • M1 Finance: While technically a brokerage, M1 acts like a robo-advisor. You create a "Pie" of stocks and ETFs, and the platform automatically allocates your deposits based on your target percentages. This is perfect for the "set it and forget it" investor.

3. Best for Active Beginners: Commission-Free Brokerages

If you want to pick your own stocks and ETFs, you need a full brokerage. The following platforms have eliminated commissions and minimums, making them ideal for small accounts.

  • Fidelity: Fidelity is a powerhouse that now offers fractional share trading with zero commissions. You can invest as little as $1 into thousands of US stocks and ETFs.
  • Webull: Known for its advanced charting tools, Webull is great if you want to learn technical analysis. It offers fractional shares and extended-hours trading.
  • SoFi Invest: SoFi combines banking and investing. It offers active investing (buying stocks) and automated investing. It is a great all-in-one financial hub for Gen Z and Millennials.

How to Choose the Right App for You

With so many options, how do you decide? It comes down to your personality and goals.

If you are lazy (in a good way): Choose a robo-advisor like Betterment or a micro-investing app like Acorns. You want to deposit money and let the experts handle the rest.

If you want to learn: Choose a brokerage like Fidelity or Stash. You want to research companies and buy specific stocks you believe in.

If you have specific goals: Look for apps with features like "Round-Ups" for short-term goals or "Retirement" accounts for long-term goals. Most of the apps listed above offer both taxable brokerage accounts and IRAs (Individual Retirement Accounts).

How to Invest Your First $100: A Step-by-Step Guide

Now that you have chosen an app, here is the practical process of how to invest 100 dollars.

  1. Open the Account: Download the app and sign up. You will need your Social Security Number (for US residents) or equivalent tax ID, and a bank account to link.
  2. Fund the Account: Transfer your $100. This usually takes 2-3 business days to clear.
  3. Choose Your Investments:
  • Option A (ETFs): Buy a broad market ETF (like one that tracks the S&P 500). This gives you instant diversification.
  • Option B (Stocks): Pick 2-3 companies you use and understand. Use fractional shares to buy $20 worth of each.
  1. Set Up Recurring Deposits: This is the most important step. Even if it's just $10 or $25 a month, automate it. This is called Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA) and it lowers your risk over time.
  2. Leave It Alone: Do not check your portfolio every day. The market goes up and down. As a beginner with $100, your time horizon is years, not days.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Trying to Get Rich Quick: Avoid "meme stocks" or options trading with your first $100. The goal is steady growth, not gambling.
  • Ignoring Fees: Even small fees eat into your returns. Stick to apps with zero commission and low expense ratios (under 0.20%).
  • Panic Selling: If the market drops 10%, do not sell. That is the time to keep buying if you can. You only lose money if you sell at a loss.

FAQ

1. Can I really start investing with only $100?

Yes. With the rise of fractional shares, you can buy into expensive companies or diversified ETFs with as little as $1. The key is consistency, not the initial lump sum.

2. What is the best app for a complete beginner?

For most people, a robo-advisor like Betterment or a micro-investing app like Acorns is the best starting point. They handle the diversification for you, so you don't have to pick individual stocks.

3. Are investing apps safe?

Yes, reputable apps are safe. Most US-based brokerages are members of the SIPC (Securities Investor Protection Corporation), which protects securities in your account up to $500,000 if the brokerage fails. Always ensure the app is SIPC or FINRA regulated.

Conclusion

You don't need a fortune to start building one. By utilizing a fractional shares app, a micro investing app, or a robo advisor for beginners, you can put your first $100 to work today. The most important step is not picking the perfect stock, but simply starting the process. Choose a platform that fits your style, set up an automatic deposit, and let time do the heavy lifting.
