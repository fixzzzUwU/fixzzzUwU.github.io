Best Robo-Advisors for Beginners: 8 Automated Investing Apps That Beat a Savings Account

A savings account is a safe place for emergency cash, but it is not designed to grow your money over decades. If you have $100 to $5,000 and want a hands-off way to start investing, a robo-advisor can be a practical first step. These apps build a diversified portfolio for you, automate deposits, and rebalance over time—so you do not have to pick individual stocks or watch the market all day.

Below is a beginner-friendly buying guide to the best automated investing apps for 2025, including how to start investing with $100, what to compare, and how Acorns vs Betterment stacks up.

What a Robo-Advisor Does for a First-Time Investor

A robo-advisor is an app or online service that manages an investment portfolio for you. You answer a few questions about your goals, timeline, and comfort with risk. The app then suggests a mix of diversified investments, usually low-cost ETFs, and handles the day-to-day management.

For beginners, the appeal is simplicity. You do not need to research individual companies or decide when to buy and sell. You can often open an account with a low minimum, set up automatic contributions, and let the portfolio run. Just remember: investing involves risk, and no app can guarantee returns that beat a savings account. The goal is long-term growth, not a guaranteed shortcut.

How to Start Investing With $100 (Without Overthinking It)

You do not need a large sum to begin. Here is a sensible checklist for how to start investing with 100 dollars:

  1. Cover the basics first. Build a small emergency fund and pay off high-interest debt before investing.
  2. Choose the right account type. In the US, consider a taxable brokerage account or a retirement account such as an IRA. In the EU, look at local tax-advantaged wrappers and reporting rules.
  3. Pick an app with a low minimum. Many robo-advisors and automated investing apps let you start small, and some support fractional shares.
  4. Automate a recurring deposit. Even $25 or $50 per month helps you build the habit.
  5. Choose a diversified portfolio. A broad, low-cost mix is usually better for beginners than a concentrated bet.
  6. Leave it alone. Check in quarterly, not daily. Automating deposits and rebalancing is the whole point.

Most apps below are US-focused. EU investors should confirm availability, tax treatment, and local equivalents before signing up.

Acorns vs Betterment: Which Is Better for Beginners?

The Acorns vs Betterment decision comes down to how you want to invest.

Choose Acorns if you want a very simple app, like the idea of round-ups, and plan to invest small amounts regularly. It is built for people who want to start with spare change and a basic portfolio. Just watch the monthly subscription cost, especially if your balance is small.

Choose Betterment if you want a more traditional robo-advisor experience with goal-based planning, automated deposits, and a wider range of account types. It may feel more like a long-term investing home than a micro-investing app.

Neither option is automatically “better.” Acorns can be great for building the habit. Betterment may suit someone who wants a clearer path toward goals such as retirement or a house fund.

How to Choose the Best Investment App for Beginners

Use this short checklist before you open an account:

  • Minimum deposit: Can you start with $100 or less?
  • Fees: Look at advisory fees, fund expenses, subscriptions, and account fees.
  • Portfolio style: Does it use diversified, low-cost ETFs?
  • Automation: Can you set recurring deposits and automatic rebalancing?
  • Account types: Does it offer the taxable or retirement account you need?
  • Tax features: US investors may want tax-loss harvesting or IRA support; EU investors need local tax reporting.
  • Usability: A clean app you understand beats a fancy app you avoid.
  • Support: Check help centers, chat, and phone support before you need them.

The best investment app for beginners is the one you will actually use consistently—not the one with the longest feature list.

Bottom Line

For most first-time investors, a low-cost robo-advisor with automatic deposits and a diversified portfolio is a sensible starting point. Betterment and Wealthfront are strong all-around choices for hands-off investing. SoFi Invest and Acorns are appealing if you want a simpler all-in-one or spare-change experience. Schwab Intelligent Portfolios, Fidelity Go, M1 Finance, and Stash round out the list for different preferences.

Start small, automate what you can afford, and treat investing as a long-term habit rather than a get-rich-quick move.

FAQ

Is a robo-advisor good for beginners?

Yes, for many beginners. A robo-advisor can simplify diversification, automatic deposits, and rebalancing. It is not a guarantee of profits, so you still need an emergency fund and a realistic timeline.

Can I start investing with $100?

Often, yes. Many automated investing apps have low or no minimums, and some support fractional shares. Check the current minimum and fees before opening an account.

Is Acorns or Betterment better for a first investment app?

Acorns is better for very simple spare-change investing and habit building. Betterment is better for a more traditional goal-based robo-advisor experience. Choose based on fees, minimums, and how hands-off you want to be.
