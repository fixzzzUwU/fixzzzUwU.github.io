How to Choose the Right Raised Garden Bed Kit for Vegetables

Not sure which one to buy? Here are a few things to keep in mind when shopping for the best raised garden beds for small backyards.

Size and depth. Most vegetables need at least 12 inches of soil depth, but root crops like carrots and potatoes prefer 18 inches or more. Measure your available space before ordering, and remember that bigger isn’t always better if it takes over your entire patio.

Material. Galvanized steel and cedar are the two most popular materials. Steel is durable and modern, while cedar is naturally beautiful and rot-resistant. Plastic and resin options are lightweight and low-maintenance, but they may not last as long.

Portability. If you’re renting or plan to move, look for a raised garden bed with legs or a lightweight fabric bed that you can easily relocate. Permanent beds are great for homeowners, but they’re hard to move once filled with soil.

Drainage. Make sure your bed has adequate drainage holes or an open bottom. Poor drainage is the fastest way to kill your vegetable plants.

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep should a raised garden bed be for vegetables?

For most vegetables, a depth of 12 inches is the minimum. Deep-rooted crops like tomatoes, carrots, and potatoes need at least 18 inches. If you’re growing shallow-rooted greens like lettuce or herbs, 6 to 8 inches can be enough.

Are metal raised garden beds better than wood?

Metal raised garden beds, especially those made from galvanized steel, are extremely durable and resistant to rot, pests, and weather damage. They also warm up faster in the spring, which extends your growing season. Wooden beds, particularly cedar, are beautiful and naturally rot-resistant, but they may need replacement after several years. Both are excellent choices — it comes down to your budget and style preference.

Can I put a raised garden bed on a patio or balcony?

Yes. Many raised garden beds are specifically designed for patios and balconies. Look for models with legs, which protect your flooring and make gardening more comfortable, or choose a fabric bed that sits directly on the ground. Just make sure your patio or balcony can support the weight of the soil when it’s wet — a 4x2-foot bed can weigh several hundred pounds.
