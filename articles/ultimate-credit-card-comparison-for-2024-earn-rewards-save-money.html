What to Look for in a Credit Card in 2024

Choosing the right credit card isn’t just about the headline reward. A balanced card should fit your spending habits, travel plans, and budget. Here’s what to scrutinize:

Rewards Structure

  • Points, miles, or cash back?

• Points are flexible—often redeemable for travel or merchandise.

• Miles are ideal for frequent flyers.

• Cash back gives you instant savings.

  • Bonus categories

Look for cards that boost rewards on categories you spend the most in: groceries, dining, gas, or streaming services.

Annual Fees & Sign‑Up Bonuses

  • Annual fee vs. rewards

A low‑to‑no fee card might be preferable if you don’t hit the spending thresholds needed to justify a higher fee.

  • Welcome offers

Many cards kick off with a sizable sign‑up bonus—often worth several hundred dollars in points or cash.

Travel Perks & International Use

  • Global acceptance

Cards backed by Visa or Mastercard are the most widely accepted worldwide.

  • Travel benefits

Complimentary lounge access, free checked bags, or travel insurance can add significant value for globetrotters.

Cash Back & Everyday Spending

  • Flat‑rate vs. tiered

Flat‑rate cards (e.g., 1.5% on all purchases) are simple, whereas tiered cards offer higher rates on specific categories.

  • Automatic redemption

Some cards let you convert cash back into statement credits or gift cards instantly.

FAQ

Q1: How does a high annual fee compare to the rewards I’ll earn?

A1: A high annual fee can be justified if you hit the spending thresholds that unlock generous rewards or travel perks. For example, a $95 fee may be worth it if you earn 2x miles on flights and receive lounge access, saving you hundreds on travel each year.

Q2: Can I use a credit card that has a foreign transaction fee for international travel?

A2: You can, but it will cost you an extra 1–3% on each purchase abroad. For frequent travelers, a card with no foreign transaction fees is more economical, as it preserves your rewards and reduces costs.

Q3: Is it better to have multiple cards for different rewards?

A3: Yes—many consumers benefit from owning a mix of cards: one for travel, one for cash back, and one no‑fee card for everyday use. Just keep track of each card’s benefits and avoid unnecessary fees.
