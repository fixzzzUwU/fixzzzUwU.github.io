10 Genius Rental-Friendly Home Upgrades You Can Do for Under $50

Renting doesn’t mean you’re stuck with beige walls, boring fixtures, and a space that feels like a waiting room. With the right renter friendly upgrades, you can turn your apartment into a home that actually reflects your style—without risking your security deposit. Whether you’re a first-time renter or a seasoned pro, these rental-friendly home decor ideas are all under $50, take an afternoon to install, and come off cleanly when it’s time to move on. Here are ten genius upgrades that prove you don’t need to own the walls to love the space.

1. Peel-and-Stick Wallpaper Accent Wall

Temporary wallpaper for renters has completely changed the game. Modern peel-and-stick designs range from bold botanicals to subtle linen textures and even faux brick. One accent wall behind your bed or sofa can completely transform the energy of a room. The best part? It goes up in about an hour and peels off in minutes with no sticky residue when your lease ends. Look for brands that specifically market their wallpaper as "renter friendly" to ensure it won't damage your paint.

2. Command Hooks and Gallery Wall Kits

Blank walls are the biggest missed opportunity in most rentals. Command hooks and adhesive picture-hanging strips let you build a gallery wall without drilling a single hole. Arrange framed prints, posters, or even lightweight floating shelves. When it’s time to move, the strips pull cleanly away from the wall, leaving no marks behind. This is one of the easiest apartment makeover on a budget ideas you can tackle in a single afternoon.

3. Smart Bulbs and Smart Plugs

Smart home gadgets for apartments don’t have to involve rewiring, drilling, or permanent installation. Smart bulbs screw straight into your existing lamps and overhead fixtures, and smart plugs fit into any outlet. You can control everything from your phone, set schedules, and switch the color temperature to match your mood or time of day. When you move out, just unscrew and unplug—everything goes with you. It’s a renter friendly upgrade that also helps you save on energy bills.

4. Swap Out Cabinet Hardware

That cheap, generic builder-grade hardware on your kitchen and bathroom cabinets is an eyesore. Replacing it with modern brass, matte black, or ceramic knobs is a temporary upgrade that takes about ten minutes per cabinet. All you need is a screwdriver. Keep the original screws and handles in a labeled bag and swap everything back before you move out. This is one of the most satisfying rental friendly home decor tricks for under $50—and it instantly makes your kitchen look custom.

5. Peel-and-Stick Backsplash Tiles

If your rental kitchen has plain white tile or laminate counters, peel-and-stick backsplash tiles are a game-changer. These adhesive panels look like real subway tile, marble, or even herringbone patterns and install in an afternoon. They’re waterproof, easy to wipe down, and remove cleanly when you leave. A backsplash is a high-impact apartment makeover on a budget upgrade that makes your kitchen look like it cost ten times more than it did.

6. Temporary Floor Tiles and Decals

Ugly rental flooring is one of the most common complaints renters have. Instead of living with it, try peel-and-stick floor tiles or vinyl decals. You can cover a small area like a bathroom floor or create a patterned rug effect in a corner of your bedroom. They’re durable enough for daily foot traffic and come off without damaging the original floor underneath. It’s a smart, budget-friendly way to personalize your space without a deposit dispute.

7. Tension Rod Room Dividers

Studio apartments and open layouts can feel chaotic. Tension rods don’t require any drilling—they press firmly between two walls or inside a doorframe. Hang sheer curtains, tapestries, or even a lightweight blackout panel to create a bedroom nook or separate your home office. This is a classic renter friendly upgrade that adds privacy, definition, and a cozy vibe to any room in your apartment.

8. No-Drill Smart Video Doorbell

Security is a top concern for apartment dwellers, but many leases forbid drilling into the door or frame. A smart video doorbell with a no-drill mounting bracket attaches with strong adhesive and can be removed without leaving marks. You’ll get motion alerts and live video right on your phone, making it one of the most practical smart home gadgets for apartments. Just check your lease and local privacy laws before installing, and you’ll have peace of mind without a permanent footprint.

9. Stick-On LED Under-Cabinet Lights

Kitchens and home offices are often poorly lit, especially in older rentals. Stick-on LED light bars are magnetic or adhesive and install under cabinets, shelves, or desks in seconds. Many are rechargeable via USB, so there are no wires to hide. Bright, warm lighting instantly makes a rental feel more polished and functional. It’s an easy, affordable way to add both style and utility to your space.

10. Temporary Window Privacy Film

If you live close to neighbors or face a busy street, temporary window film gives you privacy without blocking natural light. These static-cling or adhesive films come in frosted, rainbow, or decorative patterns. They apply with just water and a squeegee and peel off cleanly at the end of your lease. It’s a small upgrade that makes a huge difference in comfort, light, and style—especially in bathrooms and ground-floor apartments.

FAQ

Will peel-and-stick wallpaper damage my walls when I remove it?

Most renter-friendly temporary wallpaper is designed to peel off cleanly without leaving residue or pulling paint. The key is to remove it slowly and at a low angle. If you have fragile or recently painted walls, test a small corner first before committing to a full accent wall.

Are smart plugs and bulbs worth it for apartment dwellers?

Yes. Smart plugs and bulbs are completely portable, so you can take them to your next place. They help you automate lighting, save energy, and make your apartment feel more modern—all without any permanent installation or landlord approval.

Can I get my security deposit back if I use renter friendly upgrades?

In most cases, yes, as long as you return the apartment to its original condition. The upgrades on this list are intentionally removable and damage-free. Just keep all original parts, remove everything carefully before move-out, and wipe down any adhesive residue with a gentle cleaner.
