Investing for Beginners: Best Brokerage Accounts Compared

So, you’ve decided to take control of your financial future. That’s a massive first step. But if you’ve opened your phone to look at investing apps, you’ve probably realized there are dozens of options, all promising low fees and slick interfaces. It can feel overwhelming.

The good news? You don’t need to be a Wall Street pro to get started. You just need a platform that fits your habits, your budget, and your goals. Whether you want to buy fractional shares of expensive stocks or set up a fully automated portfolio, there is a perfect match for you.

In this guide, we’re breaking down the best brokerage account for beginners. We’ll compare the heavy hitters—M1 Finance, Webull, and Robinhood—to help you figure out where to park your first dollars.

What Makes a Brokerage "Beginner-Friendly"?

Before we dive into the comparison, let’s define what we are looking for. For a new investor, the ideal platform needs to remove friction. You don’t want to be bogged down by complex charts or hit with hidden fees that eat your tiny returns.

Here is what we prioritize in this comparison:

  • Low (or Zero) Fees: You shouldn't be charged a commission just to buy a stock.
  • Low Minimums: You should be able to start with $5 or $100, not $10,000.
  • Educational Resources: Good apps explain things clearly.
  • Fractional Shares: This allows you to buy a slice of a company like Amazon or Google, even if the full share costs $1,000+.

Let’s look at how the top contenders stack up.

The Big Three: A Head-to-Head Comparison

1. M1 Finance: The "Set It and Forget It" Option

If you are looking for a robo advisor comparison that still gives you control, M1 Finance is the sweet spot. It isn't a traditional broker where you stare at a ticker all day. Instead, M1 uses "Pies" to help you build a portfolio.

You create a pie (or use a pre-made one) that allocates percentages to different stocks or ETFs. When you deposit money, M1 automatically buys the pieces that are underweight. This makes it incredibly easy to dollar-cost average without thinking about it.

Why it’s great for beginners:

  • Fractional Shares: M1 allows for dynamic rebalancing, meaning it buys slices of stocks to keep your portfolio balanced.
  • Automation: You can set up automatic deposits and let the platform do the heavy lifting.
  • Low Fee Investing: There are zero commission fees, and you can open an account with just $100.

This is the best choice for people who want to invest consistently but don't want to spend hours researching individual stocks. It bridges the gap between a fully automated robo-advisor and a standard brokerage.

2. Webull: The Data-Driven Trader

Webull is often compared to Robinhood, but it offers a much more robust suite of tools. It is perfect for the beginner who wants to learn technical analysis without paying for expensive software.

The interface is a bit more complex than Robinhood, but it provides free access to data that other platforms charge for. You get multiple chart types, indicators, and even a stock screener.

Why it’s great for beginners:

  • Free Stock: Webull often runs promotions where you get a free stock just for opening an account and making a small deposit.
  • Extended Hours: You can trade before the market opens and after it closes, which is great for those with day jobs.
  • No Fees: Like the others, it offers zero commission trading.

However, the learning curve is steeper. If you are easily intimidated by numbers and graphs, this might feel like drinking from a firehose. But if you are analytical and want to understand why a stock moves, this is your platform.

3. Robinhood: The Mobile Pioneer

Robinhood gets a lot of press, both good and bad. But for the absolute beginner who just wants to buy a few shares of their favorite company, it remains one of the most user-friendly apps on the market.

It pioneered the zero-commission model and made investing feel like a game. The interface is clean, colorful, and incredibly intuitive. You can buy a stock in about three taps.

Why it’s great for beginners:

  • Simplicity: There is no clutter. You see your buying power, your stocks, and the news.
  • Fractional Shares: You can invest as little as $1 into any stock.
  • Instant Deposits: You can start trading immediately with funds from your linked bank account.

The downside is that it lacks the advanced research tools of Webull and the automation of M1. It is purely a "buy and sell" app. For how to start investing with zero stress, it is hard to beat.

How to Start Investing Today

Once you pick a platform, the process is the same. Don't overthink it.

  1. Open the Account: This takes about 10 minutes. You'll need your ID and bank details.
  2. Fund It: Transfer a small amount. Even $50 is enough to start.
  3. Pick an ETF: If you don't know what to buy, look for a "Total Stock Market" ETF (like VTI or ITOT). This gives you instant diversification.
  4. Set a Schedule: Decide to invest $50 every Friday. This is called dollar-cost averaging, and it removes the stress of trying to time the market.

The most important thing is to start. You will make mistakes, and that is okay. The market goes up and down, but time in the market beats timing the market.

Conclusion

The best brokerage account for beginners is the one you will actually use. If you want automation, choose M1 Finance. If you want data, choose Webull. If you want simplicity, choose Robinhood.

All three offer low fee investing and fractional shares, which are the two most important features for new investors. Don't get paralyzed by the choice. Pick one, deposit a small amount, and buy a broad market ETF. Your future self will thank you for starting today.

FAQ

Q: How much money do I need to start investing?

A: You can start with as little as $1 on platforms like Robinhood and M1 Finance thanks to fractional shares. However, we recommend starting with at least $50 to $100 so you can see meaningful growth and feel the impact of your contributions.

Q: Is it safe to use these online brokerage apps?

A: Yes. All three platforms mentioned are regulated by the SEC and FINRA. Your cash is insured by the SIPC (Securities Investor Protection Corporation) for up to $500,000. This protects your assets if the broker goes out of business, though it does not protect against market losses.

Q: What is the difference between a robo-advisor and a standard brokerage?

A: A robo-advisor (like M1 Finance) automatically manages your portfolio based on your risk tolerance. You don't pick the stocks. A standard brokerage (like Webull or Robinhood) gives you the tools to pick your own stocks. M1 is a hybrid, offering automation while letting you choose the assets in your "Pie."
