Best Budgeting Apps for 2025: YNAB vs Monarch vs EveryDollar

If you're still tracking your spending in a tangled web of spreadsheets, you're not alone—but you're also working harder than you need to. In 2025, the best budgeting apps have matured into genuinely powerful tools that can categorize transactions automatically, sync with your bank, and even help you pay down debt faster. The catch? They approach money management in very different ways.

Three names dominate the conversation right now: YNAB, Monarch Money, and EveryDollar. Each has a loyal following, and each is built around a distinct philosophy. This comparison breaks down how they differ, who they're best for, and which one deserves a spot on your phone. Whether you're hunting for the best budgeting app for a growing family or just want a monthly budget app that doesn't feel like homework, here's what you need to know.

How These Three Budgeting Apps Differ

Before diving into features, it helps to understand the underlying approach each app takes. That philosophy shapes everything from the interface to how much time you'll spend inside the app.

  • YNAB is built on envelope budgeting—giving every dollar a job before you spend it.
  • Monarch Money is a holistic personal finance app focused on tracking, net worth, and long-term planning.
  • EveryDollar uses a zero-based budget model with a strong emphasis on simplicity and debt payoff.

None of these is objectively "best." The right choice depends on whether you want to be actively involved in every spending decision or prefer a dashboard that shows you where things stand.

YNAB: The Envelope Budgeting App That Changes Behavior

YNAB (You Need A Budget) is the most opinionated app of the three—and that's exactly why people love it. The core method is classic envelope budgeting, digitized: you assign the money you actually have to specific categories, and when a category runs dry, you have to move money from somewhere else.

What stands out:

  • Zero-based, proactive budgeting. You plan before you spend, not after.
  • Excellent educational content. Free workshops, a robust blog, and a supportive community.
  • Loan and goal tracking. You can set targets for debt payoff, vacations, or emergency funds.
  • Strong bank syncing with manual entry as a backup.

Where it falls short:

  • The learning curve is real. Expect a week or two before it clicks.
  • The subscription price is on the higher end for a single-purpose budgeting tool.
  • No investment tracking or net worth dashboard—it's strictly about the money you're spending and saving.

YNAB is ideal if you've tried budgeting before and failed because you weren't engaged enough. It forces a level of intentionality that spreadsheets never will.

Monarch Money: The All-in-One Personal Finance App

Monarch Money positions itself differently. It's less of a strict budgeting system and more of a complete financial picture. You connect your accounts, and Monarch pulls everything into one dashboard: spending, savings, investments, and net worth.

What stands out:

  • Beautiful, intuitive interface. Widely considered the best-designed app in this category.
  • Household collaboration. Multiple users can share one account, making it strong for couples and families.
  • Customizable budgets and categories. You can build a budget that matches how you actually live.
  • Investment and net worth tracking built right in.

Where it falls short:

  • It won't nudge you the way YNAB does. If you lack discipline, Monarch won't fix that.
  • The price is comparable to YNAB, so cost isn't a differentiator.
  • Some users find the flexibility overwhelming at first.

If you want a monthly budget app that also functions as a full personal finance app—tracking retirement accounts, property, and debt in one place—Monarch is the strongest option here.

EveryDollar: Simple Zero-Based Budgeting

EveryDollar, built by Ramsey Solutions, strips budgeting down to its essentials. You create a zero-based budget each month: income minus expenses equals zero. That's it. The interface is clean, the learning curve is minimal, and the free tier is genuinely usable.

What stands out:

  • Free version available. The paid Premium tier adds bank syncing and advanced features.
  • Fast setup. You can build your first budget in under 15 minutes.
  • Debt payoff focus. Aligns with the broader Ramsey "baby steps" philosophy.
  • Simple, distraction-free design.

Where it falls short:

  • Manual entry on the free plan gets tedious fast.
  • Less powerful reporting than Monarch.
  • No investment tracking.
  • The app's philosophy is tightly tied to Ramsey's broader financial advice, which won't resonate with everyone.

EveryDollar is the best budgeting app for someone who wants to start today with minimal friction and doesn't need a full financial dashboard.

Conclusion: The Right App Is the One You'll Actually Use

The best budgeting app in 2025 isn't the one with the most features—it's the one that fits how you think about money. YNAB rewards intentionality and transforms spending habits. Monarch Money delivers a polished, comprehensive personal finance app for people who want everything in one place. EveryDollar offers a low-friction entry point with a free tier that's hard to beat.

If you're replacing spreadsheets, start by being honest about your personality. Do you need structure and accountability? Go with YNAB. Want a beautiful overview of everything? Monarch. Want simple and free? EveryDollar. Any of the three will outperform a spreadsheet—as long as you open it.

FAQ

Is YNAB or Monarch Money better for couples?

Monarch Money is generally better for couples because it's designed for shared household accounts from the ground up. YNAB can work for couples, but sharing requires more coordination.

Is there a free budgeting app among these three?

EveryDollar offers a free tier, though bank syncing requires the paid Premium plan. YNAB and Monarch Money are both paid subscriptions with no permanent free option, though both offer trials.

Can I use a budgeting app instead of a spreadsheet?

Yes—and for most people, you should. Dedicated apps sync with your bank, categorize transactions automatically, and update in real time. Spreadsheets require manual entry and offer no automation, which is why most people abandon them within a few months.
