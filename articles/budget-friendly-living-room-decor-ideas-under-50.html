Budget-Friendly Living Room Decor Ideas Under $50

There’s a specific kind of frustration that comes with looking around your living room and realizing it still feels like a holding area rather than a place you actually enjoy. Maybe the couch was handed down, the coffee table was an emergency buy, and the walls have been bare for three months. If that sounds familiar, take a breath. You don’t need a full renovation or a designer budget to change the energy of the room. In fact, with the right budget living room decor ideas, you can make it feel intentional, cozy, and put-together for less than the cost of a couple of takeout dinners.

The best part about refreshing a rental or a starter apartment is that small pieces do the heavy lifting. We’re talking about affordable home decor under $50 that you can swap out, rearrange, and carry to your next place without a second thought. No permanent damage, no expensive commitments, no regret purchases that you’ll be stuck with forever.

Start With the Biggest Blank Space: Cheap Wall Art Ideas

If your walls are empty, that’s the first place to look. Empty walls make a room feel unfinished, even when everything else is fine. But art can get expensive fast, and not everyone wants to nail fifty frames into rental drywall. The good news is that cheap wall art ideas have come a long way in the last few years. You have options that look far more expensive than they actually are.

Consider a single large-format piece instead of a gallery wall. One oversized framed print or canvas can anchor a sofa or console table without making the room feel cluttered. Large pieces in the $30 to $50 range are surprisingly easy to find, especially if you go for abstract prints, botanical line drawings, or minimal landscape photography. These styles tend to read as “curated” rather than just “decorative.”

Another approach is the floating ledge trick. Install one or two narrow picture ledges—they only need a few small screws—and lean a mix of frames, small objects, and even a plant on top. This is a good option if you like to change things often, because you’re not locked into a specific layout. Plus, it gives you a place to display a few personal touches without committing to a wall of matching frames.

For renters who really cannot drill or hammer, peel-and-stick wall decor and adhesive picture hangers are a lifesaver. Some options look like real framed art and can be repositioned without leaving marks. These work particularly well for smaller prints.

Throw Pillows Are the Quickest Refresh You’ll Ever Make

Here’s the thing about throw pillows: they’re the fastest, most forgiving decor upgrade available. You can completely change the vibe of a neutral couch with two or three new pillows. And because they’re soft and portable, you don’t need to stress about measurements or style matching too much.

When shopping for pillow covers rather than full inserts, the price drops dramatically. Many covers come in at well under $20, so you can buy two or even three different styles and swap them seasonally. For a cohesive look, stick to a small palette—maybe a warm cream, a textured weave, and a subtle pattern that pulls in a color from elsewhere in the room. Off-white and textured neutrals feel especially natural in rooms aiming for a calm, farmhouse-inspired aesthetic. That’s where farmhouse decor under $50 genuinely shines: think waffle weaves, burlap accents, linen-look covers, and earthy tones. You get that cozy, approachable look without dropping a fortune on matching sets.

One practical tip: if you’re starting from zero, don’t buy a full set of four matching pillows. Go with two complementary pairs instead. For example, one pair in a solid neutral and another in a light pattern. It reads more styled and intentional, and it costs less than you think.

Decorative Objects That Do More Than Sit There

Let’s be honest: decorative objects are the unsung heroes of a good living room. They are the difference between a space that looks settled and one that looks like it’s waiting for the last box to be unpacked. But you don’t need to fill every surface. A focused collection of three to five pieces is enough to make a console table, bookshelf, or coffee table feel complete.

Start with one sculptural object. That could be a ceramic vase, a lined basket with a blanket folded inside, a small stack of coffee-table books, or a decorative bowl. Books are especially good because they add height, color, and personality all at once. Look for secondhand or affordable options under $20, then arrange them with the spines facing inward or out, depending on the look you want.

Candles and candle holders are another easy win. A candle on a small tray, next to a tiny potted plant or a decorative orb, can instantly warm up a tabletop. If you’re nervous about styling, use the “rule of three”: three objects of varying heights always feel balanced. You can build this trio for well under $50 using a combination of items from different categories.

Baskets belong in this section too. A large woven or fabric basket placed beside the couch holds throw blankets, extra pillows, or magazines. Not only does it look more styled than a pile of blankets, but it also solves a real organizational problem. In rental living rooms where storage is limited, baskets are one of the most valuable pieces of affordable home decor under $50 you can own.

Accent Pieces That Pull the Whole Room Together

Sometimes the missing ingredient isn’t a big furniture piece, it’s a small accent element that makes the room feel considered. Floor mirrors are a classic example. A full-length mirror leaning against a wall adds light and makes a compact living room feel larger. You can find basic floor mirrors for around $40 to $50 if you shop around. They are a great solution for renters too—you don’t have to hang anything, just lean it against the wall.

Another underrated accent is the throw blanket. Even if you don’t need it for warmth, a folded blanket draped over the arm of a sofa adds softness and color. It’s an easy way to pull in an accent shade that hasn’t appeared elsewhere in the room yet.

Do not sleep on tabletop trays either. A simple metal, wooden, or rattan tray on the coffee table keeps remotes, coasters, and small plants organized. It’s one of those small touches that instantly gives a space a polished “staged” look, but it only costs a fraction of what a full decor overhaul would. Keep an eye out for light wood tones or black metal pieces if you’re leaning into a farmhouse or modern-rustic direction.

If you’re building living room decor on a budget, try shifting your mindset. Rather than asking “what does this room need?” ask “what one thing would make this corner feel finished?” That single accent—a lamp, a tray, a mirror, a plant stand—can be enough.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I decorate my living room on a tight budget?

Focus on the areas you see the most—your couch, your main wall, and your coffee table. Swapping out throw pillows, adding a throw blanket, and putting one piece of artwork on a blank wall changes a room faster than almost anything else. You can easily decorate these key spots with budget living room decor ideas and stay under $100 total.

What is the cheapest way to make my living room look put together?

The cheapest way is to declutter and style what you already have. Place books in neat stacks, group small objects into a vignette, and use baskets to hide loose items. Then add one or two affordable new accents, like throw pillow covers or a wall print. Deeper, layered styling costs nothing.

Can I use farmhouse decor under $50 in a modern apartment?

Absolutely. Farmhouse style is more about texture and warmth than it is about full rustic rooms. You can add a woven basket, a linen pillow cover, or a natural wood tray to a modern space, and it will blend seamlessly. The key is to keep the pieces neutral and mix in a few contemporary touches so the room doesn’t feel theme-heavy.

The Takeaway

Refreshing your living room on a budget isn’t about making a dramatic change all at once. It’s about a few thoughtful additions that slowly turn a space into somewhere you’re happy to spend time. Pick one wall to decorate, invest in pillow covers over full pillows, choose decorative objects that actually serve a purpose, and let accent pieces do the talking. Your rental doesn’t need to look like a showroom. It just needs to feel like you, and that can absolutely be done for under $50 per piece.
